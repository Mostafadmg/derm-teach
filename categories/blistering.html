    Blistering diseases

    How to approach blisters, then pemphigus, bullous pemphigoid, dermatitis herpetiformis and drug-induced blistering including SJS/TEN, with same-day referral routes.

    Approach to blistering diseases

    Sort a blistering rash by where the skin splits (subcorneal, intraepidermal, subepidermal), whether it is immunobullous, and how fast to refer. Biopsy for immunofluorescence is done by specialists, but the referral speed is decided in primary care.

    Never miss: SJS/TEN, pemphigus with mouth erosions, eye involvement, pemphigoid in pregnancy, eczema herpeticum and SSSS in children.

    Pemphigus vulgaris & the pemphigus family

    A rare, serious autoimmune disease with painful mouth erosions and fragile flaccid blisters that quickly become raw areas. It needs urgent or same-day dermatology; UK first-line treatment is rituximab with prednisolone.

    Never miss: Dehydration and sepsis from extensive erosions; paraneoplastic pemphigus; drug-induced pemphigus (thiol drugs: penicillamine, captopril).

    Bullous pemphigoid (incl. MMP & pemphigoid gestationis)

    The commonest autoimmune blistering disease in the UK: tense blisters on an itchy, urticarial base in older people, often after months of unexplained itch. Look for a gliptin, refer, and prefer doxycycline or clobetasol over high-dose prednisolone.

    Never miss: Ocular mucous membrane pemphigoid (blindness), pemphigoid gestationis, and steroid toxicity in frail patients.

    Dermatitis herpetiformis & coeliac disease

    An intensely itchy, symmetrical eruption of tiny blisters on the elbows, knees, buttocks and scalp that is the skin form of coeliac disease. Test for coeliac disease while still eating gluten; a strict gluten-free diet is the long-term treatment.

    Never miss: Anaemia, osteoporosis and small-bowel lymphoma risk; G6PD check and haemolysis/methaemoglobinaemia on dapsone.

    Drug-induced blistering (SJS/TEN, FDE, AGEP)

    Drugs cause a range of blistering reactions, from a single recurring fixed drug eruption to SJS/TEN, which is a medical emergency. Stop the culprit early, record the allergy and report on a Yellow Card.

    Never miss: SJS/TEN: skin pain, fever, mucosal erosions and Nikolsky-positive skin detachment.
