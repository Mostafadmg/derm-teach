    Lumps, lesions & skin cancer

    Benign lumps and lesions, BCC, SCC and cutaneous lymphoma, moles and naevi, melanoma recognition, staging and treatment, NICE NG12 referral routes and teledermatology.

    ~87 min140 images

    0 of 22 lessons complete

    1. 1

      Dermatofibroma

      A common firm button-like papule, usually on the legs, that dimples when pinched. Dermoscopy shows a central white patch with a fine peripheral network. Pigmented forms can mimic a mole or melanoma.

      4 min9 steps9 images

    2. 2

      Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP)

      A rare, slow-growing skin sarcoma that starts as a firm plaque, often on the trunk, and is easily mistaken for a scar, keloid or dermatofibroma. It rarely spreads but recurs locally unless widely excised (often Mohs).

      3 min9 steps2 images

    3. 3

      Sebaceous naevus (naevus sebaceous)

      A yellow-orange hairless plaque on the scalp or face present from birth, which thickens at puberty. Benign tumours can develop within it in adulthood; true BCC is rare, so removal is now case by case.

      3 min9 steps4 images

    4. 4

      Epidermoid & pilar cysts, milia and Gardner syndrome

      Smooth, mobile lumps under the skin. Epidermoid cysts have a central punctum; pilar cysts sit on the scalp. Recognise the inflamed cyst, when to excise, and the rare syndromic clue (multiple facial cysts in Gardner syndrome).

      3 min9 steps5 images

    5. 5

      Lipoma & the sarcoma lump pathway

      Soft, rubbery, mobile fatty lumps under the skin. Nearly all are benign; the job is to know the features (large, deep, growing, painful) that need an ultrasound or sarcoma referral.

      2 min9 steps1 image

    6. 6

      Pyogenic granuloma

      A fast-growing, bright-red, friable lump that bleeds easily, often on a finger, lip or gum after minor injury or in pregnancy. Treat by curettage and cautery with histology, because amelanotic melanoma can look the same.

      4 min9 steps7 images

    7. 7

      Angiokeratomas & Fabry disease

      Dark red to black warty papules from dilated vessels near the surface. Most are harmless (scrotum, vulva); a solitary dark one can mimic melanoma; clusters in the bathing-trunk area of a young person suggest Fabry disease.

      3 min9 steps4 images

    8. 8

      Other skin lumps: neurofibroma, pilomatricoma, myxoid cyst & more

      A quick guide to lumps you will meet less often: neurofibromas and NF1, pilomatricoma in children, digital myxoid cysts, sebaceous hyperplasia and other look-alikes.

      3 min5 steps6 images

    9. 9

      Chondrodermatitis nodularis helicis & ear nodules

      A painful, tender nodule on the rim of the ear that stops people sleeping on that side. Relieve pressure first; know the ear-nodule differentials (BCC, SCC, keratoacanthoma, gouty tophus, AK).

      4 min9 steps5 images

    10. 10

      Seborrhoeic keratosis

      The commonest benign ‘stuck-on’ warty lesion of later life. Learn the dermoscopy clues (milia-like cysts, comedo openings, fissures), when an SK needs a second look, and the sign of eruptive SKs.

      5 min9 steps13 images

    11. 11

      Clear cell acanthoma

      An uncommon benign red, moist dome-shaped papule on the lower leg with a scaly collar. Dermoscopy shows dotted vessels in a ‘string of pearls’ pattern.

      3 min9 steps3 images

    12. 12

      Basal cell carcinoma (BCC)

      The commonest skin cancer: a slowly growing pearly papule or non-healing sore, usually on the face. Learn the subtypes, the dermoscopy clues, the NICE NG12 referral speed, and the treatment ladder (surgery first-line for nodular BCC, BAD 2021).

      7 min9 steps16 images

    13. 13

      Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC)

      A growing, tender, keratotic or ulcerated nodule on sun-damaged skin that can spread to lymph nodes, especially on the lip and ear. Know the precursors (AK, Bowen’s, actinic cheilitis) and refer on the suspected-cancer pathway.

      5 min9 steps8 images

    14. 14

      Mycosis fungoides & cutaneous lymphoma

      The commonest cutaneous lymphoma: fixed, slowly changing eczema- or psoriasis-like patches in sun-protected sites that do not settle with treatment. Most patients have early-stage disease with a normal life expectancy.

      4 min9 steps5 images

    15. 15

      Melanocytic naevi (moles) & the changing mole

      Common moles, how they evolve through life, benign dermoscopy patterns, and how to assess a changing mole with the NICE weighted 7-point checklist. Includes hormones, pregnancy, sunbeds, apps and mole mapping.

      5 min9 steps11 images

    16. 16

      Congenital melanocytic naevi (CMN)

      Moles present at birth. Melanoma risk depends on size: small and medium CMN carry a low (<1%) lifetime risk, large and giant ones about 2–5%, higher with many satellites. Lifelong surveillance and support matter more than surgery.

      4 min9 steps4 images

    17. 17

      Atypical naevus syndrome & familial melanoma

      Many large, irregular moles with a personal or family history of melanoma. Know who to refer for surveillance and genetics (CDKN2A), and what to teach about self-examination and sun protection.

      4 min9 steps5 images

    18. 18

      Halo, blue, Spitz & recurrent naevi

      Special naevi that worry patients and clinicians: halo naevi with a white ring, blue naevi, Spitz and Reed naevi in children, and moles that regrow in a scar.

      4 min9 steps6 images

    19. 19

      Melanoma: recognition & referral

      How to spot melanoma: ABCDE, the NICE weighted 7-point checklist, the ugly-duckling sign and EFG for nodular melanoma, the clinical subtypes (including acral and amelanotic), dermoscopy and AI, and fast referral.

      7 min9 steps22 images

    20. 20

      Melanoma: Breslow, AJCC 8 staging & NICE NG14 treatment

      What happens after referral: excision biopsy, the histopathology report (Breslow, ulceration), AJCC 8th edition staging with current 5-year survival figures, NICE NG14 excision margins, sentinel node biopsy, adjuvant treatment and follow-up.

      5 min9 steps4 images

    21. 21

      Melanoma immunotherapy & targeted therapy: side-effects

      What a primary-care prescriber must know about patients on checkpoint inhibitors or BRAF/MEK inhibitors: immune-related side-effects (colitis, pneumonitis, hypophysitis, diabetes, skin), interactions and when to ring the acute oncology hotline.

      3 min9 steps

    22. 22

      Teledermatology & lesion photography

      How store-and-forward teledermatology works in the UK, the three images every lesion referral needs (regional, close-up with scale, dermoscopic), what it does well, and its pitfalls.

      2 min9 steps
