    Pigment & colour change

    Hypo- and hyperpigmentation. Progressive macular hypomelanosis in full, plus melasma, lichenoid pigmentation and vitiligo (Module 6 Week 3).

    Progressive macular hypomelanosis (PMH)

    Ill-defined, non-scaly pale patches merging on the trunk of young adults. Think of it when ‘pityriasis versicolor’ has no scale and does not respond to antifungals.

    Never miss: Persistent, atypical, progressive patches in an adult may be hypopigmented mycosis fungoides. Refer.

    Melasma & facial melanosis

    Acquired brown facial patches driven by hormones, UV and visible light. Strict tinted sunscreen is the foundation; avoid long-term hydroquinone (ochronosis risk).

    Never miss: Exogenous ochronosis from prolonged hydroquinone or illegal bleaching creams — stop HQ and refer; do not escalate lighteners.

    Contact & lichenoid pigmentation

    Grey-brown facial or flexural pigment from contact allergens (Riehl/PCD) or lichenoid interface dermatitis (LPP, ashy dermatosis). Allergen avoidance and patch testing are key.

    Never miss: Do not keep borrowing potent facial steroids — they mask dermatitis and damage the face.

    Phytophotodermatitis & cutaneous amyloid

    Plant psoralens plus UVA cause streaky burns then long pigmentation; macular/lichen amyloidosis cause rippled itchy brown patches from chronic friction.

    Never miss: Child hand-print phytophotodermatitis can mimic non-accidental injury — take a careful plant/sun history.
