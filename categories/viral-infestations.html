    Infections — viral & infestations

    Viral warts in full. Scabies, head lice, Lyme disease and bite reactions are next (Module 5 Week 3).

    Viral warts (HPV)

    Benign HPV-driven epidermal growths. Most clear on their own. Salicylic acid is the community first line, cryotherapy is clinic-based, and refer the immunosuppressed and anything atypical.

    Never miss: A rapidly growing or ulcerating ‘wart’, especially in an immunosuppressed patient, may be SCC. Refer.

    Scabies

    Burrows, nocturnal itch and itchy contacts. Treat the whole household with permethrin or malathion, and never miss crusted scabies.

    Never miss: Crusted (Norwegian) scabies in a frail or immunosuppressed person: urgent specialist referral the same day.

    Head lice (pediculosis capitis)

    Only a live louse confirms infestation. Wet combing or dimeticone 4% are first-line; permethrin is not recommended in the UK.

    Never miss: No live louse means no treatment. Pubic lice on a child’s eyelashes need a safeguarding pathway.

    Lyme disease & erythema migrans

    An expanding erythema migrans after a tick bite is clinical — treat with doxycycline for 21 days without serology (NICE NG95).

    Never miss: Carditis, meningitis/encephalitis, uveitis, or facial palsy with focal neurology — emergency or same-day pathways. Do not test or give antibiotics after an asymptomatic bite.

    Blister beetle & Paederus dermatitis

    Burning linear blisters after a beetle is crushed on the skin (cantharidin or paederin). Wash promptly; do not crush beetles; refer eye involvement same day.

    Never miss: Eye involvement → same-day ophthalmology. Ingestion of cantharidin → A&E / TOXBASE.

    Papular urticaria & insect bites

    Recurrent itchy grouped papules from flea, mite or bed-bug hypersensitivity. Treat the source (pets, home, pest control) as well as the itch.

    Never miss: Anaphylaxis pathways for systemic allergy; fever after bites abroad; do not antibiotic-treat ordinary itchy redness lasting up to ~10 days.

    Perineal dermatoses

    Anogenital warts and HPV counselling, groin hidradenitis first-line care (bridge to Week 5 HS), and brief DDx: molluscum, PPP, Fordyce, candida and lichen sclerosus.

    Never miss: Do not call every genital papule a wart (PPP/Fordyce are normal). Chronic white genital itch/scarring → lichen sclerosus pathway. Children with anogenital warts need specialist safeguarding consideration.
