    Overview

    Module 6 · Week 2 · Module 1 · Week 5acanthosis nigricansinsulin resistancePCOS

    • Spot it

      Symmetrical velvety grey-brown thickening of the neck or axillae, often with skin tags, in someone with overweight or PCOS.

    • Why

      High insulin / growth factors drive keratinocyte and fibroblast proliferation → papillomatosis and hyperkeratosis.

    • Treat

      Treat the metabolic driver (weight, activity, screen HbA1c/lipids); keratolytics and retinoids are cosmetic adjuncts only.

    • Never miss

      Sudden extensive AN in a slim older adult, especially with tripe palms or oral involvement → urgent suspected-cancer pathway.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    What it is

    Acanthosis nigricans (AN) is symmetrical velvety grey-brown to black thickening of flexural skin. In adolescents and young adults it almost always signals insulin resistance. Rarely, abrupt extensive AN is paraneoplastic (malignant AN).

    Acanthosis nigricans, PCOS and insulin resistance in depth

    Acanthosis nigricans (AN): symmetrical, velvety, hyperpigmented, thickened plaques in the flexures: back and sides of the neck, axillae, groin, inframammary skin, and over the knuckles/elbows. It is very common with obesity (especially in skin of colour) and is a marker of insulin resistance. Rarely it is a paraneoplastic sign.

    2

    Step 2 of 9 · 1 min

    Why it happens

    • Obesity / insulin resistance (commonest): high insulin acts on IGF-1 receptors → papillomatosis.
    • Endocrine: PCOS, Cushing’s, acromegaly, hypothyroidism; drug-induced (systemic steroids, nicotinic acid, some contraceptives, protease inhibitors).
    • Malignant AN: tumour growth factors (notably TGF-α) — strongly linked to gastric adenocarcinoma and other abdominal adenocarcinomas.
    • Malignant AN: tumour-secreted TGF-α (also EGF, IGF-1) activates EGFR on keratinocytes. Usually gastric adenocarcinoma (~60%, intra-abdominal adenocarcinomas overall).
    • Histology: papillomatosis and hyperkeratosis with only mild, irregular acanthosis and little real increase in melanin. The dark colour comes mostly from the thickened keratin. No inflammation.

    How it’s caused

    • Obesity / insulin resistance (commonest): high insulin acts on IGF-1 receptors → papillomatosis.
    • Endocrine: PCOS, Cushing’s, acromegaly, hypothyroidism; drug-induced (systemic steroids, nicotinic acid, some contraceptives, protease inhibitors).
    • Malignant AN: tumour growth factors (notably TGF-α) — strongly linked to gastric adenocarcinoma and other abdominal adenocarcinomas.

    Acanthosis nigricans, PCOS and insulin resistance in depth

    How it is caused

    • Endocrine/benign AN: Hyperinsulinaemia — at high concentrations insulin binds IGF-1 receptors on keratinocytes and dermal fibroblasts → proliferation; Also hyperandrogenism (ovarian), raised free IGF-1, and FGFR3 mutations (syndromic)
    • Malignant AN: tumour-secreted TGF-α (also EGF, IGF-1) activates EGFR on keratinocytes. Usually gastric adenocarcinoma (~60%, intra-abdominal adenocarcinomas overall).
    • Why some people and not others? It depends on the degree of hyperinsulinaemia, receptor expression/sensitivity and genetics (ethnicity, skin type), friction and maceration in flexures, and — in malignancy — the tumour's growth-factor secretion.
    • Histology: papillomatosis and hyperkeratosis with only mild, irregular acanthosis and little real increase in melanin. The dark colour comes mostly from the thickened keratin. No inflammation.
    • Other associations: obesity, T2DM, PCOS — HAIR-AN syndrome (hyperandrogenism, insulin resistance, AN); Cushing's, acromegaly, hypothyroidism; drugs: nicotinic acid, systemic glucocorticoids, insulin injection sites, oestrogens/COC, protease inhibitors, testosterone, fusidic acid (topical); genetic/syndromic forms; acral AN in skin of colour (often isolated, benign)
    Next: What you’ll see
    3

    Step 3 of 9 · 1 min

    What you’ll see

    • Neck (back/sides), axillae, groin, under breasts, knuckles; may be faint on the face.
    • Skin tags often coexist; usually asymptomatic or mildly itchy.
    • Malignant AN red flags: sudden rapid onset in an older, often non-obese adult; mucosal involvement; tripe palms; sudden multiple seborrhoeic keratoses (Leser–Trélat); weight loss or GI symptoms.

    Acanthosis nigricans, PCOS and insulin resistance in depth

    Clinical features & sites (describe the rash)

    Ill-defined, symmetrical, brown-grey velvety thickening with accentuated skin markings, ± skin tags (acrochordons), on the back of the neck; usually asymptomatic or mildly itchy.

    Malignant AN features: sudden onset and rapid spread; extensive, including mucosae (lips, oral cavity) and palms (tripe palms —; pruritus; weight loss; older and non-obese patients; ± Leser–Trélat sign.

    Next: Confirm the diagnosis
    4

    Step 4 of 9 · 1 min

    Confirm the diagnosis

    Key points

    • Clinical. Biopsy only if atypical.
    • Metabolic screen: BMI/waist, BP, HbA1c (or fasting glucose), lipids, LFTs; PCOS screen in females; TSH if indicated.
    • Suspected malignant AN → urgent GP-led suspected-cancer referral (e.g. upper GI pathway per NICE NG12).
    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Diagnosis

    • Clinical. Biopsy only if atypical.
    • Metabolic screen: BMI/waist, BP, HbA1c (or fasting glucose), lipids, LFTs; PCOS screen in females; TSH if indicated.
    • Suspected malignant AN → urgent GP-led suspected-cancer referral (e.g. upper GI pathway per NICE NG12).

    Acanthosis nigricans, PCOS and insulin resistance in depth

    (likely diagnosis)

    • Case: a 22-year-old with oligomenorrhoea for 12 months, weight gain and jawline hirsutism that emerged after stopping a COC 18 months ago, AN on the back of the neck, testosterone 2.3 nmol/L (mildly raised), normal prolactin/TFT/estradiol, fasting glucose 5.5 (upper limit) → PCOS with insulin resistance (HAIR-AN phenotype). Rotterdam criteria (2 of 3): oligo-/anovulation , clinical/biochemical hyperandrogenism , polycystic ovaries (not needed here). The COC had been masking the symptoms.
    • Further tests (International PCOS guideline 2023 / CKS): SHBG/free androgen index, LH/FSH, 17-OH-progesterone (exclude NC-CAH), consider DHEAS (adrenal tumour if very high); pregnancy test; 75 g OGTT (preferred) or HbA1c/fasting glucose for dysglycaemia, repeated every 1–3 years; lipids; BP; screen for depression/anxiety, eating disorders and OSA; exclude Cushing's if features are present; Pelvic USS is not needed when two criteria are already met

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    • Treat the cause: sustained weight management and activity; family-based approach in adolescents; metformin only via GP/specialist if insulin resistance/prediabetes.
    • Review causative drugs with the original prescriber.
    • Cosmetic adjuncts (modest evidence): urea/lactic acid keratolytics, topical retinoid or calcipotriol off-label; specialist peels/lasers.
    • Colour improves slowly over months after metabolic change — scrubbing worsens PIH.

    Acanthosis nigricans, PCOS and insulin resistance in depth

    • Treat the cause: Weight loss of 5–10% improves AN, cycles and androgens; Metformin (off-label in PCOS, BNF) improves insulin resistance and cycle regularity, with modest AN benefit; GLP-1 RAs where eligible (NICE weight-management TAs); Stop culprit drugs
    • PCOS plan (CKS/International guideline 2023): COC — cycle control, endometrial protection (with fewer than 4 periods a year, give cyclical progestogen or a COC to induce withdrawal bleeds and reduce endometrial hyperplasia risk), and hirsutism/acne benefit. Choose by UKMEC (BMI ≥35 → UKMEC 3); Cosmetic hair removal; anti-androgens via a specialist with contraception (the therapeutics notes see Acanthosis nigricans and PCOS); Annual metabolic and mood checks; Fertility advice when needed (letrozole is first-line for ovulation induction — specialist)
    • Skin (cosmetic): keratolytics (urea 10–20%, lactic acid, salicylic acid), topical retinoids (tretinoin — off-label), calcipotriol (off-label), laser (specialist/private). Gentle skin care; do not scrub.
    • Counsel: "It's not dirt and it's not caused by poor hygiene." AN is a warning sign for diabetes — test regularly.

    Red flags / refer

    Rapid onset, extensive or mucosal AN, tripe palms, weight loss, age >40, non-obese → urgent investigation for malignancy (NICE NG12 — upper GI: urgent OGD if appropriate). Hyperandrogenism with testosterone >5 nmol/L or virilisation → urgent endocrinology. Hyperglycaemia.

    IP scope

    Diagnosis support, bloods/OGTT, lifestyle/metformin/COC within competence, topical keratolytics, and referral for red flags or fertility/specialist anti-androgen therapy.

    8

    Step 8 of 9 · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • Velvet, not dirt — the alcohol-swab test separates terra firma-forme dermatosis.

    • In a young person, AN is a cardiometabolic risk marker; lifestyle advice for the whole household matters most.

    • Use weight-stigma-sensitive language.

    • Often seen alongside adult female acne / PCOS — see acne vulgaris.

    • Sudden, widespread or mucosal acanthosis nigricans, or tripe palms, in an older adult: see skin signs of lung cancer and internal malignancy.
