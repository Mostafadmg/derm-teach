    Step 1 of 9 · 1 min

    Overview

    Not every papule is acneLook for comedonesStop the culpritModule 6 Week 2

    • Spot it

      Facial papules without comedones, or monomorphic pustules after steroids or long antibiotics.

    • Why

      Neurovascular + innate immune (rosacea); drug or microbiome shifts (folliculitis variants).

    • Treat

      Rosacea: triggers, SPF, ivermectin ± doxycycline MR. Mimic? Stop the culprit steroid or tetracycline.

    • Never miss

      Rosacea fulminans and ocular keratitis: urgent. Don’t cycle antibiotics for Gram-negative folliculitis.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    What it is

    Not every facial papule is acne. This page covers the common mimics taught with Module 6 Week 2: rosacea (and rosacea fulminans), steroid-induced acne/rosacea/perioral dermatitis, and folliculitis variants (Gram-negative, Malassezia, Pseudomonas/hot-tub).

    True acne vulgaris is covered separately: acne vulgaris.

    Start: Why it happens
    2

    Step 2 of 9 · 1 min

    Why it happens

    Each mimic has its own trigger.

    • Rosacea: neurovascular, cathelicidin, Demodex, UV; heat, alcohol, spice, steroids.
    • Steroid acne / perioral dermatitis: oral, inhaled, topical or bleaching steroids.
    • Gram-negative folliculitis: months of oral tetracycline → Klebsiella, Enterobacter, Proteus.
    • Malassezia folliculitis: yeast in oily follicles, often after antibiotics or steroids.
    • Hot-tub folliculitis: Pseudomonas in under-chlorinated spa water.
    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    How it’s caused

    • Rosacea: neurovascular dysregulation, innate immunity (cathelicidin), Demodex, UV; triggers include heat, alcohol, spicy food, hot drinks, topical steroids.
    • Steroid acne / rosacea / perioral dermatitis: oral, inhaled, topical or illicit bleaching steroids.
    • Gram-negative folliculitis: long-term oral tetracyclines for acne/rosacea → overgrowth of Klebsiella, Enterobacter, Proteus.
    • Malassezia folliculitis: yeast in oily follicles, often after antibiotics or steroids.
    • Hot-tub folliculitis: Pseudomonas in under-chlorinated spa water.
    Next: What you’ll see
    3

    Step 3 of 9 · 2 min

    What you’ll see

    Comedones, age, site and history sort them out.

    • Acne: teens, comedones, face + chest + back.
    • Rosacea: 30–60, flushing, central convex face, no comedones; eyes, phyma.
    • Perioral dermatitis: papules round the mouth, vermilion spared; after facial steroid.
    • Steroid acne: sudden monomorphic papules / pustules on chest and back.
    • Gram-negative: new perinasal pustules after months of tetracycline that first worked.
    • Malassezia: itchy monomorphic pustules, chest and back. Hot tub: under swimsuit 1–4 days after spa.

    Mimics to recognise

    • Papulopustular rosacea

      Papules and pustules on a red background; no comedones.

      Cheeks

    • Rosacea fulminans

      Explosive painful nodules on a swollen face; young women.

      Central face

    • Steroid eruption

      Monomorphic papulopustules after steroid exposure.

      Face, chest

    • Perioral dermatitis

      Perioral papules, clear rim at the lip border.

      Mouth, nose

    • Gram-negative

      Sudden pustular flare round the nose on long antibiotics.

      Perinasal

    • Malassezia

      Itchy, uniform small follicular pustules.

      Chest, back

    • Hot tub

      Follicular pustules after spa use.

      Swimsuit area

    Next: Confirm the diagnosis
    4

    Step 4 of 9 · 1 min

    Confirm the diagnosis

    Clinical

    • Comedones dominate → it is acne.
    • Ask about steroids, inhalers and antibiotic duration.

    Swab

    • Worse on long antibiotics: swab pustules and anterior nares; request Gram-negative culture.

    Bloods

    • ANA only if lupus is realistic.

    Exposure

    • Spa or hot tub in the last few days?
    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Diagnosis

    Clinical. Swab pustules and anterior nares (request Gram-negative culture) when acne worsens on long antibiotics. Consider ANA only if lupus is realistic. Do not diagnose rosacea if comedones dominate — rethink acne.

    Next: Treat
    6

    Step 6 of 9 · 1 min

    Treat

    Treat rosacea by phenotype; remove the cause of the others. Check BNF.

    Treatment ladder

    1. 1

      FoundationRosacea basics

      • Trigger diary, gentle skincare, daily sunscreen; avoid topical steroids.
      • Persistent erythema: brimonidine (warn about rebound).
    2. 2

      Step 1Rosacea papules

      • Ivermectin 1% cream first line, or azelaic acid 15%, or metronidazole 0.75%.
      • Moderate–severe: add doxycycline 40 mg MR (licensed); erythromycin if tetracyclines unsuitable.
    3. 3

      Step 2Steroid / perioral

      • Stop the facial steroid; taper systemic steroids with the prescriber, never abruptly.
      • Warn of rebound. Topical metronidazole or azelaic acid; oral tetracycline 6–8 weeks if needed (erythromycin if under 12).
    4. 4

      Step 3Folliculitis variants

      • Gram-negative: stop the tetracycline, swab, refer (isotretinoin or culture-guided antibiotics).
      • Malassezia: topical or oral antifungal; stop unnecessary antibiotics / steroids.
      • Hot tub: self-limiting; antibiotics only if severe or immunocompromised.
    5. 5

      SpecialistRosacea fulminans

      • Urgent dermatology. Don’t keep cycling oral antibiotics.

    Doses as in the source notes. Always confirm in the current BNF and local formulary.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Prescriber tip

    Rosacea in depth

    Phenotypes, flushing work-up, ocular rosacea, Demodex and steroid-induced rosacea now have their own page: rosacea.

    Rosacea (NICE CKS / PCDS themes)

    • Trigger diary, gentle skincare, daily sunscreen; avoid topical steroids.
    • Persistent erythema: topical brimonidine (warn about rebound).
    • Mild–moderate papulopustular: ivermectin 1% cream first-line, or azelaic acid 15%, or metronidazole 0.75%.
    • Moderate–severe: topical ivermectin plus oral doxycycline 40 mg MR (licensed) or other tetracycline; erythromycin if tetracyclines unsuitable.
    • Ocular: lid hygiene, lubricants ± oral tetracycline; same-day ophthalmology for pain, photophobia or reduced vision.

    Steroid / perioral dermatitis

    • Stop the topical/inhaled facial steroid exposure (taper systemic steroids with the original prescriber — never abruptly).
    • Warn of a rebound flare. Topical metronidazole or azelaic acid; oral tetracycline 6–8 weeks if needed (erythromycin if under 12).

    Folliculitis variants

    • Gram-negative: stop the tetracycline; swab; specialist options include isotretinoin or culture-guided antibiotics — refer rather than endless antibiotic switches.
    • Malassezia: topical or oral antifungal per local practice; stop unnecessary antibiotics/steroids.
    • Hot-tub: usually self-limiting in days; antibiotics only if severe or immunocompromised.

    Rosacea fulminans

    • Urgent dermatology referral. Do not keep cycling oral antibiotics. Specialist care often uses a short steroid course then isotretinoin (pregnancy exclusions apply).

    UK guidance

    Check BNF

    Confirm doses, pregnancy advice and interactions before prescribing.

    Red flags & referral

    • Same day

      Ocular pain, photophobia or reduced vision → ophthalmology.

    • Urgent

      Rosacea fulminans or suspected acne fulminans → dermatology.

    • Routine

      Phyma needing procedures; diagnostic doubt.

    • Routine

      Acne worse on long tetracyclines: culture for Gram-negatives and stop the antibiotic.

    Red flags & referral

    • Ocular pain, photophobia or reduced vision → same-day ophthalmology.
    • Rosacea fulminans or suspected acne fulminans → urgent dermatology.
    • Phyma needing procedural treatment; diagnostic doubt.

    Next: Prescriber pearls
    8

    Step 8 of 9 · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • No comedones + flushing + central face = rosacea until proven otherwise.

    • Inhaled-steroid perioral dermatitis: advise rinsing and wiping the face after inhalers.

    • Rosacea relapses: maintenance topical therapy is often needed.

    • Full rosacea ladder by phenotype is on the rosacea page.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • No comedones + flushing + central face = rosacea until proven otherwise.
    • Maintenance topical therapy is often needed — rosacea is relapsing.
    • Pharmacists often spot inhaled-steroid perioral dermatitis — advise rinsing/wiping the face after inhalers.
    • Laser/IPL for telangiectasia is usually private, not NHS-funded.
    • For the full rosacea ladder by phenotype, see rosacea.
    Next: Quick self-check
    9

    Step 9 of 9 · 1 min

    Quick self-check

    1. Q1Which single sign separates acne from its mimics?Reveal

      Comedones: present in acne, absent in rosacea and the folliculitis mimics.

    2. Q2New perinasal pustules after months of a tetracycline that first worked?Reveal

      Gram-negative folliculitis: swab (incl. nares), stop the tetracycline, refer.

    3. Q3Papules around the mouth with a clear rim at the lip border after a steroid cream?Reveal

      Perioral (periorificial) dermatitis: stop the steroid, warn of rebound.

    4. Q4Explosive painful facial nodules in a young woman who is systemically well?Reveal

      Rosacea fulminans (pyoderma faciale): urgent dermatology.

    5. Q5Itchy follicular pustules under the swimsuit 2 days after a spa?Reveal

      Hot-tub (Pseudomonas) folliculitis: usually self-limiting.
