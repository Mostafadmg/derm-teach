    Acne prescribing & the combined pill

    The NICE NG198 12-week first-line combinations for acne, how to choose and counsel, antibiotic stewardship, and how combined oral contraceptives (including co-cyprindiol) fit in, with their VTE risk.

    Also calledNICE NG198benzoyl peroxideadapaleneclindamycinlymecyclinedoxycycline+3
    Related pagesAcne vulgarisRetinoids (topical & oral)Hirsutism & the Ferriman–Gallwey scoreAcne scars

    Key facts

    • NICE NG198: first-line acne treatment is a 12-week fixed-combination topical (adapalene + benzoyl peroxide, tretinoin + clindamycin, or BPO + clindamycin).
    • Add an oral tetracycline (lymecycline or doxycycline) with a topical for moderate–severe acne; never use oral and topical antibiotics together.
    • The combined oral contraceptive (COC) is an alternative or add-on for women; choose using UKMEC (VTE, migraine with aura, smoking over 35).
    • Co-cyprindiol has higher VTE risk: use only if other options fail and stop 3 months after acne control.
    • Review at 12 weeks; refer scarring, severe or persistent acne for isotretinoin.

    Overview

    (the case)

    A 19-year-old woman with numerous comedones and pustules on the cheeks, jawline, perioral and nasolabial areas, unresponsive to cleansers. Periods are regular; she does not smoke or drink and has had no treatment yet.

    • Under NICE NG198 definitions this is mild-to-moderate acne: any number of non-inflammatory lesions, up to 34 inflammatory lesions and up to 2 nodules. The lesion count, scarring or psychological impact could move her higher (course teaching: psychological impact can raise the grade).
    • Moderate-to-severe acne = ≥35 inflammatory lesions and/or ≥3 nodules.

    The lower-face/jawline distribution suggests an androgen ("hormonal") component. Regular cycles and no hirsutism make PCOS less likely, but still ask about hirsutism, weight, cycle changes and family history.

    Aetiology: causes and risk factors

    Myths to address

    • "The pill causes acne": progestogen-only methods can worsen acne; combined pills usually improve it.

    How it works (mechanism of action)

    The COC reduces acne by lowering free androgen, which drives sebum production.

    1. 1

      Ovarian suppression

      Ethinylestradiol and progestogen suppress ovulation and ovarian androgen output.

    2. 2

      Raised SHBG

      Oestrogen increases sex hormone–binding globulin, reducing free testosterone.

    3. 3

      Less sebum

      Lower androgen activity shrinks sebaceous output and comedone formation.

    4. 4

      Anti-androgenic progestogens

      Cyproterone, drospirenone and others block androgen receptors.

    How it works (acne pathophysiology → drug targets)

    Four mechanisms:

    1. Androgen-driven sebum production.
    2. Follicular hyperkeratinisation (the microcomedone).
    3. Cutibacterium acnes proliferation/biofilm.
    4. Inflammation (TLR-2, IL-1).

    Clinical features

    Clinical uses & where it fits — NICE NG198 first-line (12-week courses)

    SeverityOffer one of (fixed combinations preferred)
    Mild–moderate(a) adapalene 0.1% + BPO 2.5% gel (Epiduo); (b) tretinoin 0.025% + clindamycin 1% gel (Treclin); (c) BPO 3–5% + clindamycin 1% gel (Duac)
    Moderate–severe(a) adapalene 0.3% + BPO 2.5%; (b) tretinoin + clindamycin; (c) adapalene/BPO + oral lymecycline or doxycycline; (d) azelaic acid 15–20% + oral lymecycline/doxycycline
    AlternativesBPO alone if the above are contraindicated; COC in women (with topicals); oral erythromycin/trimethoprim if tetracyclines are contraindicated (specialist advice)
    • NG198 rules: No topical or oral antibiotic monotherapy, and no topical + oral antibiotic together; Oral antibiotics only with a topical retinoid or BPO; Review at 12 weeks; continue oral antibiotics beyond 6 months only in exceptional cases; Once clear, maintain with a topical retinoid ± BPO (fixed combination)
    • Minocycline is not recommended (lupus-like syndrome, autoimmune hepatitis, blue-grey pigmentation — see.
    • Tetracyclines: Avoid in pregnancy, breastfeeding and under-12s.; Doxycycline causes photosensitivity; take it upright with water and use sun protection; Doses: lymecycline 408 mg once daily; doxycycline 100 mg once daily (BNF — recheck)

    How to choose / assess

    For this case:

    • Start adapalene 0.1%/BPO 2.5% gel once daily at night: it covers comedones and pustules with no antibiotic resistance.
    • Use tretinoin/clindamycin instead if BPO irritation or bleaching is a concern.
    • If she could become pregnant, NICE says to discuss that topical retinoids should not be used in pregnancy; BPO/clindamycin is the option without a retinoid.
    • Discuss her contraceptive needs at the same visit. If she wants contraception anyway, a COC can be added for its acne benefit.

    Key comparisons — COCs in acne

    Does the COC have merit? Yes.

    • How it works: oestrogen ↑ hepatic SHBG (less free testosterone) and suppresses LH-driven ovarian androgen production. Progestogens with anti-androgenic activity (cyproterone, drospirenone, dienogest) also block the androgen receptor and reduce 5α-reductase activity.
    • Evidence: Systematic reviews (Cochrane; Arowojolu 2012) show every COC studied reduces inflammatory and non-inflammatory lesions vs placebo, with few consistent differences between progestogens; A meta-analysis (Koo 2014, which you cited) found COCs comparable to oral antibiotics by 6 months; antibiotics work faster at 3 months
    • In practice: benefit takes 3–6 months. COCs help inflammatory and comedonal acne and acne with hyperandrogenism (PCOS). They are not monotherapy for severe/nodular acne.

Routine monitoring is often unnecessary in young healthy women — follow the local/specialist protocol; Side effects: menstrual irregularity, breast tenderness. Not used in men (gynaecomastia). In the UK it is usually started on dermatology advice

    Diagnosis and investigations

    Mild–moderate

    • Adapalene 0.1% + BPO 2.5%; tretinoin 0.025% + clindamycin 1%; or BPO 3–5% + clindamycin 1%.

    Moderate–severe

    • Adapalene 0.3% + BPO 2.5%; tretinoin + clindamycin; adapalene/BPO + oral lymecycline or doxycycline; or azelaic acid 15–20% + oral tetracycline.

    NG198 rules

    • No antibiotic monotherapy; no topical + oral antibiotic together; oral only with retinoid or BPO; review at 12 weeks.

    Before a COC

    • UKMEC: BMI (≥ 35 = UKMEC 3), BP, migraine with aura (UKMEC 4), VTE history, thrombophilia, postpartum.

    Management

    1. Skin care: non-comedogenic products, a gentle pH-neutral cleanser twice daily, no picking or squeezing, oil-free sunscreen. Diet: no strong evidence for elimination diets (NICE).
    2. 12-week fixed-combination topical (as above), applied thinly to the whole affected area, not just the spots. If irritated, start on alternate nights or wash off after 1 h, then build up to daily.
    3. Discuss contraception, if wanted, choose a COC using UKMEC (above).
    4. Review at 12 weeks: Clear → stop antibiotics and maintain with a topical retinoid ± BPO; Improving → continue; Not improved → switch to another first-line option; Moderate–severe acne that has failed two courses, including an oral antibiotic → refer for isotretinoin
    5. Screen psychologically (mood, self-harm) at every review — acne carries a high psychosocial burden.

    IP scope

    All NICE NG198 primary-care options (topicals, oral tetracyclines, and COCs within contraceptive competence) are within IP scope if you are competent. For co-cyprindiol and spironolactone in acne, follow the local formulary (often specialist advice). Isotretinoin: refer.

    Caution

    Prescribing limits and pitfalls

    • Acne fulminans: Systemic features: dermatologist within 24 hours.
    • Mood: Screen mood and self-harm at every review.
    • BPO bleaches: Towels, clothes, hair.

    Referral

    • Same day

      Acne fulminans (fever, arthralgia, ulcerating acne): seen by dermatology within 24 hours.

    • Same day

      Calf swelling, chest pain or breathlessness on a COC: possible VTE.

    • Routine

      Scarring, nodulocystic, rapidly worsening acne or severe distress: refer for isotretinoin.

    Red flags and when to refer

    • Scarring, nodulocystic or rapidly worsening acne, or severe psychological distress: refer for isotretinoin assessment.
    • Calf swelling, chest pain or breathlessness on a combined pill: possible VTE, same-day assessment.
    • Co-cyprindiol: review at 3 months and stop once acne is controlled (MHRA).

    Red flags / refer (NICE NG198)

    • Acne fulminans → urgent referral, to be seen by a dermatologist within 24 hours (the course "why?"): It is sudden, severe, ulcerating and haemorrhagic acne with systemic features: fever, malaise, arthralgia/bone pain (osteitis), raised inflammatory markers, leucocytosis; It is often triggered by starting isotretinoin, or by testosterone/anabolic steroids; It needs oral corticosteroids quickly (± pausing or reducing isotretinoin) to prevent severe scarring
    • Refer to consultant dermatology for: diagnostic uncertainty; acne conglobata or nodulocystic acne; mild–moderate acne not responding to two 12-week courses; moderate–severe acne not responding to a course that included an oral antibiotic; scarring or persistent PIH; psychological distress or a mental health disorder (also refer to mental health services for suicidal ideation); a suspected underlying endocrine disorder (PCOS with abnormal results → GP/endocrinology)

    Complications and prognosis

    Complications

    • VTE (higher with co-cyprindiol, drospirenone, desogestrel than levonorgestrel), breakthrough bleeding, mood change.

    Prognosis

    • Benefit takes 3–6 months.

    Prescriber pearls (UK pharmacist IP)

    • "Twelve weeks before judging" — show her a calendar.

    • BPO bleaches towels, clothes and hair; retinoids dry the skin and sensitise it to sun.

    • On doxycycline: photosensitivity; oesophagitis (take upright with water); keep indigestion remedies and iron 2–3 h apart.

    • Women on a COC should know the VTE warning symptoms (calf pain/swelling, chest pain, breathlessness) and the stroke symptoms.

    Self-check quiz

    4 questions · optional
    1. Q1NICE moderate–severe threshold?Reveal

      ≥ 35 inflammatory lesions and/or ≥ 3 nodules.

    2. Q2Can you combine oral and topical antibiotics?Reveal

      No: oral antibiotics only with a topical retinoid or BPO.

    3. Q3UKMEC category for migraine with aura on a COC?Reveal

      UKMEC 4 (unacceptable risk).

    4. Q4When to refer for isotretinoin?Reveal

      Moderate–severe acne failing two courses including an oral antibiotic, or scarring / distress.
