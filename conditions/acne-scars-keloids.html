    Cosmetic & aesthetic

    Acne scars & keloids

    Module 6 · Week 6

    Atrophic (ice-pick, boxcar, rolling) vs raised (hypertrophic, keloid) scars, scar vs pigment, and which specialist modalities suit which scar. Prevention is treating active acne early.

    Coversacne scarringice-pick scarsboxcar scarsrolling scarskeloidhypertrophic scarpost-inflammatory hyperpigmentationsilicone gelsubcisionTCA CROSS
    Think of it when

    Depressions or raised scars after inflammatory acne. Patients often call flat pigment ‘scarring’ too.

    What drives it

    Inflammatory destruction of collagen (atrophic), or excess aberrant collagen (hypertrophic, or keloid beyond the original borders).

    Backbone of care

    Control active acne first, then refer to specialist modalities matched to scar type, with realistic expectations.

    Never miss

    Scarring at any acne severity is an NG198 referral trigger. Also look for psychological distress and keloid tendency before procedures.

    What it is

    Scope: counselling and recognition

    For the pharmacist/IP, this page covers recognising types, setting expectations, safety and referral. It is not a procedure how-to (no injection technique, laser settings or peel recipes).

    Acne scarring is fibrous remodelling after inflammatory acne. Atrophic scars follow collagen destruction. Hypertrophic or keloid scars follow excess collagen. Scars are usually permanent but may soften with time or combined treatment. The psychological impact is often high, even when the scarring looks ‘mild’.

    2

    How it’s caused

    • Inflammatory nodules and cysts cause net collagen destruction, giving atrophic scars (ice-pick, boxcar, rolling).
    • Excess collagen gives hypertrophic scars (within the original lesion) or keloids (spreading beyond the original borders).
    • Risk rises with acne severity and duration, picking (acne excoriée) and smoking. Keloids and PIH are more common in skin of colour.
    • Post-inflammatory erythema and hyper- or hypopigmentation are colour change, not true scarring, but patients often call both ‘scars’.
    3

    Clinical features

    TypeAppearanceTypical sites
    Ice-pickNarrow, deep, V-shaped; deeper than wide (about 60–70% of atrophic scars)Cheeks
    BoxcarRound or oval depressions with sharp vertical edgesCheeks, temples
    RollingWider, sloping edges, tethered by dermal bandsCheeks
    Perifollicular elastolysisSmall, pale, follicular atrophic scarsBack, chest
    HypertrophicRaised, same size as the original lesionJawline, chest, upper back
    KeloidFirm raised scar beyond the original borders; may itch or hurtShoulders, chest, jawline, earlobes
    4

    Diagnosis

    Clinical: history of acne plus scar morphology. Document Fitzpatrick skin type, whether acne is still active, and the patient’s goals. Biopsy is rarely needed, only for an atypical, evolving ‘keloid’.

    5

    Differentials

    Look-alikeDistinguishing clues
    PIH or erythema aloneFlat colour change, with no depression or elevation
    Chickenpox scarsHistory of varicella; scattered
    Traumatic or surgical scarHistory of injury
    Dermatofibroma or tumour mimicking a keloidSolitary, atypical nodule. Refer if in doubt
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Prevent

    Treat active acne early and adequately (see Acne vulgaris). Scarring is a referral trigger under NICE NG198. Treat active acne first, before any scar procedures.

    Specialist modalities (overview)

    Scar typeOptions (specialist or cosmetic)
    Ice-pickTCA CROSS (chemical reconstruction of skin scars), punch excision or grafting
    BoxcarResurfacing (fractional lasers, peels), punch techniques, microneedling
    RollingSubcision, dermal fillers or fat, microneedling, fractional laser
    Hypertrophic / keloidSilicone gel or sheet, potent topical steroid under occlusion, intralesional steroid (specialist), pulsed dye laser, cryotherapy, careful revision (high recurrence)

    Combined, multi-session approaches are usual. Refer to dermatology, plastic surgery or a reputable aesthetic clinic, and explain that the aim is improvement, not removal.

    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Scarring or significant distress at any acne severity: refer (NG198).
    • Low mood, anxiety or body dysmorphic concerns: signpost or refer for mental health support.
    • A rapidly growing or atypical ‘keloid’: exclude a tumour.
    • Keloid tendency or skin of colour before elective procedures: use an experienced operator only.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • Separate scar from pigment. The treatments are different.
    • Skin of colour carries a higher risk of PIH and keloid after procedures.
    • OTC silicone gel is a reasonable first step for early raised scars. Use it for months, not weeks.
    • Check isotretinoin status before procedures: see Cosmetic procedures.
