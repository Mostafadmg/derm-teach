    Actinic & seborrhoeic keratoses

    Module 6 · Week 4

    Rough sun-damaged AKs and field-directed treatment options versus harmless stuck-on seborrhoeic keratoses — and when to refer.

    Coversactinic keratosissolar keratosisAKactinic cheilitisseborrhoeic keratosisSKfield change5-FUimiquimod
    Think of it when

    Gritty, scaly, sun-exposed macules that are easier to feel than see — versus waxy “stuck-on” seborrhoeic keratoses.

    What drives it

    AK: UV-driven keratinocyte dysplasia (field cancerisation). SK: benign clonal keratinocyte proliferation.

    Backbone of care

    Photoprotection for everyone; single AKs — cryotherapy; field change — 5-FU / imiquimod / diclofenac per local pathway; reassure on typical SKs.

    Never miss

    Tender, enlarging, ulcerated or hypertrophic lesions — rule out SCC. Pigmented facial “lentigo” that is irregular — think lentigo maligna.

    What it is

    Actinic keratoses (AKs) are common UV-induced dysplastic lesions on chronically sun-exposed skin of older adults. They mark field cancerisation and a small risk of progression to squamous cell carcinoma.

    Seborrhoeic keratoses (SKs) are benign “stuck-on” waxy growths — a frequent Week 4 teaching contrast with AKs and with solar lentigines.

    2

    How it’s caused

    • AK: cumulative UVB/UVA; fair skin, outdoor work, immunosuppression (transplant) raise burden and SCC risk.
    • Actinic cheilitis is AK of the lip vermilion.
    • SK: age-related benign clones; not premalignant (though irritated SKs worry patients).
    3

    Clinical features

    • AK: rough sandpaper scale on erythematous base; face, bald scalp, ears, forearms, hands; often multiple.
    • Hypertrophic AK and cutaneous horn need careful SCC exclusion.
    • SK: waxy, verrucous, stuck-on; milia-like cysts on dermoscopy; trunk and face.
    4

    Diagnosis

    Clinical ± dermoscopy. Biopsy or 2WW/urgent skin cancer referral if SCC or melanoma cannot be excluded. Never destroy an undiagnosed pigmented lesion with cryotherapy or laser.

    5

    Differentials

    Look-alikeClues
    SCCIndurated, tender, enlarging, ulcerated
    Bowen’s diseasePersistent scaly plaque
    SK vs AKStuck-on waxy vs gritty flat scale
    Solar lentigoFlat even brown macule — see solar lentigines
    Lentigo malignaIrregular facial pigment — urgent pathway
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Everyone with AKs

    • Daily broad-spectrum SPF 30–50, hat, behaviour change; skin self-check education.
    • Emollient to reduce scale.

    Lesion- and field-directed options (local formulary / CKS themes)

    • Few discrete AKs: cryotherapy (light freeze) in trained hands.
    • Field change: topical 5-fluorouracil, imiquimod, diclofenac 3% gel, or photodynamic therapy via dermatology — expect an inflammatory treatment phase; counsel clearly.
    • Actinic cheilitis: lower threshold for specialist review.
    • Immunosuppressed: lower threshold for dermatology.

    Seborrhoeic keratoses

    • Reassure when typical. Cryotherapy/curettage only if symptomatic or diagnostic doubt — usually private/cosmetic on the NHS unless catching or bleeding.
    • Ugly-duckling or atypical → refer, do not freeze.
    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Possible SCC or melanoma → urgent suspected-cancer referral (NG12).
    • Rapidly enlarging tender nodule in an AK field.
    • Lip AK with induration or ulceration.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • Feel with a fingertip — AKs are gritty.
    • Treating the field matters more than chasing every spot once cancer is excluded.
    • Warn that 5-FU/imiquimod look worse before they look better.
