    Androgenetic alopecia (male & female)

    Module 6 · Week 6

    Male pattern: recession plus vertex. Female pattern: a widened parting with the frontal line kept. Topical minoxidil for both sexes, finasteride 1 mg for men, and an honest view of PRP and off-label options.

    CoversAGAmale pattern hair lossMPHLfemale pattern hair lossFPHLhair lossbaldingminoxidilfinasteridespironolactoneoral minoxidilPRPplatelet-rich plasma
    Think of it when

    Gradual, non-scarring thinning: frontotemporal recession and vertex in men; widening central parting or crown in women.

    What drives it

    DHT (via 5α-reductase) miniaturises genetically susceptible follicles and shortens anagen.

    Backbone of care

    Topical minoxidil (both sexes) and oral finasteride 1 mg (men), started early and continued. Gains reverse on stopping.

    Never miss

    Scarring alopecia (lost follicle openings, inflammation), hyperandrogenism in women, and telogen effluvium triggers (iron, thyroid).

    What it is

    Androgenetic alopecia (AGA) is genetically predisposed, androgen-mediated, progressive follicular miniaturisation.

    Male pattern (MPHL)Female pattern (FPHL)
    PatternFrontotemporal recession plus vertex (Norwood–Hamilton)Mid-frontal and crown thinning with a widened parting (‘Christmas tree’); frontal hairline usually preserved (Ludwig/Sinclair)
    AndrogensNormalUsually normal. Check for PCOS if there are acne, hirsutism or irregular periods
    2

    How it’s caused

    • DHT (from testosterone via 5α-reductase) shortens anagen and miniaturises follicles in a genetically susceptible scalp.
    • Polygenic inheritance from both sides of the family.
    • Women with true hyperandrogenism may show a male-type pattern with acne, hirsutism and irregular periods (consider PCOS).
    3

    Clinical features

    • Gradual, non-scarring thinning with miniaturised hairs and variable hair diameter.
    • The pull test is usually negative (positive in telogen effluvium).
    • Psychological distress is common in both sexes.
    4

    Diagnosis

    • Clinical ± dermoscopy (miniaturisation, hair diameter diversity).
    • Bloods if there is sudden shedding, signs of scarring, or features of hyperandrogenism or systemic disease in women: ferritin, TFTs, and hormones as indicated.
    • Biopsy is rarely needed.
    5

    Differentials

    Look-alikeDistinguishing clues
    Telogen effluviumAcute diffuse shedding 2–3 months after a trigger; positive pull test
    Alopecia areataPatchy, with exclamation-mark hairs; may be diffuse
    Traction / trichotillomaniaTrauma pattern; broken hairs of different lengths
    Scarring alopeciasLoss of follicle openings, redness, scale, pain. Refer
    Nutritional / thyroidSystemic features; blood tests
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Check doses in the current BNF and SmPC. Off-label agents follow local dermatology advice.

    Established options

    • Topical minoxidil 2% or 5% for both sexes. It must be used continuously. An initial shed is possible in the first weeks. Facial hair can appear if it runs onto the face. Allow about 6 months to judge the effect.
    • Oral finasteride 1 mg (men), a type II 5α-reductase inhibitor. Counsel on sexual and mood side effects (MHRA warning on psychiatric effects and suicidal thoughts). It is teratogenic to a male fetus: contraindicated in pregnancy, and women who are or may become pregnant must not handle crushed or broken tablets. Not licensed for women of childbearing potential.

    Other options: overview, caution and referral

    OptionNotes
    Topical finasterideEmerging; systemic absorption possible. Specialist
    DutasterideOff-label in men. Specialist
    Spironolactone (FPHL)Off-label anti-androgen, especially with hyperandrogenism. Monitor potassium and BP; contraception needed. Specialist or an experienced IP within competence
    Low-dose oral minoxidilOff-label, with growing evidence. Side effects: hypertrichosis, fluid retention, tachycardia. Specialist initiation, not a casual community start
    PRP (platelet-rich plasma)Autologous concentrated platelets injected into the scalp. Mixed evidence quality, non-standard protocols, private cost. An adjunct, not a replacement
    LLLT, transplant, micropigmentation, camouflageVariable evidence. Set expectations
    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Signs of scarring alopecia (lost follicle openings, inflammation, pain or itch): refer.
    • Rapid or patchy loss, or a positive pull test: look for another diagnosis.
    • Women with acne, hirsutism and irregular periods: assess for PCOS or hyperandrogenism.
    • Mood change or sexual dysfunction on finasteride: review and stop if needed (MHRA).
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • Early treatment preserves more hair than late rescue.
    • Stopping treatment means gains reverse over months, so explain this before starting.
    • Women: exclude scarring alopecia and reversible causes (iron, thyroid) before long-term cosmetic treatment.
    • Finasteride counselling must cover psychiatric and sexual side effects and pregnancy safety for partners.
    • PRP: avoid overselling it. Document informed expectations. Platelet disorders and anticoagulation need specialist judgement.
