    Annular (ring-shaped) rashes: differential

    Not every ring is ringworm. Tinea, granuloma annulare, discoid eczema, pityriasis rosea, erythema annulare centrifugum and subacute lupus all form rings. Scale, its position and a skin scraping sort most of them.

    Module 4 · Week 1 · 7 images

    Also coversringworm or notannular scaly patchgranuloma annulareerythema annulare centrifugumherald patchnummular eczematinea corporis
    Think of it when

    A ring-shaped or annular patch, especially one that failed antifungal or steroid treatment.

    What drives it

    Rings form when inflammation spreads outward and clears centrally; the pattern of scale reflects the process.

    Backbone of care

    Scrape scaly rings before treating; avoid steroid–antifungal combinations on undiagnosed rashes; biopsy what persists.

    Never miss

    Tinea incognito, subacute cutaneous lupus (photosensitive, drug-induced), mycosis fungoides and Lyme erythema migrans.

    01

    What it is

    (Module 4 Case 3 teaching frame: solitary annular scaly erythema on chest → later widespread eruption diagnosed as pityriasis rosea. Use the initial lesion to practise annular DDx.)

    An annular (ring-shaped) scaly erythematous patch/plaque is a common primary-care puzzle. Leading differentials: tinea corporis, annular / plaque psoriasis, granuloma annulare (usually non-scaly), herald patch of pityriasis rosea, erythema annulare centrifugum (EAC), discoid eczema, and less often subacute cutaneous lupus or erythema migrans.

    02

    How it’s caused

    ConditionMechanism (plain language)
    Tinea corporisDermatophyte infection of stratum corneum
    Annular psoriasisSame immune axis as plaque psoriasis; ring morphology
    Granuloma annulareGranulomatous dermal inflammation; often idiopathic; DM association sometimes discussed
    Pityriasis roseaLikely viral (HHV-6/7 hypothesis); self-limiting reactive eruption
    EACReactive figurate erythema; trailing scale; hunt for triggers (infection, drugs, rarely malignancy)
    03

    Clinical features

    ConditionScale / edgeItchClues
    Tinea corporisActive raised scaly edge; central clearingVariableAsymmetry; pet/soil exposure; feet/nails reservoir
    Annular psoriasisSilvery scale; may be symmetricMild–modOther plaques, nails, scalp, family Hx
    Granuloma annulareUsually smooth, non-scaly ringsMild/noneDorsum hands/feet, ankles; kids/young adults
    PR herald patchOval plaque with collarette scaleMildDays–weeks later → Christmas-tree secondary rash
    EACTrailing (inner) scale; slow centrifugal spreadMildTrunk/thighs; chronic/recurrent

    Case 3 arc: Herald patch misread as isolated annular lesion → antifungal or mild TCS may “fail” as secondary PR eruption appears.

    04

    Diagnosis

    • History: pets, steroids already used, herald then cascade, joint/nail clues, Lyme geography/tick bite.
    • Mycology scrape before committing to prolonged TCS if tinea plausible (single most useful primary-care test).
    • Wood’s lamp limited value for most corporis species.
    • Review in 1 week if solitary lesion — watch for PR secondary rash or tinea expansion.
    • Biopsy if persistent atypical annular disease (EAC, SCLE, GA confirmation).
    05

    Differentials

    Look-alikeDistinguishing clues
    Tinea vs PR heraldMycology; PR often follows with widespread oval lesions on cleavage lines
    Tinea vs GAScale vs no scale; KOH
    Annular psoriasis vs tineaSymmetry, silvery scale, nail/scalp; KOH negative
    Erythema migransExpanding after tick bite; Lyme serology/pathway
    Nummular eczemaCoin plaques, very itchy, often legs; not true advancing ring
    SCLEPhotosensitive annular plaques; ANA/Ro; systemic screen
    06

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Pharmacist / IP
    • Suspected tinea: topical terbinafine or imidazole (duration per site/BNF); oral terbinafine if extensive/failed topical — check interactions/LFTs per guidance; treat foot reservoir.
    • Suspected PR: reassure self-limiting (often 6–12 weeks); emollient ± mild–moderate TCS for itch; BAD/PCDS PIL; atypical (palms/soles, very symptomatic) → consider syphilis serology / derm.
    • GA: many localised cases observe; TCS/tacrolimus sometimes tried; extensive → derm.
    • Psoriasis: NICE topical pathway.
    • EAC: treat underlying trigger if found; topical steroids symptomatic; derm if persistent.
    07

    Red flags & referral

    Escalate
    • Expanding ring after a tick bite: erythema migrans, treat on clinical grounds (NICE NG95).
    • Photosensitive annular plaques with systemic features or a new drug (PPI, terbinafine, thiazide): think subacute cutaneous lupus.
    • Persistent, fixed, non-healing annular plaques in an older adult: biopsy to exclude cutaneous lymphoma.
    08

    Prescriber pearls

    • Never use potent TCS long-term on an undiagnosed annular plaque — risk of tinea incognito.
    • Explain PR herald → secondary rash so patients do not think treatment “caused” spread.
    • Recheck feet and nails whenever you diagnose body tinea.
