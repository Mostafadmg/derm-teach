    Biologics, JAK inhibitors & conventional systemics

    Overview

    Specialist-initiatedShared careNo live vaccinesMTX once weekly

    • Spot it

      A patient on methotrexate, ciclosporin, a biologic or a JAK inhibitor needs a prescription, vaccine, antibiotic or advice.

    • Why

      Targeted blockade of TNF, IL-17, IL-23, IL-4/13 or JAK signalling, or broad immunosuppression.

    • Treat

      Specialist-initiated (NICE TAs); shared-care monitoring; no live vaccines; check every new drug (MTX + trimethoprim).

    • Never miss

      Serious infection, TB reactivation, methotrexate toxicity, VTE and MACE with JAK inhibitors (MHRA 2023).

    Start: Why it happens
    2

    Step 2 of 9 · 1 min

    Why it happens

    Target the cytokine that drives the disease.

    • Psoriasis = IL-23 / Th17 axis: dendritic cells release IL-23 → Th17 cells make IL-17A/F → keratinocyte overgrowth and neutrophils; TNF-α amplifies.
    • Blocking IL-23 (p19) or IL-17 gives PASI 90 in most; IL-17 blockade works fastest.
    • Atopic eczema = Th2: IL-4 and IL-13 weaken the barrier and drive IgE and itch; IL-31 is an itch cytokine.
    • Dupilumab blocks IL-4Rα (IL-4 + IL-13); tralokinumab / lebrikizumab block IL-13; JAK inhibitors block many cytokines inside the cell.

    Step 3 of 9 · 1 min

    What you’ll see

    Know the classes, so you recognise them on a hospital-only record.

    • TNF-α: adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab, certolizumab. IL-12/23: ustekinumab.
    • IL-23 (p19): guselkumab, risankizumab, tildrakizumab. IL-17: secukinumab, ixekizumab, bimekizumab, brodalumab.
    • Eczema: dupilumab; tralokinumab, lebrikizumab; JAK inhibitors abrocitinib, upadacitinib, baricitinib.
    • Oral small molecules: apremilast, dimethyl fumarate, deucravacitinib (TYK2).
    • Conventional: methotrexate, ciclosporin, acitretin; azathioprine, mycophenolate off-label in eczema.

    Adverse effects to recognise

    • Dupilumab eye

      Conjunctivitis, blepharitis, keratitis (~10–25%).

      Eyes

    • Severe plaque psoriasis

      Biologic eligibility: PASI ≥ 10 and DLQI > 10.

      Trunk, limbs

    Clinical features

    How it works (target → disease)

    • Psoriasis is driven by the IL-23/Th17 axis: Dendritic cells release IL-23, which drives Th17 cells to make IL-17A/F and IL-22. These cause keratinocyte hyperproliferation and recruit neutrophils; TNF-α amplifies the loop; Blocking IL-23 (p19) or IL-17 gives PASI 90 responses in most patients; IL-17 blockade works fastest
    • Atopic eczema is Th2-dominant: IL-4 and IL-13 impair barrier proteins (filaggrin, loricrin) and drive IgE and itch; IL-31 is a key itch cytokine; Dupilumab blocks the shared IL-4Rα, so it blocks both IL-4 and IL-13 signalling. Tralokinumab and lebrikizumab neutralise IL-13; JAK inhibitors block intracellular signalling for many cytokines (IL-4, IL-13, IL-31, IFN-γ), giving rapid itch relief

    Clinical uses & where it fits (NICE)

    • Psoriasis biologics: Typically for severe disease — PASI ≥10 and DLQI >10 — that has not responded to, is intolerant of, or is contraindicated for conventional systemic therapy (methotrexate, ciclosporin, acitretin) and phototherapy. Some TAs use lower DLQI thresholds; check each one; Choice depends on comorbidity:; psoriatic arthritis → TNF, IL-17 or IL-23 options; IBD → avoid IL-17 (may trigger or worsen IBD); TNF or IL-23 preferred; demyelination or heart failure → avoid TNF; pregnancy → certolizumab; also consider TB and hepatitis risk
    • Eczema biologics/JAKi: moderate–severe AE (e.g. EASI ≥16, DLQI ≥10) after at least one conventional systemic has failed or is unsuitable.
    • Where primary care fits: These are hospital-initiated and hospital/homecare-supplied ("red") drugs. GPs and IPs do not prescribe them; They must still be recorded on the GP record as "hospital-prescribed", so that interactions, vaccines and infection risk are visible

    How to choose / assess (what you do in primary care)

    • Specialist screen before starting: TB (IGRA ± CXR), hepatitis B/C, HIV, VZV serology, pregnancy, malignancy and demyelination history, vaccines up to date (give live vaccines ≥4 weeks before starting).
    • During treatment: infections, vaccines, surgery planning, pregnancy, new symptoms.

    Step 4 of 9 · 1 min

    Confirm the diagnosis

    Psoriasis (NICE)

    • PASI ≥ 10 and DLQI > 10 after conventional systemic and phototherapy failed or unsuitable.

    Eczema (NICE)

    • Moderate–severe (e.g. EASI ≥ 16, DLQI ≥ 10) after ≥ 1 conventional systemic.

    Pre-start screen

    • TB (IGRA ± CXR), hepatitis B/C, HIV, VZV, pregnancy; live vaccines ≥ 4 weeks before.

    Step 5 of 9 · 1 min

    Lookalikes

    • TNF inhibitors

      TB reactivation, serious infection, paradoxical PPP, demyelination, heart failure.

    • IL-17 inhibitors

      Candidiasis, new or worse IBD: avoid in IBD.

    • Dupilumab

      Ocular surface disease, facial redness, eosinophilia, oral HSV.

    • JAK inhibitors

      MHRA 2023: MACE, malignancy, VTE, serious infection; caution ≥ 65, smokers, CV risk.

    • Ciclosporin

      BP and creatinine: reduce or stop if creatinine rises > 25–30%.

    More detailfull notes · 3 min

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Patients on biologics/JAKi — primary-care checklist

    • Record the drug (hospital-only flag), the dose interval and the homecare provider; check interactions with every new prescription.
    • Infection: Lower threshold for review and antibiotics; Hold the next dose during a significant infection and contact the specialist team; Fever or serious infection → same-day assessment (presentations may be atypical)
    • Vaccines: Inactivated vaccines (influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal) are encouraged; No live vaccines (e.g. MMR, yellow fever, live intranasal flu in children); For shingles, use the non-live Shingrix, which immunosuppressed adults are eligible for under the NHS programme, not the live Zostavax
    • Pregnancy planning: specialist advice. Many biologics can be continued; JAKi are contraindicated.
    • Skin-cancer checks (JAKi, TNF-i, previous PUVA or ciclosporin).

    Methotrexate safety (IP level — the conventional systemic you are most likely to co-manage)

    • Once WEEKLY. MHRA DSU (2020) followed fatal overdoses from inadvertent daily dosing: The prescriber must record the day of the week on the prescription and in the record; The patient gets clear counselling and the patient card/booklet; Pharmacies dispense 2.5 mg tablets only (avoid 10 mg tablets in the same patient) and check understanding at every supply; Never write "as directed"
    • Folic acid 5 mg once weekly on a different day (some regimens use up to 6 days a week, never on the methotrexate day). It reduces GI, mucosal and hepatic toxicity.
    • Monitoring (BSR/BAD shared care — follow the local protocol): Baseline: FBC, U&E/creatinine, LFTs (ALT/albumin), ± hepatitis B/C, HIV, VZV, CXR. In psoriasis, add PIIINP or transient elastography/FIB-4 (liver fibrosis risk with metabolic syndrome or alcohol); Then every 2 weeks until on a stable dose for 6 weeks, then monthly for 3 months, then every 3 months; Test more often after a dose change, in renal impairment and in older people
    • Stop and seek advice if (check local thresholds): WCC <3.5 × 10⁹/L, neutrophils <1.6, platelets <140; ALT/AST >100 IU/L (or >2× ULN), or an unexplained fall in albumin; falling eGFR; new cough or breathlessness (pneumonitis); mouth ulcers, sore throat, fever or bruising (marrow suppression); rash
    • Key interactions: Trimethoprim and co-trimoxazole: contraindicated (pancytopenia); other antifolates; NSAIDs reduce renal clearance. Occasional low-dose use is often accepted in RA; psoriasis protocols may avoid them — follow the specialist; PPIs (with high-dose MTX) and penicillins reduce clearance; Live vaccines; Alcohol (keep within limits, or avoid in psoriasis — hepatotoxicity); acitretin (hepatotoxicity); leflunomide
    • Pregnancy/fertility (UK SmPC/BNF): contraindicated in pregnancy: Women: effective contraception during treatment and for at least 6 months after the last dose; Men: contraception during treatment and for at least 3 months after (some SmPCs say 6 months — check the product); no semen donation
    • Dose in skin disease: typically 7.5–25 mg once weekly, oral or subcutaneous; sometimes a 2.5–5 mg test dose first. Specialist-initiated.

    Ciclosporin (eczema/psoriasis; specialist-initiated, short courses)

    • Monitor BP and creatinine; stop or reduce if creatinine rises >25–30% from baseline.
    • Many CYP3A4 interactions (clarithromycin, azoles, grapefruit, St John's wort).
    • Avoid phototherapy and sun (skin cancer).
    • Gingival hyperplasia, hypertrichosis, tremor; hyperkalaemia (avoid potassium-sparing drugs).

    Oral corticosteroids — course Case 5 comment

    • Psoriasis: avoid systemic steroids. Withdrawal can trigger generalised pustular or erythrodermic psoriasis (photo A06 shows localised pustular psoriasis; see the the psoriasis notes).
    • Severe eczema: A short course (e.g. prednisolone ~0.5 mg/kg, up to 30–40 mg, for ≤1–2 weeks, tapered as needed — specialist/GP judgement) can "buy time" while phototherapy or systemics are arranged; Rebound is common; avoid repeated courses; If used for ≥3 weeks or repeatedly, consider HPA suppression, bone health and the NHS Steroid Emergency Card (NatPSA 2020)

    Red flags / refer

    On a biologic, JAKi, MTX or ciclosporin:

    • fever or sepsis; TB symptoms
    • new neurological symptoms (TNF-i — demyelination)
    • jaundice; unexplained bruising or bleeding
    • persistent cough or breathlessness (MTX pneumonitis; infections on JAKi)
    • new IBD symptoms on an IL-17 inhibitor
    • eye pain or visual loss on dupilumab
    • chest pain or VTE symptoms on a JAKi
    • pregnancy.

    IP scope

    No initiation of biologics or JAKi (hospital-only). Methotrexate and ciclosporin only under shared care, if competent and locally agreed: continuing supply, monitoring and acting on results. Every IP should flag hospital-only drugs on the record and manage interactions.

    Red flags & referral

    • Same day

      Fever, sore throat, mouth ulcers or bruising on methotrexate: same-day FBC; withhold until reviewed.

    • Same day

      Serious infection or TB symptoms on a biologic or JAKi: urgent assessment, contact team.

    • Urgent

    • Urgent

      New IBD symptoms on IL-17 inhibitor; eye pain or visual loss on dupilumab.

    Red flags & referral
