    Blister beetle & Paederus dermatitis

    Burning linear blisters after a beetle is crushed on the skin (cantharidin or paederin). Wash promptly; do not crush beetles; refer eye involvement same day.

    Step 1 of 9 · 1 min

    Overview

    Cantharidin / paederinNo biteDon’t crushEye → same day

    • Spot it

      Burning (not itchy) blisters or linear ‘kissing’ plaques on exposed skin 24–48 h after night-time outdoor exposure or travel.

    • Why

      Vesicant toxin in beetle haemolymph (cantharidin in Meloidae, paederin in Paederus) causing intraepidermal blistering, without a bite.

    • Treat

      Flick off, don’t crush; wash with soap and water; cool compresses; short topical steroid; leave small blisters intact.

    • Never miss

      Eye involvement → same-day eye casualty. Ingested cantharidin → A&E / TOXBASE.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    What it is

    Blister beetle dermatitis is an acute irritant contact reaction to cantharidin in Meloidae beetles (e.g. Lytta vesicatoria, Mylabris). The beetle does not bite — lesions follow crushing or brushing it on the skin, often at night near lights. It is uncommon in the UK and mainly seen in travellers or outdoor workers from endemic regions.

    Paederus (rove beetle) dermatitis is the closely related look-alike taught alongside it. The toxin is paederin, producing linear and “kissing” lesions. Management overlaps heavily.

    Start: Why it happens
    2

    Step 2 of 9 · 1 min

    Why it happens

    A chemical burn from a crushed beetle.

    • Blister beetles (Lytta vesicatoria, Mylabris): cantharidin breaks desmosomes → acantholysis, usually no scarring (also used on warts / molluscum).
    • Paederus rove beetles (‘Nairobi fly’): paederin → delayed linear erythema, vesicles, pustules.
    • Uncommon in the UK: travellers and outdoor workers; beetles attracted to night lights.
    • Swallowed cantharidin (‘Spanish fly’) is systemically toxic.
    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    How it’s caused

    • Cantharidin breaks desmosomes → acantholysis and an intraepidermal blister with little dermal inflammation (usually no scarring). The same mechanism is used therapeutically on warts and molluscum.
    • Paederin from crushed Paederus beetles causes delayed linear erythema then vesicles/pustules.
    • Ingesting cantharidin (e.g. as a purported aphrodisiac) is systemically toxic — a medical emergency, not a skin problem.
    Next: What you’ll see
    3

    Step 3 of 9 · 2 min

    What you’ll see

    Burning, linear, on exposed skin.

    • Onset hours to ~24–48 h; the patient often doesn’t link it to an insect.
    • Vesicles / bullae on red skin, linear or bizarre where the beetle was wiped.
    • ‘Kissing’ lesions across flexures (elbow, neck) favour Paederus.
    • Burning / stinging > itch. Sites: face, neck, arms, legs; finger-transfer to eyes or genitals.
    • Heals in ~1 week; pigment may last in skin of colour.

    Clinical features

    • Onset within hours to about 24–48 h; the patient often does not link the insect.
    • Vesicles/bullae on an erythematous base — often linear or bizarre where the beetle was wiped; “kissing” lesions in flexures favour Paederus.
    • Burning or stinging more than itch.
    • Exposed sites: face, neck, arms, lower legs; toxin can be finger-transferred to eyes or genitals.
    • Heals in about a week, sometimes with lasting pigmentation in skin of colour.

    Confirm the diagnosis

    Clinical

    • Burning blisters after beetle contact, travel, camping, night lights; clusters of cases support it.

    Diagnosis

    Clinical: burning blisters on exposed skin after possible beetle contact, travel, camping or night lights. Clusters of cases support the diagnosis. Swab only if secondary infection or HSV/impetigo is realistic.

    Next: Treat
    6

    Step 6 of 9 · 1 min

    Treat

    Same first aid for both beetles.

    Treatment ladder

    1. 1

      FoundationFirst aid

      • Blow or flick the beetle off; don’t crush or swat.
      • Wash promptly with soap and water; keep hands off eyes and genitals.
      • Cool compresses; leave small blisters intact; drain large tense bullae aseptically, roof on; simple analgesia.
    2. 2

      Step 1Prescriber options

      • Short potent topical steroid on inflamed non-facial skin (mild / moderate on face or flexures).
      • Antihistamine if itchy; sedating at night if sleep is poor.
    3. 3

      Step 2Infection

      • Treat as impetigo / cellulitis per NICE; no routine prophylactic antibiotics.

    Safety limits

    Prevention

    Nets, screens, cover skin, shake bedding, lights off near open windows.

    Doses as in the source notes. Always confirm in the current BNF and local formulary.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    First aid

    • If a beetle lands on skin, blow or flick it off — do not crush or swat.
    • Wash promptly with soap and water; avoid touching eyes or genitals.
    • Cool compresses; leave small blisters intact; drain large tense bullae aseptically keeping the roof on.
    • Simple analgesia.

    Prescriber options

    • Short course of potent topical corticosteroid on inflamed non-facial skin (mild/moderate on face or flexures).
    • Antihistamine if itch; sedating at night if sleep is poor.
    • Secondary infection: treat as impetigo/cellulitis per NICE — no routine prophylactic antibiotics.

    7

    Step 7 of 9 · 1 min

    Red flags & referral

    • Same day

      Eye pain, redness, photophobia or reduced vision: irrigate and same-day eye casualty.

    • 999 / A&E

      Ingestion of cantharidin: A&E / NPIS (TOXBASE).

    • Urgent

      Extensive blistering, genital involvement, severe pain or systemic illness; returned traveller with fever.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Red flags & referral

    • Eye pain, redness, photophobia or reduced vision → irrigate and same-day eye casualty.
    • Extensive blistering, genital involvement, severe pain or systemic illness.
    • Ingestion → A&E / NPIS (TOXBASE).
    • Returned traveller with fever — think beyond beetle dermatitis.
    Next: Prescriber pearls
    8

    Step 8 of 9 · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • Ask “Did it burn or itch?” Burning linear blisters → beetle; itchy grouped papules → bites.

    • Quiz photos of kissing lesions are usually Paederus; first aid is the same.

    • Never crush a beetle on the skin.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • Ask “Did it burn or itch?” Burning linear blisters after lights outdoors → beetle; itchy grouped papules → bites.
    • Prevention: nets, screens, cover skin, shake bedding; never crush beetles on skin.
    • Teaching: quiz images of linear kissing lesions are usually Paederus, not Meloidae — but first aid is the same.
