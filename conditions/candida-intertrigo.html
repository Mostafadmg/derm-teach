    Infections — fungal

    Candidal intertrigo

    Adult core · deepened with Module 5 Week 1

    Beefy-red, macerated skin folds with satellite pustules. Moisture control plus a topical azole or nystatin, and look for diabetes or recent antibiotics.

    Coverscandidacandidiasisthrush skinyeast infection skin foldsintertrigoerosio interdigitalisnystatinclotrimazole
    Think of it when

    Bright, beefy-red, moist plaques in a skin fold, with satellite papules or pustules beyond the edge.

    What drives it

    Opportunistic Candida albicans overgrowth in warm, wet, occluded folds.

    Backbone of care

    Keep the fold dry, then a topical imidazole or nystatin. Address the risk factors.

    Never miss

    Undiagnosed diabetes or immunosuppression, and flexural psoriasis or tinea that will not respond.

    What it is

    Candidal intertrigo is a yeast infection of moist skin folds, usually caused by Candida albicans. Common sites are under the breasts, under an abdominal apron, the groin, the axillae and the finger or toe webs (erosio interdigitalis blastomycetica).

    Intertrigo itself is inflammation of the skin folds from heat, moisture, friction and poor ventilation. Secondary Candida infection is common, and satellite papules and pustules are the key diagnostic clue.

    2

    How it’s caused

    Candida is an opportunist. It overgrows where skin is wet, warm, occluded and macerated.

    Risk factors

    • Diabetes (look for it)
    • Recent antibiotics or systemic steroids
    • Immunosuppression
    • Obesity and deep skin folds
    • Incontinence, nappies and pads
    • Wet work, for finger-web disease
    3

    Clinical features

    • Beefy-red, macerated plaques in folds, often glistening or eroded.
    • Satellite papules and pustules beyond the main edge. This is the key sign.
    • Peripheral peeling scale at the margin.
    • Soreness, itch or burning.
    • In the groin, Candida often involves the scrotum, which tinea tends to spare.

    Look elsewhere

    Candidal intertrigo can coexist with oral or genital thrush, so ask and examine.

    4

    Diagnosis

    • Usually clinical, based on the site and satellite lesions.
    • Take a swab or scrapings for yeast if uncertain, recurrent or not responding. Swab for bacteria if secondary infection is suspected.
    • Check HbA1c or glucose if recurrent or there are other risk features.
    5

    Differentials

    Look-alikeDistinguishing clues
    Irritant (simple) intertrigoFriction and moisture redness without satellites; mirror-image on both sides of the fold
    Flexural (inverse) psoriasisSharply defined salmon-pink plaque, no satellites; psoriasis at other sites or nails
    Tinea crurisArcuate scaly active edge spreading onto the thigh; scrotum usually spared
    ErythrasmaBrown-red, finely scaly patch; coral-red under Wood’s light (Corynebacterium)
    Seborrhoeic dermatitisGreasy scale; also scalp and face
    Hailey–Hailey disease / pemphigus vegetans (rare)Chronic, eroded, recurrent fold disease that does not respond. Refer
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Moisture control: the most important step

    • Dry the folds thoroughly after washing (pat dry, or use a cool hairdryer).
    • Use absorbent interface textiles or cotton separators. Choose barrier products for incontinence.
    • Wear loose cotton clothing, and give weight-management advice where relevant.

    Topical antifungal

    • Imidazole (clotrimazole or miconazole) or nystatin. Continue until 1–2 weeks after clearance.
    • For very inflamed folds, a short course of a combination with mild steroid (e.g. hydrocortisone with clotrimazole or miconazole) is sometimes used. Follow the local formulary and limit to about 7 days.

    Oral

    Fluconazole for severe or recalcitrant disease. Check interactions and pregnancy status.

    Look for and treat the cause

    Review for diabetes, recent antibiotics and immunosuppression.

    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Recurrent or extensive candida: screen for diabetes and consider immunosuppression.
    • No response to an adequate course: reconsider the diagnosis (psoriasis, tinea, erythrasma) and take samples.
    • Spreading cellulitis, fever or a systemically unwell patient: escalate.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • Satellites mean Candida. No satellites and a very sharp edge suggests flexural psoriasis.
    • Treatment fails without moisture control. Make the drying advice practical and specific.
    • OTC clotrimazole is a sensible first pharmacy intervention for limited disease.
    • In finger-web disease, ask about wet work and rings.
    • Satellites mean Candida until proven otherwise.
    • The toe-web ‘dermatophytosis complex’ is often a mixed yeast, dermatophyte and bacterial picture. It needs thorough drying plus the right antimicrobial.
    • KOH-negative fold rash, or one that keeps coming back? Work through intertrigo and the flexural differential.
