    Chondrodermatitis nodularis helicis & ear nodules

    A painful, tender nodule on the rim of the ear that stops people sleeping on that side. Relieve pressure first; know the ear-nodule differentials (BCC, SCC, keratoacanthoma, gouty tophus, AK).

    Step 1 of 9 · 1 min

    Overview

    Painful ear noduleSide slept onPressure relief 4–6 weeksBiopsy if not settling

    • Spot it

      A small painful crusted nodule on the helix or antihelix, worse lying on that side, in an older adult.

    • Why

      Pressure on cartilage with poor blood supply → inflammation and cartilage damage (‘a pressure sore of the ear’).

    • Treat

      Pressure-relief pillow / ear protector for 4–6 weeks; consider potent topical steroid; refer if failing or atypical.

    • Never miss

      SCC or BCC of the ear; keratoacanthoma (rapid crater); tophaceous gout.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    What it is

    A benign, painful, inflamed nodule of the ear cartilage and overlying skin. It is commonest on the helix in older men and on the antihelix in women.

    Ear nodule differentials

    The pinna is a high-risk site for skin cancer: it is sun-exposed and thin-skinned, and spread to the parotid and cervical nodes happens early. BAD 2020 classifies ear cSCC as high-risk by site.

    Start: Why it happens
    2

    Step 2 of 9 · 1 min

    Why it happens

    A pressure sore of the ear cartilage.

    • Little padding and poor blood supply over cartilage.
    • Sustained pressure (sleeping on one side, headsets, phones, hats, CPAP), cold, sun damage → perichondritis → focal cartilage necrosis that extrudes.
    • One-sided sleeping (after stroke, arthritis, CPAP).
    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    How it’s caused

    Pressure-induced ischaemia. Ear cartilage has little subcutaneous padding and a poor blood supply. Sustained pressure (sleeping on one side, headsets, phones, hats), cold or sun damage causes perichondrial inflammation and focal cartilage necrosis that extrudes through the skin. One-sided sleeping is typical, e.g. after a stroke, or with arthritis or CPAP masks. In Case 2 with a previous right-sided stroke, ask which side she lies on.

    Ear nodule differentials

    Chronic UV (helix, especially in men with short hair), immunosuppression, and for gout, urate deposition in cool tissue.

    Next: What you’ll see

    Step 3 of 9 · 1 min

    What you’ll see

    Small, crusted, exquisitely tender.

    • Firm round 4–10 mm nodule with a central crust, scale or keratin plug; may ulcerate.
    • Wakes them at night lying on it; light squeeze → disproportionate pain.
    • Helix (men) or antihelix (women), on the side slept on.
    • Dermoscopy is non-specific: can’t exclude SCC / BCC.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Clinical features

    • A firm, round, 4–10 mm nodule with a central crust, scale or keratin plug, sometimes ulcerated.
    • Exquisitely tender: it wakes the patient at night when they lie on it, and light squeezing causes disproportionate pain (the course bedside test). Usually on the side the patient sleeps on.

    Ear nodule differentials

    DiagnosisCluesPhoto
    CNHVery tender, central crust, the side slept on
    cSCCIndurated, keratotic or ulcerated, growing over weeks to months, bleeds. Often painless or mildly tender. ± palpable pre-auricular/cervical node
    KeratoacanthomaRapid growth over weeks, a dome with a central keratin crater, may involute
    BCCPearly, rolled edge, telangiectasia, slowly growing; often posterior ear or sulcus
    Actinic keratosisRough, flat-to-slightly-raised scale; not nodular or tendersee Worked cases

    Weathering nodules / elastotic nodulesSmall, white, painless, bilateral helical-rim papules—
    Cutaneous hornKeratin projection; the base may be SCC
    Next: Confirm the diagnosis
    4

    Step 4 of 9 · 1 min

    Confirm the diagnosis

    History

    • Which side do they sleep on? Headsets, CPAP?

    Examine

    • Squeeze test; palpate pre-auricular / cervical nodes.

    Biopsy when

    • Rapid growth, bleeding, induration, > 1 cm, pearly edge, painless, no improvement after 6–8 weeks offloading, immunosuppression, anxiety.

    Urate

    • If tophi suspected (not in a flare).
    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Diagnosis

    • Clinical: site + pain + central crust + sleeping history.
    • Dermoscopy: central white-yellow keratin/ulcer with a radiating whitish "starburst" and peripheral dotted or linear vessels. It is non-specific and cannot reliably exclude SCC/BCC.
    • Biopsy when: atypical features (rapid growth, bleeding, induration, a size >1 cm, a rolled pearly edge, no pain); no improvement after 6–8 weeks of strict offloading; immunosuppression; or patient anxiety.

    Ear nodule differentials

    Pain pattern, growth tempo, and palpation of regional nodes. Biopsy anything unexplained. Serum urate (when not in an acute flare) if tophi are suspected.

    Differentials

    See Ear nodule differentials (SCC, BCC, keratoacanthoma, AK, gouty tophi, also cutaneous horn, wart, elastotic nodules of the ear, weathering nodules, chilblain).

    Ear nodule differentials

    As above. Also chilblain (cold, bilateral, purple), relapsing polychondritis (the whole ear red, sparing the lobe), and discoid lupus.

    Next: Treat
    6

    Step 6 of 9 · 1 min

    Treat

    Offload first, for everyone.

    Treatment ladder

    1. 1

      FoundationPressure relief

      • Foam ear protector (OTC / online), pillow with a hole or travel pillow; sleep on the other side; remove headset / phone pressure.
      • At least a month; recurrence common if offloading stops.
      • Analgesia; emollient / protective dressing over the crust.
    2. 2

      Step 2Second line

      • Potent topical steroid; intralesional triamcinolone (trained; atrophy risk) via GP / GPwSI / dermatology.
    3. 3

      SpecialistRefer

      • Failing or atypical: biopsy / surgery (cartilage excision).

    Safety limits

    Aspirin

    Don’t stop for minor skin surgery (BSDS 2023).

    Doses as in the source notes. Always confirm in the current BNF and local formulary.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Step 1: pressure relief, for everyone (first-line; the course answer)

    • A pressure-relieving device: a CNH prosthesis/"ear protector" made of foam with a hole for the ear (sold OTC and online), a pillow with a hole, or a travel pillow; sleep on the other side. Remove headphone and phone pressure.
    • Resolution or major improvement occurs in a large proportion of patients within weeks to months. Recurrence is common if offloading stops.
    • Analgesia as needed. Emollient/protective dressing over the crust.

    Step 2: second-line (GP, GPwSI or dermatology)

    OptionNotes
    Intralesional triamcinoloneSpecialist/trained GP. Risk of atrophy
    Potent topical steroid ± occlusionLow-quality evidence. IP-prescribable (e.g. betamethasone valerate 0.1%) for a short trial only once SCC/BCC has been reasonably excluded
    Topical nitroglycerin (GTN) 2% gelOff-label. Small studies show benefit via vasodilatation. Headache and hypotension. Check PDE-5 inhibitor co-prescription (contraindicated). Not routinely available as a dermatology product in the UK
    CryotherapyPainful, variable results
    Surgical excision of the abnormal cartilage ± skinFor refractory cases. Recurrence 10–30%

    Refer: NG12 1.7.4 says to consider a suspected cancer pathway referral if SCC is suspected (a growing, indurated, bleeding, non-tender or atypical ear nodule). Refer routinely if CNH is not settling after 6–8 weeks of offloading.

    Ear nodule differentials

    • Suspected SCC or keratoacanthoma: NG12 1.7.4, consider a suspected cancer pathway referral. BAD guidance is to treat KA as SCC (excise) because they cannot be reliably distinguished clinically.
    • Suspected BCC: NG12 1.7.5, routine referral, unless the site or size makes delay important (1.7.6). The ear is a high-risk "H-zone" site.
    • Gouty tophi: a GP/IP gout pathway. Urate-lowering therapy (allopurinol, treat to target <360 µmol/L, or <300 if tophaceous), per NICE NG219 (gout, 2022). Review diuretics.
    • AK: cross-reference the older skin pages notes see Worked cases (field therapy).

    Step 7 of 9 · 1 min

    Red flags & referral

    • Urgent

      Rapidly growing, ulcerating or bleeding ear nodule, or with a nearby node: suspected SCC (NG12).

    • Routine

      No improvement after 6–8 weeks of strict offloading: biopsy.

    Next: Prescriber pearls
    8

    Step 8 of 9 · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • Very painful at night → CNH. Painless and growing → SCC / KA. Both can coexist.

    • Explain it as a pressure sore; adherence to offloading is the key.

    • Check immunosuppressant history (azathioprine, ciclosporin): SCC risk.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • Case 2 medicines: aspirin is relevant only if a biopsy or excision is done. Do not stop aspirin for minor skin surgery (BSDS 2023; see the skin surgery notes). Losartan and simvastatin are not causative.
    • Explain the mechanism ("a pressure sore of the ear cartilage"). Adherence to offloading for at least a month is the key to success.

    Ear nodule differentials

    • Painless ear nodule plus rapid growth means think SCC/KA. Very painful at night means think CNH. But both can coexist, which is why non-resolving CNH gets a biopsy.
    • Check transplant or immunosuppressant history (azathioprine, ciclosporin, tacrolimus): cSCC risk rises up to 65–100× in organ transplant recipients.
