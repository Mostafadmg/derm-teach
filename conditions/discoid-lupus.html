    Discoid lupus erythematosus

    Module 4 · Week 1

    Chronic cutaneous lupus causing red, scaly plaques on the face, ears and scalp that scar, depigment and cause permanent hair loss. Strict photoprotection, potent steroids and early referral prevent disfigurement.

    CoversDLEchronic cutaneous lupusscarring plaquescarpet-tack scalehydroxychloroquinescarring alopecia lupus
    Think of it when

    Red, scaly, coin-shaped plaques on the face, ears or scalp that leave scarring, pigment change or bald patches.

    What drives it

    Autoimmune interface dermatitis triggered by UV; smoking makes it worse and blunts hydroxychloroquine.

    Backbone of care

    Broad-spectrum sunscreen and sun avoidance, stop smoking, potent topical or intralesional steroid; specialist hydroxychloroquine.

    Never miss

    Scarring alopecia, systemic lupus (screen symptoms and bloods), and SCC in long-standing scars.

    What it is

    Discoid lupus erythematosus is the most common form of chronic cutaneous lupus. Scarring inflammatory plaques favour head and neck; scalp disease causes scarring alopecia. Most patients have skin-limited disease, but a minority evolve toward systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE). Module 4 Formative Quiz-1 answer was DLE (vs alopecia areata, LPP, tinea capitis, trichotillomania).

    2

    How it’s caused

    • Autoimmune interface dermatitis with follicular involvement.
    • UV exposure flares lesions.
    • Risk factors emphasised in teaching: darker skin phototypes have higher rates of DLE and more disfiguring dyspigmentation/scarring; smoking associated with DLE activity/treatment resistance; female predominance overall (quiz noted male sex is not the higher-risk factor).
    3

    Clinical features

    FeatureDetail
    PlaquesWell-defined erythematous discs; adherent scale; follicular plugging; “carpet tack” scale when removed
    ScarringCentral atrophy, permanent hair loss on scalp, pigment change
    SitesFace, ears, scalp, neck; occasionally below neck
    SymptomsMay burn/itch; cosmetic distress high (quiz vignette)
    4

    Diagnosis

    • Clinical + skin biopsy (most important confirmatory test in quiz cluster).
    • Bloods: ANA, ENA, complement — useful to screen for systemic disease but may be negative in purely cutaneous DLE.
    • Mycology if tinea capitis plausible; trichoscopy helps vs AA/LPP.
    5

    Differentials

    Look-alikeDistinguishing clues
    Alopecia areataNon-scarring; exclamation hairs; no discoid scale plaques
    Lichen planopilarisScarring; perifollicular scale; less classic facial discoid plaques
    Tinea capitisBroken hairs, lymphadenopathy, mycology+
    TrichotillomaniaBizarre geometric; hairs of different lengths
    Psoriasis / seb derm scalpNon-scarring usually; different morphology
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    • Strict photoprotection (SPF, hats); smoking cessation.
    • Potent/very potent TCS or intralesional steroid (often derm); topical calcineurin inhibitors on face sometimes.
    • Antimalarials (hydroxychloroquine) — specialist/shared-care with monitoring.
    • Early derm referral for scalp/face to minimise irreversible scarring.
    • Screen for SLE symptoms (arthritis, serositis, renal, photosensitivity beyond skin, cytopenias).
    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Joint pain, mouth ulcers, fatigue, chest pain or Raynaud’s: screen for SLE (ANA, FBC, urine dipstick) and refer.
    • Scalp DLE with hair loss: urgent dermatology (scarring alopecia).
    • Hydroxychloroquine: baseline and annual eye screening after 5 years (RCOphth).
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • Emphasise scarring risk — early treatment matters.
    • Camouflage / psychological support for visible disease.
    • Check BNF interactions if hydroxychloroquine co-prescribed later (retinopathy monitoring is specialty).
