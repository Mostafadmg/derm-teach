    Drug eruptions

    DRESS & AGEP (severe drug reactions)

    Module 1 · Week 3

    Two severe cutaneous adverse reactions that are easy to miss: DRESS (late-onset rash, fever, facial oedema, eosinophilia and organ damage) and AGEP (sudden sheets of tiny sterile pustules within days of a drug).

    Coversdrug reaction with eosinophiliaDIHSacute generalised exanthematous pustulosisRegiSCARallopurinolcarbamazepine
    Think of it when

    A widespread rash with fever 2–8 weeks after a new drug (DRESS), or sudden pinhead pustules in the folds within days (AGEP).

    What drives it

    T-cell mediated reactions, with viral (HHV-6) reactivation in DRESS and neutrophil recruitment in AGEP.

    Backbone of care

    Stop the culprit and arrange same-day hospital assessment; bloods for eosinophils, LFTs, renal function; lifelong avoidance and alert.

    Never miss

    Liver, kidney, lung or heart involvement in DRESS; misdiagnosing AGEP as pustular psoriasis or infection.

    What it is

    • DRESS (drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms; also called DIHS): a delayed, multi-organ reaction with fever, rash and internal organ involvement. Mortality ~2–10%.
    • AGEP (acute generalised exanthematous pustulosis): an abrupt eruption of sterile, non-follicular pinhead pustules on oedematous erythema. Usually self-limiting; mortality <5%.
    2

    How it’s caused

    How it is caused

    • DRESS: type IVb (Th2/eosinophils) with sequential herpesvirus reactivation (HHV-6, then EBV/CMV), which explains the relapsing course. Culprits: allopurinol, carbamazepine, phenytoin, lamotrigine, phenobarbital, sulfonamides/dapsone, minocycline, vancomycin, abacavir/nevirapine, strontium. Latency 2–8 weeks (long).
    • AGEP: type IVd (T cells → IL-8 → neutrophils). Culprits: aminopenicillins, macrolides, cephalosporins, pristinamycin, hydroxychloroquine, diltiazem, terbinafine. Latency <48 h (antibiotics) — up to 1–2 weeks for some drugs. IL36RN variants overlap with generalised pustular psoriasis.

    Diagnosis

    FBC with differential and film, LFT, U&E, urinalysis, troponin/ECG if symptomatic, TFT, lipase, HHV-6 PCR, skin biopsy. AGEP: biopsy shows subcorneal/intraepidermal spongiform pustules (to distinguish it from pustular psoriasis). Patch testing later (useful for both).

    5

    Differentials

    DRESS: viral exanthem, lymphoma (especially angioimmunoblastic), hypereosinophilic syndrome, SJS/TEN, Kawasaki disease. AGEP: generalised pustular psoriasis (history of psoriasis, slower onset, longer course), subcorneal pustular dermatosis, pustular DRESS, folliculitis, SJS/TEN (early AGEP can show Nikolsky-like sloughing).

    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    • Stop the culprit, document, Yellow Card, and avoid for life (including cross-reacting drugs).
    • DRESS: Same-day hospital assessment if fever/facial oedema/eosinophilia or abnormal LFTs; Mild disease without significant organ involvement: potent topical steroids; With organ involvement: systemic corticosteroids (e.g. prednisolone 0.5–1 mg/kg/day) tapered slowly over 6–12 weeks — rapid tapers cause relapse; Monitor organs for months, and check TFT/glucose for 1–2 years
    • AGEP: stop the drug; potent TCS; antipyretics; supportive care. Systemic steroids are rarely needed. Usually managed in dermatology or with admission if unwell.

    Red flags / refer

    Fever + rash + facial oedema/lymphadenopathy 2–8 weeks after a new anticonvulsant/allopurinol/sulfonamide → same-day admission (check FBC/LFT/U&E). Widespread pustules within days of an antibiotic → same-day dermatology.

    IP scope

    Recognise, stop, refer, document, Yellow Card. After discharge: support the specialist steroid taper (steroid card if prolonged), and monitor TFT/glucose per the specialist letter.

    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Suspected DRESS: same-day hospital assessment, because hepatitis, nephritis and myocarditis can follow.
    • Allopurinol (HLA-B*58:01), carbamazepine (HLA-B*15:02) and lamotrigine are high-risk starts: counsel to report a rash early.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • DRESS has the longest latency of the SCARs (2–8 weeks), and AGEP the shortest (hours–2 days).
    • "Allopurinol + CKD + recent start + rash = DRESS/SJS risk" — dose adjustment and HLA-B*58:01 awareness.
