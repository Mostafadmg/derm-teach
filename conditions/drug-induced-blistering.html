    Blistering diseases

    Drug-induced blistering (SJS/TEN, FDE, AGEP)

    Module 4 · Week 6

    Drugs cause a range of blistering reactions, from a single recurring fixed drug eruption to SJS/TEN, which is a medical emergency. Stop the culprit early, record the allergy and report on a Yellow Card.

    CoversSJSTENStevens-Johnsontoxic epidermal necrolysisfixed drug eruptionAGEPlinear IgAdrug eruptionallopurinollamotriginecarbamazepineHLA-B*1502
    Think of it when

    Blistering, dusky or pustular rash 4–28 days after starting a drug (days for AGEP; hours for repeat fixed drug eruption).

    What drives it

    T-cell-mediated keratinocyte death (SJS/TEN, FDE), neutrophilic reactions (AGEP) or drug-triggered autoantibodies (linear IgA, pemphigoid, pemphigus).

    Backbone of care

    Identify and stop the culprit, admit SJS/TEN, document the allergy clearly, Yellow Card, and screen high-risk drugs (HLA-B*1502 before carbamazepine in at-risk groups).

    Never miss

    SJS/TEN: skin pain, fever, mucosal erosions and Nikolsky-positive skin detachment.

    What it is

    This is a group of reactions in which a drug causes vesicles, bullae, pustules or epidermal necrosis. They range from trivial (localised FDE) to life-threatening (TEN). For the IP, drug causation is the main reason to know them.

    Clinical features

    SJS/TEN classification by detached BSA: SJS <10%, SJS/TEN overlap 10–30%, TEN >30% (Module 4 exam Q27, 40% = TEN).

    • Prodrome: fever, sore throat and eye symptoms 1–3 days before the skin.
    • Then painful dusky macules or atypical flat targets, which form bullae and sheet-like detachment with a positive Nikolsky sign.
    • ≥2 mucosal sites are involved (oral haemorrhagic crusting of the lips, conjunctivitis, genital; photo E02).

    SSSS is the toxin-mediated mimic in young children: superficial peeling, mucosa spared, and the child is irritable and tender.

    4

    Diagnosis

    Diagnosis is mostly clinical, with urgent specialist confirmation and frozen-section or standard biopsy. SCORTEN (calculated in the first 24 h) predicts mortality. Its 7 points are:

    1. age ≥40
    2. malignancy
    3. HR ≥120
    4. BSA ≥10%
    5. urea >10 mmol/L
    6. bicarbonate <20
    7. glucose >14

    For drug causation use ALDEN (for SJS/TEN). Patch testing is useful later for FDE and AGEP. For DRESS (the systemic DDx), RegiSCAR.

    5

    Differentials

    • SSSS (child, superficial, no mucosa)
    • EM major (HSV, acral targets, <10%)
    • generalised bullous FDE
    • AGEP (pustules, not detachment)
    • DRESS (facial oedema, eosinophilia, organ involvement, latency 2–8 weeks)
    • GVHD
    • paraneoplastic pemphigus
    • linear IgA (TEN-like)
    • phototoxic reactions
    • burns
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    • Suspected SJS/TEN or extensive or mucosal blistering: STOP all non-essential drugs immediately, especially the likely culprits started 4–28 days ago; 999 / same-day ED for transfer to a burns or dermatology intensive unit (UK SJS/TEN guideline, Creamer 2016). Don't wait for a GP appointment; Ophthalmology within 24 h, and urology/gynaecology/oral medicine input; Specialist adjuncts: ciclosporin, IVIG (the evidence for blocking Fas-FasL is mixed and UK guidance does not routinely recommend it), steroids, and anti-TNF (etanercept, being studied)
    • AGEP: stop the drug, give supportive care and a potent TCS. Refer the same day if febrile or extensive.
    • FDE: stop and avoid the culprit; a potent TCS for 1–2 weeks; document the specific drug.
    • Drug-induced BP or pemphigus: stop the culprit and follow the specialist pathway. Recovery may take months.
    • Allergy documentation: Record the exact drug and reaction in the GP record (SNOMED) and on the patient's allergy list; Report to MHRA Yellow Card (all serious reactions; anything involving a black-triangle drug); For SJS/TEN: lifelong avoidance of the culprit and structurally related drugs, and advice for first-degree relatives about HLA risk. A MedicAlert bracelet is reasonable
    • Prevention (pharmacist role): HLA-B*15:02 screening before carbamazepine in people of Han Chinese or Thai ancestry (BNF/MHRA); Start allopurinol low (100 mg) and titrate in CKD; Follow the lamotrigine titration schedule (halve doses with valproate), and counsel all new starters to stop and seek help with any rash, fever or mouth ulcers in the first 8 weeks
    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Skin pain, fever, dusky targets, mucosal erosions or skin detachment: SJS/TEN, stop all non-essential drugs and call 999 / admit (burns unit or ICU).
    • Widespread sterile pustules with fever after a new drug (AGEP): same-day assessment.
    • Rash with fever, facial swelling, lymph nodes and abnormal liver tests: DRESS.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • Ask "What new medicine did you start in the last 8 weeks?" at every acute rash with fever, skin pain or mucosal involvement.
    • Skin pain out of proportion + mucosal erosions + dusky skin = SJS/TEN until proven otherwise.
    • Paracetamol can cause FDE, so don't assume over-the-counter means innocent. Always ask about OTC and herbal products.
