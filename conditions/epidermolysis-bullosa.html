    Epidermolysis bullosa (EB)

    Inherited skin fragility in which minimal friction causes blisters. Recognise it, handle the baby gently, and refer urgently to a specialist EB centre. Primary care supports under the specialist plan.

    Step 1 of 9 · 1 min

    Overview

    Module 6 · Week 1EBEB simplexjunctional EB

    • Spot it

      A neonate or infant with blisters and erosions from minimal friction (hands, feet, nappy area), ± absent skin at birth, nail dystrophy or mouth blisters.

    • Why

      Inherited defects in adhesion proteins; the level of split defines the type (simplex, junctional, dystrophic, Kindler).

    • Treat

      Urgent referral to an EB centre, gentle handling, no adhesives, and soft silicone dressings under specialist guidance.

    • Never miss

      Airway involvement (hoarse cry in JEB), sepsis, dehydration or failure to thrive, and SCC in chronic RDEB wounds.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    What it is

    A group of inherited mechanobullous disorders: skin (and often mucosa) blisters after minimal trauma, because adhesion molecules are defective. This is different from EB acquisita, which is autoimmune and usually affects adults.

    Step 2 of 9 · 1 min

    Why it happens

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    How it’s caused

    Loss of structural integrity at a specific skin level means minor shear causes a blister. Inheritance is autosomal dominant or recessive depending on subtype. Key Module 6 links: DEB is caused by type VII collagen (COL7A1) defects, and chronic RDEB wounds carry a high risk of cutaneous SCC.

    Next: What you’ll see
    3

    Step 3 of 9 · 1 min

    What you’ll see

    • Blisters and erosions at friction sites (hands, feet, belt line, nappy area). They may be present at birth.
    • Nail dystrophy, milia and scarring (especially dystrophic types). Mitten deformities in severe RDEB.
    • Mucosa: mouth, oesophagus (dysphagia), larynx (hoarse cry in severe JEB).
    • Localised EBS often presents when the child starts crawling or walking.
    • A neonate with unexplained blistering, with or without congenital absence of skin: consider EB until proven otherwise.

    Confirm the diagnosis

    Key points for this step are in More detail below.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Diagnosis

    Urgent recognition and referral to a specialist EB centre (UK: e.g. GOSH and other DEBRA-linked centres). Subtyping uses immunofluorescence mapping, electron microscopy and genetic testing. Primary care does not attempt to subtype.

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Note

    A specialist multidisciplinary disease

    The IP and pharmacist role is recognition, safe interim handling, timely referral, and medicines support under the EB team’s plan.

    Immediate

    • Refer urgently any baby with unexplained fragility or blistering to paediatric dermatology or the EB team. For GOSH, contact the dermatology registrar via switchboard.
    • Handle gently: no adhesive tapes, lift on a soft pad rather than under the arms, keep the environment cool, and reduce friction.
    • Do not lance blisters unless trained in EB technique. Specialist nurses teach sterile piercing and silicone dressings.

    Ongoing primary-care support

    • Dressings: soft silicone or low-adherent, with no ordinary adhesives. Emollients and paraffin to lubricate.
    • Pain, constipation, nutrition, dental and eye care through the MDT.
    • Infection: colonisation is common. Discuss significant infection with the EB team.
    • Medicines optimisation: liquid formulations if there are oesophageal strictures, avoid rectal routes where possible, and check interactions.
    • Awareness: Oleogel-S10 (birch triterpenes) is a specialist wound therapy for selected JEB and DEB wounds.
    • RDEB: surveillance for SCC. Any changing chronic wound needs urgent dermatology review.

    • Routine

    Step 8 of 9 · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • Give parents DEBRA UK support details early.

    • Genetic counselling for future pregnancies is a specialist role.

    • Never blame ‘rough handling’ alone when true fragility is present.

    • Even mild localised EBS benefits from a correct diagnosis (footwear and blister care education).
