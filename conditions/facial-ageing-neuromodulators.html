    Overview

    Module 6 · Week 5wrinklesfacial ageingdynamic lines

    • Spot it

      Someone asking about frown lines, crow’s feet or “preventative Botox”, or worried after a recent aesthetic treatment.

    • Why

      Intrinsic ageing plus UV/smoking drive volume loss and elastosis; dynamic lines from repeated muscle movement; static lines persist at rest.

    • Treat

      Photoprotection and expectations first; botulinum toxin is POM and face-to-face prescribing only; recognise ptosis, infection and filler emergencies.

    • Never miss

      Filler vascular occlusion (pain, livedo, visual loss) → emergency pathway. Suspected iatrogenic botulism after unlicensed toxin → 999 / UKHSA.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    What it is

    Facial ageing mixes intrinsic (genetics, time) and extrinsic (UV, smoking, pollution) change: volume loss, bone remodelling, elastosis and wrinkles. Dynamic lines appear with expression; static lines remain at rest.

    This page is counselling-level only for community pharmacist IPs: what patients ask about neuromodulators and fillers, UK regulation, expected effects and red flags. It does not teach injection technique, doses, units or reconstitution.

    Why it happens

    • Dynamic lines: repeated frontalis, corrugator, orbicularis oculi and other facial muscle activity.
    • Static lines and folds: dermal thinning, fat-pad descent, bone resorption, gravity.
    • Botulinum toxin type A temporarily blocks acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction → reduced muscle contraction for about 3–4 months.
    • HA fillers restore volume by placing hyaluronic-acid gel in soft tissue — different products and planes (specialist detail).

    Step 3 of 9 · 2 min

    What you’ll see

    • Glabellar frown lines, forehead lines, crow’s feet are the common aesthetic requests.
    • Expected toxin timeline: onset over days, peak around 2 weeks, duration about 3–4 months.
    • Common local effects: bruising, short-lived headache, asymmetric smile if diffusion occurs.
    • Filler: immediate volume; swelling and bruising common early.

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Before anyone injects (counselling)

    • Daily SPF, stop smoking, realistic expectations — toxin softens dynamic lines; it does not lift sagging skin or replace volume.
    • Botulinum toxin for cosmetic use is a prescription-only medicine. GPhC guidance: pharmacist prescribers must examine face to face; remote prescribing of these medicines is not appropriate.
    • Illegal under-18 cosmetic toxin/fillers (Botulinum Toxin and Cosmetic Fillers (Children) Act 2021).
    • Advise licensed UK product via a regulated, insured practitioner — never buy toxin online for self-injection.

    Aftercare themes (practitioner-led detail)

    • Follow the treating clinician’s aftercare (often avoid rubbing, lying flat or vigorous exercise for the first hours).
    • Review at about 2 weeks rather than unplanned “top-ups”.
    • OTC “Botox-like” peptide creams have weak evidence — do not oversell.

    Filler adverse-effect awareness (no procedure detail)

    • Early: swelling, bruising, lumps. Delayed: nodules, biofilm infection — refer back to the injector/dermatology.
    • Vascular occlusion: severe pain, livedo, blanching soon after injection → emergency (injector/A&E; hyaluronidase is a specialist treatment).
    • Visual loss / ophthalmoplegia after filler: sight-threatening emergency — call 999; do not delay.

    Red flag

    Unlicensed toxin / botulism

    Descending weakness, dysarthria, dysphagia or breathing difficulty after aesthetic injections → 999. UKHSA may advise on antitoxin; notify health protection; Yellow Card. Check UK-licensed product and legitimate supply chain.

    Note

    Therapeutic facial palsy toxin

    Specialist facial-palsy teams use toxin for synkinesis and asymmetry alongside physiotherapy. Acute Bell’s palsy pathways (steroid within 72 h, eye protection) are separate — forehead-sparing weakness is stroke until proven otherwise.

    Red flags & referral

    • 999 / A&E

      Visual symptoms or severe pain after filler → emergency.

    • 999 / A&E

      Bilateral descending paralysis after unlicensed injections → 999 / UKHSA botulism pathway.

    • Urgent

      Drooping eyelid with corneal exposure risk → urgent eye care.

    • 999 / A&E

      Stroke pattern facial weakness → 999.
