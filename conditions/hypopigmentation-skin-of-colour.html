    Overview

    Module 6 · Week 3PIHpost-inflammatory hyperpigmentationpiebaldism

    • Spot it

      Dark marks after acne/eczema/friction, or milky-white patches — decide hyperpigmentation vs true depigmentation (vitiligo/piebaldism).

    • Why

      Inflammation drives melanin excess (PIH) or autoimmune/congenital melanocyte loss (vitiligo/piebaldism).

    • Treat

      Treat the cause, stop friction and illegal bleaching, daily tinted SPF, azelaic acid/retinoid for PIH; refer vitiligo/piebaldism pathways.

    • Never miss

      Unlicensed steroid/mercury/hydroquinone “fairness” creams — stop and counsel on harm; Waardenburg features with white forelock need hearing assessment.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    What it is

    In skin of colour, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) and constitutional/flexural darkening are everyday presentations. True depigmentation (vitiligo, piebaldism) needs a different pathway. This page also flags harm from unlicensed bleaching and steroid creams.

    Start: Why it happens
    2

    Step 2 of 9 · 1 min

    Why it happens

    • PIH: any inflammation (acne, eczema, bites, procedures, friction from shaving) leaves excess epidermal ± dermal melanin.
    • Constitutional pigment: periorbital dark circles, maturational and flexural darkening.
    • Piebaldism: congenital KIT-pathway melanocyte absence (stable white forelock + patches).
    • Vitiligo: acquired autoimmune melanocyte loss; associated thyroid and other autoimmunity.

    Step 3 of 9 · 3 min

    What you’ll see

    Types to recognise

    • PIH

      Prior inflammation/injury; brown to grey; fades slowly

    • Pityriasis alba

      Children; ill-defined slightly scaly pale patches; hypo- not de-pigmented

    • Piebaldism

      Present at birth; white forelock; stable islands of normal skin inside patches

    • Vitiligo

      Acquired milky-white patches; Wood’s lamp enhances; may spread

    • Bleaching harm

      Atrophy, striae, telangiectasia, ochronosis, infection

    Step 4 of 9 · 1 min

    Confirm the diagnosis

    Key points

    • History of inflammation, procedures, shaving, cosmetics and bleaching products.
    • Wood’s lamp: chalk-white sharp edges favour vitiligo/piebaldism over pityriasis alba.
    • Vitiligo: consider TSH ± antibodies if indicated; piebaldism: genetics/hearing if Waardenburg features.
    • Cross-check fungal differentials (pityriasis versicolor) when scale is present.

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    PIH and flexural darkening

    • Treat the driver (acne, eczema, bites). Stop harsh scrubs, lemon juice and bleaching creams.
    • Hair removal in darker skin: prefer clippers/trimming or accredited long-pulsed Nd:YAG; avoid aggressive IPL settings.
    • Daily tinted broad-spectrum sunscreen; azelaic acid; topical retinoid off-label; time-limited specialist HQ only.
    • Vitamin D advice for people with darker skin who avoid sun (UK public-health framing).

    Vitiligo / piebaldism

    • Piebaldism: reassurance that it is stable; cosmetic options; genetics if needed — no “repigmenting cream” expectation.
    • Vitiligo: potent topical steroid or calcineurin inhibitor on limited areas; phototherapy/JAK inhibitors are specialist; camouflage and psychological support.
    • Refer early for extensive, facial, or rapidly progressive vitiligo.

    Red flag

    Illegal bleaching / steroid creams

    Advise stopping immediately. Counsel on skin atrophy, infection, adrenal suppression and ochronosis. Signpost licensed pathways and Trading Standards/MHRA concerns for supply.

    Red flags & referral

    • Routine

      Rapidly progressive vitiligo on the face — early dermatology.

    • Routine

      White forelock plus deafness/heterochromia → Waardenburg work-up.

    • Routine

      Systemic steroid side-effects from bleaching creams — medical review.

    Step 8 of 9 · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • Set timelines: epidermal PIH months; dermal longer.

    • Ask what people already put on their skin — many worsen PIH with home acids and bleaching.

    • Milky-white + Wood’s lamp ≠ pityriasis alba.

    • See also progressive macular hypomelanosis and pityriasis versicolor.
