    Keratolysis exfoliativa

    Module 3 · Week 6

    A common, harmless condition of recurrent superficial peeling on the palms and fingers, starting as air-filled blisters, usually in warm weather. Reassure, use emollients and avoid irritants.

    Coverslamellar dyshidrosispeeling palmspalm peelingair-filled blisterscollarette
    Think of it when

    Recurrent ring-like peeling (collarettes) on the palms and fingertips, often in summer, with little itch or inflammation.

    What drives it

    A defect in the cohesion of the outer stratum corneum; sweating and irritants make it worse.

    Backbone of care

    Reassure, emollients and urea or lactic acid creams, avoid soaps and solvents; steroids rarely help.

    Never miss

    Pompholyx, hand eczema, tinea manuum and peeling after infection (scarlet fever, Kawasaki disease in children).

    What it is

    A common, benign, non-inflammatory disorder of recurrent superficial peeling of the palms (soles less often), mostly in young active adults. Synonyms: exfoliative keratolysis, dyshidrosis lamellosa sicca, (recurrent) focal palmar peeling, lamellar dyshidrosis. Often under-recognised and mislabelled as "hand eczema", which is why patients arrive having failed topical steroids.

    2

    How it’s caused

    • Cause unknown; may run in families.
    • Splitting occurs within the stratum corneum — corneodesmosomes (the "rivets" between corneocytes) appear to break down prematurely, so sheets of horny layer detach (Chang et al., BJD 2012 described it as a distinct peeling entity; no causative gene identified).
    • Aggravating factors: heat/summer, palmar hyperhidrosis, and irritants — water, soap, detergents, solvents, friction. Roughly half of patients notice summer worsening.
    • Not an allergy, not infective, and not steroid-responsive inflammation.
    3

    Clinical features

    FeatureDetail
    Earliest signOne or more tiny air-filled (not fluid-filled) superficial blisters on palms/fingers
    EvolutionBlisters burst → expanding collarettes of scale → round/oval peeled patches; multiple lamellae may peel
    SymptomsNot itchy; peeled areas can feel tender/dry and crack
    InflammationAbsent or minimal (no oedema, no vesicles with fluid, no lichenification)
    FingertipsSplit can be deeper — skin feels hard and numb before it sheds (explains the Day 1 numbness)
    CourseHeals to normal skin, then recurs every few weeks; chronic over years; often worse in summer
    NailsNot involved (useful vs psoriasis)
    4

    Diagnosis

    • Clinical — history of recurrent non-itchy peeling + collarettes on non-inflamed palms.
    • Supportive tests if doubt: skin scrapings negative for fungus; patch tests negative; biopsy (rarely needed) shows cleavage within the stratum corneum.
    • Day 1 pearl — the numbness: usually explained by thick, deeply split fingertip horny layer; still ask about diabetes, B12/alcohol, carpal tunnel symptoms and vibration-tool use if numbness is persistent, sensory-distributed or present when skin is intact (one student raised diabetic neuropathy — good safety-netting, but the history here has no risk factors).

    Differentials

    (tutor-endorsed comparison)

    ConditionItchInflammationDistinguishing clues
    Keratolysis exfoliativaNoNoAir-filled blisters → collarettes; recurs every few weeks; summer/sweat; steroid-unresponsive; no nail change; no smoking link
    Irritant contact (hand) dermatitisYes/soreYesErythema, dryness, fissures, glazed fingertips; wet work/irritant history; improves with avoidance + steroid
    PompholyxIntenseVesicularDeep-seated fluid-filled "tapioca" vesicles on finger sides/palms, then peeling
    Palmoplantar psoriasisMild/soreYesSharp border, thick silvery scale, fissures, nail pits/onycholysis, plaques elsewhere, smoking link; usually worse in winter
    Early palmoplantar pustulosis (PPP)Variable/burningYesSterile yellow pustules → brown "rusty" dots; smoker; insteps/thenar eminences

    Others (rare)——Keratolytic winter erythema (inherited, red peeling, winter), palmoplantar keratoderma, localised EB simplex (fluid blisters with friction), circumscribed palmar hypokeratosis, peeling skin syndrome (acral, genetic)
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Explain first: benign, cosmetic-nuisance condition, recurrent, not contagious and not an allergy; tends to improve with age; steroids won't help (stop them — avoids palmar atrophy and wasted courses).

    Self-care / first-line (OTC/GSL/P):

    • Irritant avoidance: cotton-lined waterproof gloves for wet work; soap substitutes; dry hands thoroughly.
    • Emollient / keratolytic hand creams, applied frequently: urea (e.g. 5–10% for maintenance, 10–25% for peeling — several UK OTC brands), lactic acid or salicylic acid-containing products; silicone/dimeticone barrier creams. Students cited higher strengths (urea 20–40%, ammonium lactate 12%, salicylic acid 6%) from US sources — UK availability is limited, so use licensed UK products/specials per formulary.
    • Treat hyperhidrosis if present (topical aluminium chloride hexahydrate at night; refer for tap-water iontophoresis if severe) — reduces summer flares.

    Specialist options (refer if severe, painful fissuring, or diagnostic doubt): acitretin (teratogenic, specialist-only, Pregnancy Prevention Programme), hand PUVA/photochemotherapy — limited evidence, reserved for refractory cases given skin-cancer and burden trade-offs.

    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Peeling fingertips in a child with fever for 5 days or more: think Kawasaki disease (urgent).
    • Peeling after a sore throat and rash: scarlet fever.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • "Peeling but not itchy, not red, worse in summer, steroid-proof" = think KE before re-issuing steroid.
    • Scrape any one-handed or treatment-resistant peeling to exclude tinea before labelling KE.
    • Avoid picking/peeling the lamellae — trim loose edges with clean scissors to reduce painful cracks.
    • Fingertip numbness that persists between episodes → examine/assess for neuropathy.
