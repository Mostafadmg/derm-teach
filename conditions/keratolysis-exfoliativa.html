    Keratolysis exfoliativa

    A common, harmless condition of recurrent superficial peeling on the palms and fingers, starting as air-filled blisters, usually in warm weather. Reassure, use emollients and avoid irritants.

    Step 1 of 9 · 1 min

    Overview

    CommonHarmlessSummerSteroid-proof

    • Spot it

      Recurrent ring-like peeling (collarettes) on palms and fingertips, often in summer, with no itch or redness.

    • Why

      Corneodesmosomes break down early, so sheets of stratum corneum detach.

    • Treat

      Reassure; urea / lactic acid creams, emollients, avoid soaps and solvents. Steroids don’t help.

    • Never miss

      Fingertip peeling in a febrile child: Kawasaki disease. After sore throat and rash: scarlet fever.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    What it is

    A common, benign, non-inflammatory disorder of recurrent superficial peeling of the palms (soles less often), mostly in young active adults. Synonyms: exfoliative keratolysis, dyshidrosis lamellosa sicca, (recurrent) focal palmar peeling, lamellar dyshidrosis. Often under-recognised and mislabelled as "hand eczema", which is why patients arrive having failed topical steroids.

    Start: Why it happens
    2

    Step 2 of 9 · 1 min

    Why it happens

    A split inside the outermost layer, not inflammation.

    • Cause unknown; may run in families.
    • Splitting within the stratum corneum: the ‘rivets’ (corneodesmosomes) between corneocytes fail early.
    • Worse with heat / summer, palmar sweating, water, soap, detergents, solvents, friction.
    • Not an allergy, not infective, and not steroid-responsive.
    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    How it’s caused

    • Cause unknown; may run in families.
    • Splitting occurs within the stratum corneum — corneodesmosomes (the "rivets" between corneocytes) appear to break down prematurely, so sheets of horny layer detach (Chang et al., BJD 2012 described it as a distinct peeling entity; no causative gene identified).
    • Aggravating factors: heat/summer, palmar hyperhidrosis, and irritants — water, soap, detergents, solvents, friction. Roughly half of patients notice summer worsening.
    • Not an allergy, not infective, and not steroid-responsive inflammation.
    Next: What you’ll see
    3

    Step 3 of 9 · 1 min

    What you’ll see

    Air bubble → collarette → peeled patch → normal skin → repeat.

    • Starts as tiny air-filled (not fluid) blisters on palms or fingers.
    • Burst into expanding collarettes; several layers may peel.
    • Not itchy; no redness, oedema or lichenification; may crack and feel tender.
    • Fingertips may feel hard and numb before shedding.
    • Recurs every few weeks, often for years; nails not involved.

    Clinical features

    FeatureDetail
    Earliest signOne or more tiny air-filled (not fluid-filled) superficial blisters on palms/fingers
    EvolutionBlisters burst → expanding collarettes of scale → round/oval peeled patches; multiple lamellae may peel
    SymptomsNot itchy; peeled areas can feel tender/dry and crack
    InflammationAbsent or minimal (no oedema, no vesicles with fluid, no lichenification)
    FingertipsSplit can be deeper — skin feels hard and numb before it sheds (explains the numbness patients describe)
    CourseHeals to normal skin, then recurs every few weeks; chronic over years; often worse in summer
    NailsNot involved (useful vs psoriasis)
    Next: Confirm the diagnosis
    4

    Step 4 of 9 · 1 min

    Confirm the diagnosis

    Clinical

    • Recurrent non-itchy peeling + collarettes on non-inflamed palms.

    Scrape

    • Skin scrapings negative for fungus.

    Patch test

    • Negative if done; biopsy rarely needed.

    Numbness?

    • Usually thick split fingertip skin; persistent or dermatomal → check for neuropathy (diabetes, B12, carpal tunnel).
    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Diagnosis

    • Clinical — history of recurrent non-itchy peeling + collarettes on non-inflamed palms.
    • Supportive tests if doubt: skin scrapings negative for fungus; patch tests negative; biopsy (rarely needed) shows cleavage within the stratum corneum.
    • Pearl — the numbness: usually explained by thick, deeply split fingertip horny layer; still ask about diabetes, B12/alcohol, carpal tunnel symptoms and vibration-tool use if numbness is persistent, sensory-distributed or present when skin is intact (diabetic neuropathy is a sensible safety-net).

    Next: Treat
    6

    Step 6 of 9 · 1 min

    Treat

    Explain first: benign, recurrent, not contagious, not an allergy.

    Treatment ladder

    1. 1

      FoundationExplain and protect

      • Stop steroids: they don’t help and risk atrophy.
      • Cotton-lined gloves for wet work; soap substitutes; dry hands well.
    2. 2

      Step 1Keratolytic creams

      • Urea 5–10% maintenance, 10–25% for peeling; lactic or salicylic acid creams; dimeticone barrier creams.
      • Use licensed UK products per formulary.
    3. 3

      Step 2Treat sweating

      • Aluminium chloride hexahydrate at night; iontophoresis if severe.
    4. 4

      SpecialistSpecialist

      • Acitretin (teratogenic, specialist only) or hand PUVA for refractory cases: limited evidence.

    Doses as in the source notes. Always confirm in the current BNF and local formulary.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Explain first: benign, cosmetic-nuisance condition, recurrent, not contagious and not an allergy; tends to improve with age; steroids won't help (stop them — avoids palmar atrophy and wasted courses).

    Self-care / first-line (OTC/GSL/P):

    • Irritant avoidance: cotton-lined waterproof gloves for wet work; soap substitutes; dry hands thoroughly.
    • Emollient / keratolytic hand creams, applied frequently: urea (e.g. 5–10% for maintenance, 10–25% for peeling — several UK OTC brands), lactic acid or salicylic acid-containing products; silicone/dimeticone barrier creams. US sources cite higher strengths (urea 20–40%, ammonium lactate 12%, salicylic acid 6%) — UK availability is limited, so use licensed UK products/specials per formulary.
    • Treat hyperhidrosis if present (topical aluminium chloride hexahydrate at night; refer for tap-water iontophoresis if severe) — reduces summer flares.

    Specialist options (refer if severe, painful fissuring, or diagnostic doubt): acitretin (teratogenic, specialist-only, Pregnancy Prevention Programme), hand PUVA/photochemotherapy — limited evidence, reserved for refractory cases given skin-cancer and burden trade-offs.

    Step 7 of 9 · 1 min

    Red flags & referral

    • Urgent

      Peeling fingertips in a child with fever ≥ 5 days: think Kawasaki disease.

    • Routine

      Painful fissuring, severe disease or diagnostic doubt: dermatology.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Red flags & referral

    • Peeling fingertips in a child with fever for 5 days or more: think Kawasaki disease (urgent).
    • Peeling after a sore throat and rash: scarlet fever.
    Next: Prescriber pearls
    8

    Step 8 of 9 · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • ‘Peeling, not itchy, not red, worse in summer, steroid-proof’ = think KE.

    • Scrape any one-handed or resistant peeling first.

    • Trim loose edges with clean scissors; don’t pick.

    • Numbness between episodes → assess for neuropathy.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • "Peeling but not itchy, not red, worse in summer, steroid-proof" = think KE before re-issuing steroid.
    • Scrape any one-handed or treatment-resistant peeling to exclude tinea before labelling KE.
    • Avoid picking/peeling the lamellae — trim loose edges with clean scissors to reduce painful cracks.
    • Fingertip numbness that persists between episodes → examine/assess for neuropathy.

    Step 9 of 9 · 1 min

    Quick self-check

    1. Q1Are KE blisters fluid-filled?Reveal

      No: tiny air-filled blisters that burst into collarettes.

    2. Q2Why stop the steroid?Reveal

      KE isn’t inflammatory; steroids don’t help and risk palmar atrophy.

    3. Q3Useful first-line cream?Reveal

      Urea (5–10% maintenance, 10–25% for peeling) or lactic / salicylic acid creams.

    4. Q4Peeling fingertips with fever for 5 days in a child?Reveal

      Kawasaki disease: urgent.
