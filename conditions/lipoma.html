    Lumps, lesions & skin cancer

    Lipoma & the sarcoma lump pathway

    Soft, rubbery, mobile fatty lumps under the skin. Nearly all are benign; the job is to know the features (large, deep, growing, painful) that need an ultrasound or sarcoma referral.

    Module 2 · Week 1 · 1 image

    Also coversfatty lumpangiolipomaDercum’s diseasesoft tissue sarcomalump larger than 5 cm
    Think of it when

    A soft, painless, mobile, slowly growing lump on the trunk, neck or limbs.

    What drives it

    Benign overgrowth of mature fat cells; angiolipomas are tender; Dercum’s disease gives many painful lipomas.

    Backbone of care

    Reassure if small and typical; ultrasound or refer if uncertain; excision only for symptoms or diagnosis.

    Never miss

    Soft-tissue sarcoma: larger than 5 cm, deep to fascia, growing, painful or recurrent.

    01

    What it is

    • Lipoma: a benign tumour of mature fat. The commonest soft-tissue tumour; age 40–60.
    • Angiolipoma: a vascular variant. Small (<2 cm), often multiple and tender, on the forearms and trunk of young adults (often men). Familial in about 5%.
    • Dercum's disease (adiposis dolorosa): a rare chronic condition with multiple painful lipomas or painful fatty tissue, usually in obese women aged 35–50. It is often accompanied by fatigue, depression, sleep and cognitive problems, and joint pain. It is a diagnosis of exclusion.
    • Related syndromes: familial multiple lipomatosis; Madelung disease (benign symmetric lipomatosis, linked to alcohol); PTEN hamartoma (see Other lumps and syndromes); Gardner (see Epidermoid, pilar and dermoid cysts; milia; Gardner syndrome).
    02

    How it’s caused

    Lipomas show clonal rearrangements (often HMGA2 at 12q13–15). The cause of Dercum's pain is unclear; proposed mechanisms include nerve compression, inflammatory and neuroendocrine factors.

    03

    Clinical features

    • Soft, rubbery, mobile, lobulated subcutaneous mass with normal overlying skin. Trunk, shoulders, neck and arms. Painless (except angiolipoma or Dercum).
    • Red flags for sarcoma (refer): size >5 cm, increasing in size, deep to fascia (fixed, doesn't move with the skin), painful, recurrence after excision. Thigh, buttock and retroperitoneal sites are higher risk, as is any lump that is hard or irregular.
    04

    Diagnosis

    Clinical for small, typical superficial lesions. Ultrasound is first-line imaging. MRI for deep or large lesions. Biopsy in a sarcoma centre, never by excision in primary care for a suspicious lump (an "unplanned excision" (the "whoops" procedure) compromises sarcoma outcomes).

    05

    Differentials

    Epidermoid cyst (punctum), liposarcoma and other soft-tissue sarcoma, lymph node, hernia, haematoma, neurofibroma (buttonhole sign), angiolipoma, lipodystrophy injection-site lipohypertrophy (insulin sites, so check injection technique).

    06

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Pharmacist / IP
    • Typical small lipoma: reassure; removal for cosmetic reasons is usually not NHS-funded.
    • NICE NG12 (sarcoma): consider an urgent direct-access ultrasound (performed within 2 weeks) for adults with an unexplained lump that is increasing in size. Refer on the suspected cancer pathway if the ultrasound suggests sarcoma or is uncertain but clinical concern persists. In children/young people, an unexplained lump increasing in size warrants a very urgent ultrasound (within 48 hours).
    • If the IP does not have direct-access imaging rights, the route is via the GP the same week, with the red flags documented.
    • Dercum's disease: a GP/specialist (dermatology, pain clinic) diagnosis. Management is symptomatic: analgesia, weight management, psychological support; specialist options include lidocaine infusions, liposuction and excision of the worst lesions. Screen for and support depression. Chronic pain medicines need careful IP review: avoid escalating opioids, and consider neuropathic agents per NICE NG193 (chronic primary pain) principles and local formulary.
    • Lipohypertrophy from insulin/GLP-1 injections: counsel on rotating sites. It also impairs absorption and glycaemic control.
    07

    Red flags & referral

    Escalate
    • Lump that is increasing in size or larger than 5 cm, deep or painful: urgent direct-access ultrasound (within 2 weeks) or a suspected sarcoma pathway (NICE NG12).
    08

    Prescriber pearls

    • "Is it bigger than a golf ball, growing, deep or painful?" Then it needs a scan.
    • Angiolipoma tenderness is common and benign, but multiple painful lumps plus systemic symptoms should prompt thinking of Dercum.
