    Local anaesthetics & allergy in skin surgery

    Lidocaine maximum doses (BNF: 200 mg plain, 500 mg with adrenaline), how to calculate them, adrenaline cautions, local anaesthetic systemic toxicity, topical anaesthetics and allergy to latex, chlorhexidine or local anaesthetic.

    Also calledlidocaine maximum doselidocaine with adrenalineLASTIntralipidEMLAAmetop+3

    Key facts

    • Planning or checking a local anaesthetic dose, or a patient reporting an ‘allergy’ to local anaesthetic, latex or skin prep.
    • Sodium-channel blockade stops nerve conduction; adrenaline prolongs action and reduces bleeding; overdose affects brain and heart.
    • 1% = 10 mg/ml; BNF maximum 200 mg plain and 500 mg with adrenaline (weight-based 3 and 7 mg/kg as a cross-check); reduce in frailty, liver disease and heart failure; latex-free room and alternative prep for allergy.
    • Local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (perioral tingling, seizures, arrhythmia): stop, call for help, AAGBI/Intralipid protocol; chlorhexidine anaphylaxis.

    Overview

    Amide local anaesthetics (lidocaine, prilocaine, bupivacaine, levobupivacaine, articaine, mepivacaine) and esters (tetracaine/amethocaine, benzocaine, chloroprocaine) block voltage-gated sodium channels in nerves. Adrenaline is added for vasoconstriction: less bleeding, longer action, and a higher maximum dose.

    Allergy: latex, chlorhexidine and local anaesthetic

    • Latex allergy is either type I (IgE-mediated: urticaria, rhinitis, asthma, anaphylaxis) or type IV (allergic contact dermatitis to rubber accelerators: thiurams, carbamates, MBT). Irritant hand dermatitis from gloves is not an allergy.
    • Chlorhexidine is an increasingly recognised cause of perioperative anaphylaxis (MHRA 2012 alert). It is found in skin preps, catheters, lubricants and wipes.

    Aetiology: causes and risk factors

    Allergy: latex, chlorhexidine and local anaesthetic

    Risk factors

    • Healthcare workers.
    • Spina bifida and multiple childhood surgeries.
    • Atopy.
    • Latex–fruit syndrome: cross-reaction with banana, avocado, kiwi, chestnut and others.

    Clinical features

    Lidocaine maximum dose (BNF): the key Case 1 calculation

    Useful conversion: 1% = 10 mg/ml; 2% = 20 mg/ml.

    BNF adult maximum (infiltration)Weight-based convention (common teaching)
    Plain lidocaine200 mg~3 mg/kg (some SmPCs: 4.5 mg/kg, maximum 300 mg)
    Lidocaine with adrenaline500 mg~7 mg/kg
    Children (BNFC)Up to 3 mg/kg (check the BNFC entry and age band)—
    • Use ideal body weight in obesity. Reduce the dose in frail elderly people, in hepatic impairment, in heart failure (reduced clearance), and in highly vascular sites (absorption).
    • Case 1, worked (teaching only): 60 kg × 7 mg/kg = 420 mg with adrenaline. This is below the BNF absolute ceiling of 500 mg. At 2% (20 mg/ml), 420 mg is 21 ml (course teaching rounded to "20 ml"). Plain lidocaine at 3 mg/kg is 180 mg = 9 ml of 2% (and under the 200 mg BNF cap). For a small DF excision, the volume actually used is typically a few millilitres, far below the maximum.
    • Note on 1:80,000 adrenaline: lidocaine 2% with adrenaline 1:80,000 is the dental cartridge formulation. For skin surgery in UK practice, 1% or 2% with 1:200,000 adrenaline is more usual. The maximum-dose principle is the same.

    Local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST): recognise and respond

    • Early (CNS): perioral numbness or tingling, metallic taste, tinnitus, light-headedness, visual disturbance, slurred speech, agitation or drowsiness.
    • Late: muscle twitching → seizures → coma; cardiovascular: bradycardia, arrhythmias, hypotension, cardiac arrest (bupivacaine is most cardiotoxic).
    • Response (Association of Anaesthetists LAST guideline): stop injecting; call for help (999 in community settings); airway, 100% oxygen, ABC; treat seizures (benzodiazepine); CPR if arrested; IV 20% lipid emulsion (Intralipid®) per the guideline, where available. Settings using larger LA volumes should know where it is kept
    • Prevention: calculate the maximum dose before starting, aspirate before injecting, inject slowly, use the lowest effective volume, and keep patients under observation.
    • Distinguish from vasovagal (pallor, sweating, bradycardia, quick recovery when lying flat; the commonest event) and adrenaline effect (palpitations, tremor, anxiety, tachycardia).

    Tetracaine (ester)Topical—Higher allergy potential (PABA)

    True LA allergy is rare. Most "allergies" are vasovagal, adrenaline effects or anxiety. Ester allergy (PABA) does not cross-react with amides. Methylparaben (the course point) is a preservative in multi-dose vials and can cause reactions in paraben-sensitive patients, so use single-dose, preservative-free ampoules. Refer suspected LA allergy for allergy testing before elective surgery.

    Allergy: latex, chlorhexidine and local anaesthetic

    Clinical features

    Type I reactions within minutes of contact: urticaria, angioedema, wheeze, hypotension. Type IV: eczema at the contact site 24–72 h later.

    Diagnosis and investigations

    Allergy: latex, chlorhexidine and local anaesthetic

    History. Specific IgE to latex (k82) and components (Hev b 1, 3, 5, 6.01); skin prick test; patch testing for type IV.

    Differential diagnoses

    Allergy: latex, chlorhexidine and local anaesthetic

    Irritant glove dermatitis; reaction to chlorhexidine, LA (rare), antibiotics, or adhesive dressings (acrylates, colophony); vasovagal syncope.

    Management

    Allergy: latex, chlorhexidine and local anaesthetic

    Latex-allergic patient:

    • First on the list (the course answer) so latex particles in the room are minimised. A latex-free environment: nitrile or neoprene gloves, latex-free tourniquets, BP cuffs, catheters, dressings and drug vial stoppers (check), and a sign on the door.
    • Prepared anaphylaxis kit.
    • Document the allergy clearly (type and reaction) in the record and referral.

    Anaphylaxis (Resuscitation Council UK 2021):

    • IM adrenaline 1 mg/ml (1:1000): adults and children >12 years 500 micrograms (0.5 ml), into the anterolateral thigh. Repeat after 5 minutes if there is no improvement.
    • Lie flat (or sit up if breathless), legs raised; call 999; high-flow oxygen; IV fluids if trained.
    • Antihistamines and steroids are not first-line (RCUK 2021 de-emphasised them for the emergency phase).
    • Refractory anaphylaxis (two IM doses without improvement) needs specialist help urgently.
    • Mast cell tryptase samples (as soon as possible, at 1–2 h and at 24 h or later) and referral to an allergy clinic (NICE CG134).

    Chlorhexidine: for an allergic patient use an alternative prep, e.g. povidone-iodine if not allergic, or alcohol alone.

    Referral

    • 999 / A&E

      Perioral tingling, metallic taste, confusion, seizures or arrhythmia after injection: local anaesthetic systemic toxicity, stop, 999 and lipid emulsion per the AAGBI guideline.

    • Don’t

      Never exceed the BNF maximum: lidocaine 200 mg plain or 500 mg with adrenaline (adult).

    Red flags and when to refer

    • Perioral tingling, metallic taste, confusion, seizures or arrhythmia after injection: local anaesthetic systemic toxicity, stop, 999 and lipid emulsion per the AAGBI guideline.
    • Never exceed the BNF maximum: lidocaine 200 mg plain or 500 mg with adrenaline (adult).

    Prescriber pearls (UK pharmacist IP)

    • Warm, buffered lidocaine (with 8.4% sodium bicarbonate, 1:10) injected slowly through a fine needle reduces pain (practitioner technique; local policy).

    • Tell the patient the numbness lasts 1–3 h (longer with bupivacaine). Avoid hot drinks or biting the lip after facial or oral blocks.

    • Adrenaline auto-injector patients: check they carry two devices in date (MHRA 2017 advice), and that they know latex is hidden in balloons, condoms, rubber gloves and some dental and medical products.

    • Latex-fruit syndrome foods may cause oral allergy or anaphylaxis.
