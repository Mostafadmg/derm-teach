    Melanoma: Breslow, AJCC 8 staging & NICE NG14 treatment

    What happens after referral: excision biopsy, the histopathology report (Breslow, ulceration), AJCC 8th edition staging with current 5-year survival figures, NICE NG14 excision margins, sentinel node biopsy, adjuvant treatment and follow-up.

    Also calledBreslow thicknessAJCC 8stage IIAsentinel lymph node biopsySLNBwide local excision+2
    Related pagesMelanoma: recognition & referralMelanoma immunotherapy & targeted therapy: side-effectsAtypical naevus syndrome & familial melanomaSkin surgery procedures: excision, curettage, cryotherapy & Mohs

    Key facts

    • Melanoma outcome depends mostly on Breslow thickness, ulceration and nodal status (AJCC 8th edition).
    • Excision biopsy with a 2 mm margin first; then wide local excision (1 cm for <1 mm; 1–2 cm for 1–2 mm; 2 cm for >2 mm thickness).
    • Sentinel lymph node biopsy offered from stage IB (pT1b) upwards (NICE NG14).
    • Adjuvant immunotherapy or BRAF/MEK inhibitors for resected stage III (and some IIB/C); systemic therapy for stage IV.
    • Follow-up by stage; teach self-examination of skin and lymph nodes.

    Overview

    Breslow thickness is the depth of invasion in millimetres. It is measured from the top of the granular layer (or the base of an ulcer) to the deepest invasive melanoma cell, and it is the single strongest prognostic factor in localised melanoma. It is a depth in mm, never a lesion diameter in cm.

    Aetiology: causes and risk factors

    Myths to address

    • "Removing the mole means it's all gone": thicker melanomas need wide excision and staging.

    Pathophysiology and pathology

    The deeper a melanoma grows, the more likely it has reached lymphatics and blood vessels.

    1. 1

      Radial growth

      Early melanoma spreads within the epidermis: excellent prognosis.

    2. 2

      Vertical growth

      Invasion into the dermis gives access to lymphatics and vessels.

    3. 3

      Ulceration and mitoses

      Ulceration and high mitotic rate mark aggressive biology.

    4. 4

      Margins

      Wide local excision removes in-transit cells around the scar.

    Clinical features

    Biopsy principles

    • Excision biopsy with a 2 mm clinical margin, full thickness including subcutaneous fat, oriented to allow later WLE/SLNB (along the long axis of limbs).
    • Done by the skin cancer team/specialist. In primary care, refer rather than excise suspected melanoma (NICE IOG).
    • Avoid shave, punch or incisional biopsy except for specialist situations (large facial LM, acral/nail).

    Types to recognise

    • In-transit metastases

      Lumps between scar and nodes. Site: Limb.

    • Nodal disease

      New regional node lump. Site: Groin, axilla.

    • Cutaneous metastases

      Blue-grey nodules. Site: Skin.

    • SLNB tattoo

      Persistent blue dye mark. Site: Node site.

    Diagnosis and investigations

    Histology. Immunohistochemistry: SOX10, Melan-A, HMB-45, PRAME, Ki-67. FISH/NGS for ambiguous spitzoid lesions.

    Case 3 worked example:

    • Breslow 1.4 mm with ulceration = T2b.
    • If N0 (clinically and after SLNB), this is stage IIA, with ~93–94% 5-year MSS. The course "~80%" reflects older AJCC 7-era data, and it is closer to stage IIC today.
    • Mitoses >5/mm² do not alter the stage in AJCC 8 (they are prognostic only).
    • If SLNB is positive → stage III (e.g. T2b N1a = IIIA).
    • Until SLNB is done, the stage is clinical ("cIIA").

    Management

    Know what the letter means, so you can help patients understand it. "Breslow 0.6 mm, no ulceration" = stage IA, an excellent prognosis. Encourage patients to ask their clinical nurse specialist (CNS) keyworker any questions.

    NICE NG14 management

    Wide local excision (WLE) margins (NG14 2022)

    StageClinical peripheral margin (measured from the biopsy scar/lesion edge)
    0 (in situ)At least 0.5 cm (LM: consider imiquimod or radiotherapy if surgery would be disfiguring or unsuitable; specialist)
    I1 cm
    II2 cm (consider 1 cm if a 2 cm margin would cause unacceptable morbidity/disfigurement, e.g. face)
    IIIAs stage II, plus nodal management

    So Case 3 (stage IIA) needs WLE with a 2 cm margin (two-stage surgery, the course answer).

    Sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB)

    • What: lymphoscintigraphy plus blue dye, a persistent blue tattoo) identifies the first draining node(s). These are excised for histology at the time of WLE.
    • NG14 2022: Do not offer SLNB (or imaging) for stage IA; Consider SLNB for Breslow 0.8–1.0 mm with ≥1 of: ulceration, lymphovascular invasion, or mitotic rate ≥2/mm²; Consider (offer to discuss) SLNB for Breslow >1.0 mm; Discuss the pros and cons: SLNB is a staging procedure (it determines eligibility for adjuvant therapy). It does not improve melanoma-specific survival (MSLT-I). Complications include seroma, infection, lymphoedema and nerve injury
    • Completion lymph node dissection after a positive SLNB is no longer routine (MSLT-II, DeCOG-SLT showed no survival benefit). Nodal ultrasound surveillance is used instead.
    • Case 3: consider SLNB (Breslow >1 mm). The course answer was "consider SLNB" .

    Imaging

    • Stage I–IIA: no routine imaging.
    • Stage IIB: consider contrast-enhanced CT (CE-CT) staging.
    • Stage IIC–IV: offer CE-CT (and MRI brain for stage IV, and for III where relevant; children may have whole-body MRI).
    • Measure vitamin D at diagnosis and give advice on correction/supplementation.

    SettingNICE-recommended optionsTA
    Stage IIB/IICPembrolizumab (age ≥12)TA837
    Stage IIB/IICNivolumab: no NICE TA identified in this session (licensed by MHRA; check for updates)—
    Stage III (node-positive, completely resected)Nivolumab, pembrolizumab, dabrafenib + trametinib if BRAF V600—
    Resected stage IVNivolumab—
    Stage IIANone. Adjuvant therapy is not recommended at IIA—

    Unresectable stage III / stage IV

    • BRAF testing for all patients with stage III–IV disease.
    • Immunotherapy (checkpoint inhibitors): Ipilimumab + nivolumab (anti-CTLA-4 + anti-PD-1; TA400): highest response and highest toxicity; Pembrolizumab or nivolumab monotherapy; Nivolumab + relatlimab (anti-LAG-3; NICE-recommended for untreated advanced melanoma in people aged ≥12; check the current TA)
    • Targeted therapy (BRAF V600): dabrafenib + trametinib, encorafenib + binimetinib, vemurafenib + cobimetinib.
    • Talimogene laherparepvec (T-VEC) intralesional oncolytic virus for unresectable injectable stage IIIB–IVM1a without bone/brain/lung/visceral disease.
    • Other: radiotherapy and stereotactic radiosurgery (brain metastases); TIL therapy (lifileucel; licensed in the US; not routine in the UK at the time of writing); trials.
    • In-transit metastases: surgery, T-VEC, isolated limb perfusion/infusion, electrochemotherapy, systemic therapy.

    Follow-up

    • Stage IA: 2–4 follow-up visits in the first year, then discharge with self-examination education.
    • Stage IB–IIB (and IIC without imaging): every 3 months for 3 years, then every 6 months to 5 years.
    • Stage IIC–III: imaging surveillance (CT or whole-body MRI) per NG14 schedules (e.g. 6-monthly for 3 years, then 12-monthly to 5 years) in addition to clinical review.
    • Teach self-examination of the scar, the in-transit route, regional nodes and the whole skin.
    • Psychological support and a CNS keyworker. Fertility and pregnancy advice where relevant.

    Prescriber pearls & counselling

    A new lump near the scar or in the groin/axilla/neck after melanoma means urgent contact with the skin cancer CNS/MDT. Do not wait for a routine appointment.

    Treatment ladder

    1. 1

      Explain

      Breslow = depth, not size. “0.6 mm, no ulceration” = stage IA, excellent prognosis. Link patients with their CNS keyworker.

    2. 2

      WLE margins

      In situ ≥ 0.5 cm; stage I 1 cm; stage II 2 cm (1 cm if 2 cm would disfigure); III as II + nodes.

    3. 3

      SLNB

      Consider if Breslow > 0.8 mm with risk features / > 1 mm; staging, not survival. Completion dissection no longer routine (MSLT-II): nodal ultrasound surveillance.

    4. 4

      Adjuvant (resected)

      IIB / IIC: pembrolizumab (TA837). III: nivolumab, pembrolizumab, or dabrafenib + trametinib (BRAF V600). Resected IV: nivolumab. IIA: none.

    5. 5

      Advanced

      BRAF test all III–IV. Ipilimumab + nivolumab (TA400), PD-1 monotherapy, nivolumab + relatlimab; BRAF / MEK combos; T-VEC; radiotherapy / SRS.

    Caution

    Prescribing limits and pitfalls

    • Follow-up: IA: 2–4 visits in year 1 then discharge. IB–IIB: 3-monthly for 3 years, then 6-monthly to 5 years. IIC–III: plus imaging surveillance. Teach self-examination.

    Referral

    • Urgent

      New lumps in or near the scar, between scar and nodes, or new nodes: recurrence or in-transit disease.

    • Routine

      Counsel with AJCC 8 (Gershenwald 2017) survival figures.

    Red flags and when to refer

    • New lumps in or near the scar, in the limb between the scar and nodes, or new nodes: urgent review for recurrence or in-transit disease.
    • Survival figures change with each AJCC edition: use AJCC 8 (Gershenwald 2017) when counselling.

    Complications and prognosis

    Complications

    • Local recurrence, in-transit and nodal metastases, distant spread (lung, liver, brain).

    Prognosis

    • Five-year survival over 95% for thin melanoma (<1 mm); much lower with nodal or distant disease.

    Prescriber pearls (UK pharmacist IP)

    • Patients often confuse lesion size with thickness. Explain: "Breslow is how deep it went, measured under the microscope."

    Self-check quiz

    4 questions · optional
    1. Q1Breslow 1.4 mm, ulcerated, node-negative: stage?Reveal

      IIA (T2b N0), ~93–94% 5-year MSS.

    2. Q2WLE margin for stage II?Reveal

      2 cm (1 cm if 2 cm would cause unacceptable morbidity).

    3. Q3Adjuvant therapy at stage IIA?Reveal

      None; pembrolizumab is for IIB / IIC.

    4. Q4Does SLNB improve survival?Reveal

      No: it stages (MSLT-I).
