    Melanoma: recognition & referral

    Module 2 · Week 5

    How to spot melanoma: ABCDE, the NICE weighted 7-point checklist, the ugly-duckling sign and EFG for nodular melanoma, the clinical subtypes (including acral and amelanotic), dermoscopy and AI, and fast referral.

    Coversmalignant melanomaABCDE7-point checklistugly ducklingEFGnodular melanomalentigo malignaacral melanomaamelanotic melanomasuperficial spreading melanoma
    Think of it when

    A new or changing pigmented lesion, an ugly duckling, a firm growing nodule (pink or dark), a dark streak on the sole or nail, or a slowly spreading facial patch.

    What drives it

    UV-driven (or, on acral skin, non-UV) mutations in melanocytes (BRAF, NRAS, NF1, KIT) with inherited risk (CDKN2A, MC1R).

    Backbone of care

    NICE NG12: weighted 7-point checklist score of 3 or more, or dermoscopy suggesting melanoma = suspected cancer pathway; do not excise or biopsy in primary care; teledermatology with good images.

    Never miss

    Nodular and amelanotic melanoma (EFG: elevated, firm, growing; may not fit ABCD); acral melanoma in skin of colour; repeated reassurance of a changing lesion.

    What it is

    Melanoma is a malignant tumour of melanocytes. In the UK it is the 5th commonest cancer (about 17,500–20,000 new cases a year), with rising incidence, especially in people over 65. It is one of the commonest cancers in young adults (15–34).

    Clinical subtypes

    Subtypes describe the growth pattern. Breslow thickness, not subtype, drives stage and prognosis.

    2

    How it’s caused

    • Intermittent intense UV and sunburn (especially in childhood), sunbeds, fair skin (MC1R), many or atypical naevi, family history, previous melanoma, and immunosuppression.
    • Driver mutations: BRAF V600 in ~40–50% (intermittently sun-exposed skin). This is the target for BRAF/MEK inhibitors; NRAS ~20%; NF1 (chronically sun-damaged skin, desmoplastic); KIT in acral/mucosal/CSD melanomas (the course c-KIT)
    • Growth phases: radial (in situ / thin), then vertical (nodular, invasive). Breslow thickness tracks the vertical phase.

    Clinical features

    Clinical features: the recognition tools

    Case 1 shows why EFG matters: a nodule growing for 12 months scores change in size (2) + possibly diameter ≥7 mm (1), so the threshold is met, and nodular melanoma needs urgent referral even when it is symmetrical or uniformly coloured.

    Diagnosis

    • Diagnostic accuracy by eye rises with experience. The course figures (consultant ~80%, registrar 50–60%, GP ~40%) are in line with older studies, but published estimates vary widely.
    • Dermoscopy improves accuracy in trained users: meta-analyses show sensitivity rising from about 70% to about 90%, with an odds ratio of ~15 for diagnostic accuracy versus the naked eye. Untrained use may not help.
    • Dermoscopic melanoma features: atypical network; irregular streaks / pseudopods; irregular dots and globules; blue-white veil; regression structures; negative network; shiny white lines; atypical (polymorphous/dotted) vessels; acral parallel-ridge pattern; facial LM: asymmetric follicular openings, rhomboid structures, annular-granular pattern; Algorithms: 3-point checklist (asymmetry, atypical network, blue-white structures; ≥2 = refer), 7-point dermoscopic checklist, ABCD rule (Stolz), CASH, TADA, "chaos and clues"
    • Training and simulators: PCDS dermoscopy courses, the BAD/BDNG, International Dermoscopy Society (dermoscopy-ids.org) online tutorials, DermNet's dermoscopy course, and image-based simulators (e.g. "Dermoscopedia", quiz banks). The evidence supports structured training with regular practice and feedback.
    • AI and algorithms: NICE HTG746 (formerly HTE24) supports conditional NHS use of DERM (Skin Analytics) to triage lesions already referred on the urgent skin cancer pathway, with dermatologist oversight. Images of black or brown skin (Fitzpatrick V–VI) still need human review. Consumer apps are mostly unregulated. An app result never overrides clinical concern in either direction.
    5

    Differentials

    SK (the benign lesion notes see Biopsy, Breslow thickness and the histopathology report; ), atypical naevus, pigmented BCC, DF, angiokeratoma (the skin lumps notes see Immunotherapy and targeted therapy: what the IP must know), haemangioma/thrombosed angioma, pyogenic granuloma (amelanotic nodular melanoma mimic; ), subungual haematoma (nail), acral naevus, lentigo/flat SK (vs LM), and metastasis.

    Clinical subtypes

    –2.5 Diagnosis and differentials

    As in Recognition, diagnostic accuracy, dermoscopy and AI. LM vs solar lentigo/flat SK (dermoscopy, biopsy). ALM vs acral naevus, haematoma (talon noir, "black heel"), wart, and fungal (tinea nigra).

    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    • Suspected cancer pathway referral (2WW) if: the 7-point score is ≥3; dermoscopy suggests melanoma; or; a nodule fits EFG (nodular melanoma)
    • Make the referral within 1 working day of deciding. Include dermoscopic/clinical images if a teledermatology triage route exists (see Teledermatology).
    • Document size (ruler), duration, change, the 7-point score, and safety-netting.
    • Do not: freeze, shave, laser or "watch and wait" on a lesion meeting criteria; or reassure because an app is negative.
    • Previous repeated reassurance is a classic diagnostic-delay pattern. When a patient re-presents with persistent concern, take it seriously, and consider reporting it as a learning event.

    Clinical subtypes

    • Check soles, palms and nails in every skin of colour presentation, and do not label a pigmented sole lesion as a "bruise" or "verruca" without follow-up. Subungual: Hutchinson's sign → suspected cancer pathway (the nail disorders notes).
    • LM on the face of an older person: an elderly red flag (the older skin pages notes quick reference), so use the suspected cancer pathway.

    Worked cases

    Case 1: 54-year-old, nodule >12 months, repeatedly reassured; an app flagged it

    • Diagnosis: nodular melanoma (EFG: elevated, firm, growing).
    • Action: NG12 suspected cancer pathway (1.7.1 score ≥3 and/or 1.7.3 nodular melanoma), referral within 1 working day.
    • Excision biopsy with a 2 mm margin (the course "narrow margin") by the skin cancer team, then WLE per Breslow.
    • Diagnostic accuracy: consultants ~80%, registrars 50–60%, GPs ~40% (course teaching; study-dependent); dermoscopy adds about 10–20% in trained hands.
    • Learning points: repeated reassurance = diagnostic delay; an app is not a diagnostic tool, but a flag deserves clinical review.

    Case 2: 17-year-old of Irish ancestry, >50 atypical moles, father and grandfather with melanoma in their 30s, other family cancers

    • Diagnosis: FAMMM (familial atypical multiple mole melanoma) syndrome.
    • Gene: CDKN2A (p16). Also consider CDK4, BAP1 and MC1R modifiers.
    • Associated cancer: pancreatic adenocarcinoma.
    • Plan: dermatology surveillance (total-body photography plus digital dermoscopy), clinical genetics, sun protection, no sunbeds (under-18 law), self-examination, no smoking.

    Case 3: 30-year-old, lesion changing over 2 years, teledermatology images

    • Pathway: 2WW with images → excision biopsy (2 mm) → WLE.
    • Pathology: Breslow 1.4 mm (depth, not 1.5 cm diameter), ulcerated → T2b.
    • Stage: IIA if node-negative; SLNB decides (if positive → IIIA or higher).
    • 5-year MSS: ~93–94% (AJCC 8), not ~80%. Mitoses >5/mm² are prognostic only, not staging.
    • WLE: 2 cm margin (stage II, NG14).
    • SLNB: consider/discuss (Breslow >1 mm).
    • Imaging: not required at IIA (consider at IIB).
    • Adjuvant: none at IIA (pembrolizumab TA837 is for IIB/IIC).
    • Follow-up: 3-monthly for 3 years, then 6-monthly to 5 years. Self-examination; vitamin D measured at diagnosis.
    7

    Red flags & referral

    • NICE weighted 7-point checklist score 3 or more, or dermoscopy suggesting melanoma: suspected cancer pathway referral (NG12 1.7.1–1.7.3).
    • A firm, growing nodule over a month (EFG), even if pink or symmetrical: suspected nodular melanoma.
    • A new dark mark on the sole, palm or nail that is growing: suspected acral melanoma (check the nail fold for Hutchinson’s sign).
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • "Any spot that is raised, firm and growing, of any colour, needs to be seen within two weeks."
    • Sun-protection counselling and full-skin self-checks for family members (first-degree relatives have about twice the risk).

    Clinical subtypes

    • A "verruca" that doesn't respond to salicylic acid or cryo and is pigmented or bleeding needs referral, not more destructive treatment.
