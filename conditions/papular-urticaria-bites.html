    Papular urticaria & insect bites

    Recurrent itchy grouped papules from flea, mite or bed-bug hypersensitivity. Treat the source (pets, home, pest control) as well as the itch.

    Also calledpapular urticariainsect bite hypersensitivityflea bitesbed bugsCimexbreakfast lunch dinner
    Related pagesScabiesUrticaria & angioedemaImpetigoLyme disease & erythema migransCutaneous larva migrans

    Key facts

    • Papular urticaria is a persistent itchy hypersensitivity reaction to insect bites (fleas, bed bugs, mites, mosquitoes).
    • Grouped or linear ('breakfast, lunch and dinner') papules, sometimes with central punctum or blisters, on exposed skin or ankles.
    • Commonest in children aged 2–10; other household members may be unaffected.
    • Eradicate the source (treat all pets, professional pest control for bed bugs); antihistamine and mild–moderate topical steroid.
    • Most bites do not need antibiotics; treat only clear secondary infection.

    Overview

    Papular urticaria is a chronic or recurrent eruption of intensely itchy grouped papules (± vesicles). It is a hypersensitivity reaction to arthropod bites — most often fleas, mites, bed bugs, mosquitoes and gnats. Despite the name it is not true urticaria (lesions last days, not hours). It is commonest in children aged about 2–10.

    Epidemiology

    • Who
      • Children, newcomers to a household, travellers; tolerance develops with ongoing exposure.

    Aetiology: causes and risk factors

    • Arthropod saliva triggers type I and type IV hypersensitivity. Bites are often unnoticed; reactions arrive hours to days later.
    • Cat fleas are the commonest fleas biting UK humans (even in dog-only homes). The home holds most of the eggs/larvae/pupae.
    • Bed bugs hide in mattress seams and feed at night; linked to travel and second-hand furniture.
    • Most children grow out of it as they desensitise.

    Risk factors and associations

    • Type I and IV hypersensitivity to insect saliva antigens.

    Myths to address

    • "Only one child is affected, so it can't be bites": sensitivity varies between people.

    Pathophysiology and pathology

    The rash is an allergic reaction to insect saliva, not the bite itself.

    1. 1

      Bite

      The insect injects saliva containing anticoagulant and enzyme proteins.

    2. 2

      Immediate reaction

      IgE on mast cells triggers a wheal within minutes.

    3. 3

      Delayed reaction

      T cells respond over 24–48 hours, producing a firm itchy papule that lasts days to weeks.

    4. 4

      Desensitisation

      With repeated exposure, reactions fade (tolerance).

    Histopathology

    • Wedge-shaped perivascular infiltrate with eosinophils; rarely needed.

    Clinical features

    • Firm red itchy papules 3–10 mm, sometimes with a punctum, vesicle or blister; excoriation and later pigmentation are common.
    • Grouping in clusters or lines of three (“breakfast, lunch and dinner”).
    • Seasonal spring–autumn for fleas/outdoor insects; bed bugs all year.
    SourceClues
    FleasLower legs/ankles, waistband; pets; often only one person reacts; new house can awaken pupae
    Bed bugsFace, neck, arms (exposed in bed); linear clusters; blood/faecal spots on sheets; travel
    Mosquitoes/midgesExposed outdoor sites; seasonal

    Types to recognise

    • Papular urticaria

      Grouped firm itchy papules. Site: Legs, arms.

    • Flea bites

      Clusters at the ankles. Site: Ankles.

    • Bed bug bites

      Linear clusters on sleep-exposed skin. Site: Arms, neck.

    • Bullous reaction

      Tense blisters on bites. Site: Lower legs.

    Special groups

    • Bed bugs
      • Linear bites on the face, neck and arms; blood spots on sheets; professional eradication needed.

    Diagnosis and investigations

    Clinical from morphology, grouping and history (pets, new house, travel, season). Find the source: flea-comb pets, inspect mattress seams. Investigations are rarely needed. Explain that only the sensitised person reacts.

    History

    • Pets, new house, travel, season, who else itches.

    Find the source

    • Flea-comb pets; inspect mattress seams.

    Tests

    • Rarely needed.

    Differential diagnoses

    Management

    1. Eradicate the source

    • Fleas: veterinary flea products for all pets; hot-wash bedding; vacuum thoroughly; household IGR spray for weeks.
    • Bed bugs: council pest control or BPCA/NPTA professional firm — home sprays rarely suffice.
    • Outdoor: clothing and DEET-type repellent.

    2. Symptom control

    • Cool compresses, short nails, soap and water.
    • Non-sedating antihistamine by day; short sedating antihistamine at night if sleep is poor (check age licence).
    • Short mild-to-moderate topical corticosteroid on new itchy spots (potent short courses only on thick adult lesions).

    3. Secondary infection

    • Most bites do not need antibiotics — redness and itch can last ~10 days.
    • If spreading cellulitis/impetigo: flucloxacillin first-line (NICE NG182); Pharmacy First pathway for infected insect bites (England, age ≥1) within PGD rules.

    Treatment ladder

    1. 1

      Eradicate the source

      Fleas: vet flea products for all pets; hot-wash bedding; vacuum; household IGR spray for weeks. Bed bugs: council or professional (BPCA / NPTA) pest control.

    2. 2

      Symptom control

      Cool compresses, short nails. Non-sedating antihistamine by day; short sedating antihistamine at night (check age licence).

    3. 3

      Secondary infection only

      Spreading cellulitis / impetigo: flucloxacillin first-line (NICE NG182); Pharmacy First infected insect bite pathway (England, ≥ 1 year) within PGD.

    Caution

    Prescribing limits and pitfalls

    • No routine antibiotics: Redness and itch can last ~10 days without infection.
    • Long-term potent steroids: Avoid.

    Referral

    • 999 / A&E

      Anaphylaxis or airway compromise.

    • Same day

      Systemically unwell, spreading cellulitis, bites in mouth / throat or around eyes.

    • Urgent

      Fever or a persisting unusual lesion after bites abroad.

    • Routine

      Atypical persistent eruption or suspected delusional infestation: GP / dermatology.

    Red flags and when to refer

    • Anaphylaxis or ABC compromise → emergency.
    • Systemically unwell, spreading cellulitis, immunocompromised with infection, bites in mouth/throat or around eyes.
    • Bites abroad with fever or persistent exotic lesion.
    • Atypical persistent eruption or suspected delusional infestation → GP/dermatology.

    Complications and prognosis

    Complications

    • Impetigo, scarring, post-inflammatory pigmentation, sleep disturbance.

    Prognosis

    • Settles when the source is removed; children grow out of it.

    Prescriber pearls (UK pharmacist IP)

    • The pet may look fine — the house is the reservoir.

    • Antihistamines and steroids control symptoms; recurrences stop only when the source is gone.

    • Bed bug vs flea: sleep-exposed sites and linen spots vs ankles/pets.

    • Avoid long-term potent steroids and antibiotics without infection.

    Self-check quiz

    4 questions · optional
    1. Q1Why does only one family member get spots?Reveal

      Only sensitised people react to the bites.

    2. Q2Best treatment for flea bites that keep recurring?Reveal

      Treat all pets with vet products + vacuum + household spray.

    3. Q3When are antibiotics needed?Reveal

      Only for genuine secondary infection (flucloxacillin, NG182).

    4. Q4Lines of three bites on arms after a hotel stay?Reveal

      Bed bugs.
