    Papular urticaria & insect bites

    Module 5 · Week 3

    Recurrent itchy grouped papules from flea, mite or bed-bug hypersensitivity. Treat the source (pets, home, pest control) as well as the itch.

    Coverspapular urticariainsect bite hypersensitivityflea bitesbed bugsCimexbreakfast lunch dinner
    Think of it when

    Crops of very itchy firm papules in clusters or lines, often on a child’s legs or on exposed sleep sites — only one household member may react.

    What drives it

    Immediate and delayed hypersensitivity to arthropod saliva; new bites can flare old lesions. Children usually desensitise over time.

    Backbone of care

    Eradicate the source (vet flea products + vacuum / professional bed-bug control) plus short topical steroid and antihistamine.

    Never miss

    Anaphylaxis pathways for systemic allergy; fever after bites abroad; do not antibiotic-treat ordinary itchy redness lasting up to ~10 days.

    What it is

    Papular urticaria is a chronic or recurrent eruption of intensely itchy grouped papules (± vesicles). It is a hypersensitivity reaction to arthropod bites — most often fleas, mites, bed bugs, mosquitoes and gnats. Despite the name it is not true urticaria (lesions last days, not hours). It is commonest in children aged about 2–10.

    2

    How it’s caused

    • Arthropod saliva triggers type I and type IV hypersensitivity. Bites are often unnoticed; reactions arrive hours to days later.
    • Cat fleas are the commonest fleas biting UK humans (even in dog-only homes). The home holds most of the eggs/larvae/pupae.
    • Bed bugs hide in mattress seams and feed at night; linked to travel and second-hand furniture.
    • Most children grow out of it as they desensitise.
    3

    Clinical features

    • Firm red itchy papules 3–10 mm, sometimes with a punctum, vesicle or blister; excoriation and later pigmentation are common.
    • Grouping in clusters or lines of three (“breakfast, lunch and dinner”).
    • Seasonal spring–autumn for fleas/outdoor insects; bed bugs all year.
    SourceClues
    FleasLower legs/ankles, waistband; pets; often only one person reacts; new house can awaken pupae
    Bed bugsFace, neck, arms (exposed in bed); linear clusters; blood/faecal spots on sheets; travel
    Mosquitoes/midgesExposed outdoor sites; seasonal
    4

    Diagnosis

    Clinical from morphology, grouping and history (pets, new house, travel, season). Find the source: flea-comb pets, inspect mattress seams. Investigations are rarely needed. Explain that only the sensitised person reacts.

    5

    Differentials

    Look-alikeDistinguishing clues
    ScabiesWeb spaces, wrists, genitals, burrows; whole household itchy
    Acute urticariaWheals fade within 24 h; no crusted papules
    ChickenpoxFever; crops at different stages including scalp
    MolluscumPearly umbilicated; minimally itchy
    Delusional infestationConviction of infestation without confirming insects or lesions
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    1. Eradicate the source

    • Fleas: veterinary flea products for all pets; hot-wash bedding; vacuum thoroughly; household IGR spray for weeks.
    • Bed bugs: council pest control or BPCA/NPTA professional firm — home sprays rarely suffice.
    • Outdoor: clothing and DEET-type repellent.

    2. Symptom control

    • Cool compresses, short nails, soap and water.
    • Non-sedating antihistamine by day; short sedating antihistamine at night if sleep is poor (check age licence).
    • Short mild-to-moderate topical corticosteroid on new itchy spots (potent short courses only on thick adult lesions).

    3. Secondary infection

    • Most bites do not need antibiotics — redness and itch can last ~10 days.
    • If spreading cellulitis/impetigo: flucloxacillin first-line (NICE NG182); Pharmacy First pathway for infected insect bites (England, age ≥1) within PGD rules.
    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Anaphylaxis or ABC compromise → emergency.
    • Systemically unwell, spreading cellulitis, immunocompromised with infection, bites in mouth/throat or around eyes.
    • Bites abroad with fever or persistent exotic lesion.
    • Atypical persistent eruption or suspected delusional infestation → GP/dermatology.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • The pet may look fine — the house is the reservoir.
    • Antihistamines and steroids control symptoms; recurrences stop only when the source is gone.
    • Bed bug vs flea: sleep-exposed sites and linen spots vs ankles/pets.
    • Avoid long-term potent steroids and antibiotics without infection.
