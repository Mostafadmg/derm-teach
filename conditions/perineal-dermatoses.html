    Perineal dermatoses

    Anogenital warts and HPV counselling, groin hidradenitis first-line care (bridge to Week 5 HS), and brief DDx: molluscum, PPP, Fordyce, candida and lichen sclerosus.

    Module 3 · Week 4 · 20 images

    Also coversanogenital wartscondylomagenital wartsHPVhidradenitis groinpearly penile papulesFordyce spotsmolluscum genitallichen sclerosus
    Think of it when

    Flesh-coloured coalescing genital papules after sexual contact — or a recurrent painful groin nodule with scarring.

    What drives it

    Low-risk HPV 6/11 in basal keratinocytes (warts); follicular occlusion and autoinflammation in HS.

    Backbone of care

    Warts: expectant care or podophyllotoxin/imiquimod/cryotherapy via SHS pathways + HPV vaccine catch-up. Groin HS: smoking/weight advice, antiseptic wash, topical clindamycin ± doxycycline; refer Hurley II+.

    Never miss

    Do not call every genital papule a wart (PPP/Fordyce are normal). Chronic white genital itch/scarring → lichen sclerosus pathway. Children with anogenital warts need specialist safeguarding consideration.

    01

    What it is

    Module 3 Week 4 focuses on perineal papules and folds: full coverage of anogenital warts, a groin hidradenitis bridge to the Week 5 HS deep-dive, and a short differential set (molluscum, pearly penile papules, Fordyce spots, candidal intertrigo, lichen sclerosus).

    Cutaneous viral warts are separate: viral warts. Candidal folds also deepen on candida / intertrigo.

    02

    How it’s caused

    • Anogenital warts: sexually acquired low-risk HPV 6/11 infecting basal keratinocytes through microabrasions; incubation weeks to months.
    • HS: follicular occlusion → tunnels and scarring in apocrine folds; smoking and obesity are major drivers — not contagious infection.
    • Molluscum: poxvirus; genital adult disease may be STI-context.
    • PPP and Fordyce: normal anatomical variants (angiofibromas; ectopic sebaceous glands).
    03

    Clinical features

    Anogenital warts

    • Flesh-coloured, pink, grey or brown verrucous papules; may coalesce into cauliflower plaques on vulva, penis, scrotum, perianal skin.
    • Often asymptomatic; itch, bleeding or psychosexual distress common.

    Groin hidradenitis (bridge)

    • Recurrent painful nodules/abscesses, double-ended comedones, sinus tracts and rope-like scars in the inguinal folds.
    • Hurley I: abscesses without tracts; II: separated tracts/scarring; III: diffuse interconnected tracts — deepen staging/escalation in Module 3 Week 5 when the dedicated HS page is published.

    Brief DDx strip

    EntityClue
    MolluscumSmooth pearly papules with central umbilication
    Pearly penile papulesUniform rows on the corona — normal
    Fordyce spotsTiny yellow-white sebaceous papules — normal
    CandidaBright fold erythema + satellite pustules
    Lichen sclerosusWhite atrophic plaques, itch, scarring — separate pathway
    04

    Diagnosis

    • Warts: usually clinical; biopsy if atypical/ulcerated/resistant. Offer STI screen and partner discussion via local sexual-health services — routine HPV typing is not needed in primary care.
    • HS: clinical (typical lesions + sites + chronicity). Swabs often colonisers — do not withhold the diagnosis for a negative culture.
    • Children with anogenital warts: specialist paediatric/SHS + safeguarding pathways.

    06

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    Pharmacist / IP

    Anogenital warts (NICE CKS themes)

    • ~30% may regress in months — no treatment is valid if acceptable to the patient.
    • Patient-applied (external): podophyllotoxin or imiquimod per SmPC — avoid podophyllotoxin in pregnancy; teach exact application.
    • Clinic: cryotherapy, TCA (often preferred in pregnancy if treatment needed), excision/electrosurgery for selected disease via SHS.
    • Recurrence is common because latent HPV persists — set expectations.
    • HPV vaccine catch-up (Gardasil 9 programme themes): discuss if eligible (routine Year 8; catch-up to 25th birthday for many; GBMSM and immunosuppressed schedules differ) — vaccine does not clear existing warts but may protect against other types.
    • Pregnancy: prefer expectant care or clinic methods; many home topicals contraindicated.

    Groin HS — first-line IP actions

    • Smoking cessation; weight management; loose clothing; absorbent dressings; reassure not contagious.
    • Antiseptic wash (e.g. chlorhexidine) + topical clindamycin to active areas.
    • Oral doxycycline (or other tetracycline) as a prolonged anti-inflammatory course if needed — not a short “boil antibiotic”.
    • Hurley II–III or refractory disease → dermatology (Week 5 covers combination antibiotics, hormonals, biologics, surgery).

    DDx management snippets

    • Molluscum: often expectant; treat for cosmesis/spread via local practice; offer SHS screen if genital adult.
    • PPP/Fordyce: reassure — procedures only for significant distress.
    • Candida: topical azole ± brief hydrocortisone; keep dry; see candidal intertrigo page.
    • Lichen sclerosus: do not manage as “thrush forever” — potent steroid pathways and referral for chronic white genital disease.
    07

    Red flags & referral

    Escalate
    • Ulcerated/indurated “wart” → urgent specialist.
    • Hurley II–III HS or non-healing ulcers in long-standing sinuses (SCC risk, especially older men).
    • Anogenital warts in children → safeguarding-aware specialist referral.
    • LS with architectural change or any suspicion of neoplasia.
    08

    Prescriber pearls

    • Condoms reduce but do not abolish HPV transmission (skin outside the condom).
    • Incision and drainage alone almost always fails in HS — treat the disease, not only the abscess.
    • Sexual-health interface: STI screen offer, partner pathways, vaccine catch-up.
    • Always verify current Green Book / NHS HPV schedule and BNF/SmPC before advising.
    • Hidradenitis suppurativa beyond the groin, with Hurley staging and the treatment ladder: see hidradenitis suppurativa.
