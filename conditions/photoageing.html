    Photoageing

    UV-driven dermatoheliosis: solar elastosis, Favre–Racouchot, poikiloderma of Civatte, IGH and related patterns — prevention first.

    Also calledphotoageingdermatoheliosissolar elastosisFavre-Racouchotcutis rhomboidalis nuchaepoikiloderma of Civatte+2
    Related pagesSolar lentiginesActinic keratoses (solar keratoses)Skin fragility & dermatoporosisRetinoids (topical & oral)Facial ageing & neuromodulators

    Key facts

    • Photoageing is premature skin ageing from chronic UV (and visible/infrared) exposure, on top of intrinsic ageing.
    • Wrinkles, coarse texture, mottled pigment, solar lentigines, telangiectasia, yellowish thickening (solar elastosis).
    • Marks cumulative sun damage and higher risk of AK, BCC, SCC and melanoma: examine the skin.
    • Prevention: sun protection and stopping smoking; topical retinoids have the best evidence for treatment.
    • Procedures (peels, lasers, injectables) are cosmetic and not NHS-funded.

    Overview

    Photoageing (dermatoheliosis) is the cumulative UV contribution to ageing skin, distinct from intrinsic chronological ageing. Patterns include solar elastosis, cutis rhomboidalis nuchae, Favre–Racouchot (nodular elastosis with cysts and comedones), poikiloderma of Civatte, idiopathic guttate hypomelanosis (IGH) and senile comedones.

    Epidemiology

    • Who
      • Fair skin, outdoor workers, sunbed users, sunny climates; smoking adds damage.

    Aetiology: causes and risk factors

    • UVA/UVB → matrix metalloproteinases, DNA damage, solar elastosis.
    • Smoking and pollution amplify extrinsic ageing.
    • Favre–Racouchot: severe elastosis + follicular plugging on the malar/periorbital skin, mainly in older men with heavy sun/smoking history.

    Risk factors and associations

    • UVA penetrates the dermis (collagen and elastin damage); UVB damages the epidermis and DNA.
    • Smoking, pollution, infrared radiation.

    Myths to address

    • "A tan protects the skin": a tan is a sign of DNA damage.
    • "Anti-ageing creams reverse wrinkles": only retinoids and sun protection have good evidence.

    Pathophysiology and pathology

    UV turns on collagen-destroying enzymes while slowing new collagen formation.

    1. 1

      Free radicals

      UV generates reactive oxygen species in skin cells.

    2. 2

      Matrix breakdown

      Signalling (AP-1) switches on matrix metalloproteinases that digest collagen.

    3. 3

      Reduced repair

      New collagen synthesis falls; fragmented collagen weakens the dermis: wrinkles.

    4. 4

      Solar elastosis

      Abnormal elastic material builds up in the upper dermis, causing yellow, thickened skin.

    5. 5

      Pigment and vessels

      Uneven melanocyte activity (lentigines) and dilated vessels (telangiectasia) appear.

    Histopathology

    • Solar elastosis (blue-grey amorphous elastic material in the papillary dermis), reduced collagen, atrophic or uneven epidermis.

    Clinical features

    • Atrophy or thickened leathery skin, deep wrinkles, blotchy pigment, telangiectasia.
    • Cutis rhomboidalis nuchae: deep crossed furrows on the nape.
    • Poikiloderma of Civatte: red-brown reticulate pigment and atrophy on the sides of the neck, sparing shaded under-chin.
    • IGH: small porcelain-white macules on sun-exposed limbs.
    • Senile comedones / Favre–Racouchot: open blackheads in elastotic cheek skin.

    Special groups

    • Skin of colour
      • Photoageing appears later and is mainly pigmentary (uneven tone, dermatosis papulosa nigra) rather than wrinkles.

    Diagnosis and investigations

    Clinical. Use photoageing as a prompt to examine for skin cancer and AK field change. Biopsy atypical pigmented or ulcerated lesions.

    Management

    • Prevention: daily SPF 30–50, shade, clothing, no sunbeds; stop smoking.
    • Emollients; treat xerosis as on the itch/xerosis page.
    • Topical retinoids (POM) can modestly improve texture — irritation and photosensitivity counselling; avoid in pregnancy.
    • Extraction/retinoids for Favre–Racouchot comedones; procedures (laser, peels, IPL) are usually private — counselling only here.
    • Evidence for oral collagen/HA supplements is limited — do not oversell; prioritise sunscreen and lifestyle.
    • Cross-link cosmetic counselling: facial ageing & neuromodulators, solar lentigines.

    Referral

    • Urgent

      Any changing, bleeding or irregular lesion in a photoaged field → urgent skin-cancer pathway.

    • Routine

      Unilateral or atypical “poikiloderma” — reconsider contact, connective tissue disease or mycosis fungoides (rare).

    Red flags and when to refer

    • Any changing, bleeding or irregular lesion in a photoaged field → urgent skin-cancer pathway.
    • Unilateral or atypical “poikiloderma” — reconsider contact, connective tissue disease or mycosis fungoides (rare).

    Complications and prognosis

    Complications

    • Actinic keratoses and skin cancers; skin fragility, purpura and skin tears.

    Prognosis

    • Partly preventable and partly reversible with photoprotection and retinoids.

    Prescriber pearls (UK pharmacist IP)

    • Photoageing is a risk marker, not only a cosmetic complaint.

    • Under-chin sparing is a useful Civatte clue.

    • Supplements are not a substitute for SPF.

    • Sun-damaged skin is where keratinocyte cancers arise: see basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma.

    Self-check quiz

    5 questions · optional
    1. Q1When should you think of Photoageing?Reveal

      Leathery, wrinkled, unevenly pigmented sun-exposed skin with open comedones on the cheeks or rhomboid nuchal furrows.

    2. Q2What drives it?Reveal

      Chronic UV degrades collagen/elastin (solar elastosis) and dysregulates pigment and follicular keratinisation.

    3. Q3What is the backbone of care?Reveal

      Lifelong photoprotection, smoking cessation, emollients; retinoids/procedures are adjunctive and usually private.

    4. Q4What must you never miss?Reveal

      Photoaged fields hide AK, BCC, SCC and lentigo maligna — examine the whole field, not just the wrinkle.

    5. Q5Name a red flag that needs escalation.Reveal

      Any changing, bleeding or irregular lesion in a photoaged field → urgent skin-cancer pathway.
