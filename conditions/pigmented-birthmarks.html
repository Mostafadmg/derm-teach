    Pigmented & epidermal birthmarks

    Module 6 · Week 1

    Naevus of Ota, achromic and anaemic naevi, Becker naevus, Mongolian spots and linear epidermal naevi. Mostly benign and needing reassurance, with key exceptions: the eye in Ota, tuberous sclerosis, and documenting Mongolian spots for safeguarding.

    Coversnaevus of Otanevus of Otaachromic naevusnaevus depigmentosusnaevus anaemicusnevus anemicusBecker naevusMongolian spotdermal melanocytosisepidermal naevuslinear verrucous epidermal naevusILVENbirthmark
    Think of it when

    A congenital or early-childhood patch: blue-grey (dermal melanocytosis), pale (achromic or anaemic), brown and hairy (Becker), or linear and warty (epidermal naevus).

    What drives it

    Developmental pigment or mosaic abnormalities: dermal melanocytes, reduced melanin, vasoconstriction, or keratinocyte mosaicism.

    Backbone of care

    Reassure, offer camouflage, document carefully, and refer selectively for specialist laser or surgery.

    Never miss

    Ocular Ota (glaucoma), multiple ash-leaf macules (tuberous sclerosis), extensive or syndromic epidermal naevus, and misreading a Mongolian spot as a bruise.

    What it is

    BirthmarkColour / lookTypical siteKey point
    Naevus of OtaBlue-grey or brown-violet, mottledForehead, temple, around the eye (V1/V2) ± scleraCheck the eye: ophthalmology (glaucoma)
    Achromic naevus (naevus depigmentosus)Off-white, stable, serrated edgeTrunk; may be segmentalNot vitiligo; consider ash-leaf macules
    Naevus anaemicusPale; does not redden when rubbedTrunkVasoconstriction, not melanin loss; NF1 association
    Becker naevusLarge brown patch, hairy after pubertyShoulder or upper trunk; men about 5:1Rare Becker naevus syndrome (breast or pectoral hypoplasia)
    Mongolian spotFlat blue-greyLower back and buttocksDocument to avoid confusion with bruising; most fade by about age 4
    Linear verrucous epidermal naevusLinear, warty, along Blaschko linesTrunk or limbs, usually one sideRefer if extensive or syndromic
    2

    How it’s caused

    • Dermal melanocytoses (Ota, Mongolian spot): melanocytes arrested in the dermis during embryonic migration (GNAQ/GNA11 mutations described).
    • Achromic naevus: a mosaic developmental pigment defect (melanocytes present, but melanin production or transfer reduced). It is not autoimmune.
    • Naevus anaemicus: localised α-adrenergic hypersensitivity of dermal vessels.
    • Becker naevus: a hamartoma of epidermis, melanocytes and hair follicles, triggered by androgens around puberty.
    • Epidermal naevus: postzygotic keratinocyte mosaicism (FGFR3, PIK3CA, HRAS).
    3

    Clinical features

    See the table under ‘What it is’. Extra points:

    • Naevus of Ota: present at birth in about half; more common in East Asian women. Rarely, melanoma develops within dermal melanocytosis.
    • Naevus anaemicus: diascopy (pressing the surrounding skin) makes the contrast disappear.
    • Epidermal naevus: starts flat and tan, then becomes warty, and may extend through childhood. ILVEN is an itchy inflammatory variant.
    4

    Diagnosis

    Clinical, with Wood’s lamp and dermoscopy where helpful. Achromic naevus shows a faint residual network, unlike the ‘dead white’ of vitiligo. Biopsy is rarely needed.

    5

    Differentials

    BirthmarkMain differentials
    Naevus of OtaMongolian spot, blue naevus, melasma, drug pigmentation, bruise
    Achromic naevusVitiligo, ash-leaf macules (tuberous sclerosis), pityriasis alba, post-inflammatory hypopigmentation, naevus anaemicus, piebaldism
    Becker naevusCafé-au-lait macule, congenital melanocytic naevus, plexiform neurofibroma, smooth muscle hamartoma
    Mongolian spotBruising (acute, tender, colour changes over time), naevus of Ota/Ito
    Epidermal naevusLichen striatus, linear lichen planus, linear porokeratosis, sebaceous naevus (scalp)
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    • Reassure. Most need no medical treatment. Offer camouflage cosmetics.
    • Naevus of Ota: specialist laser (Q-switched Nd:YAG or picosecond) over several sessions. Ophthalmology if the eye is involved. Any changing nodule needs dermatology.
    • Achromic naevus: refer if there is doubt, especially with multiple ash-leaf-like macules (tuberous sclerosis work-up).
    • Becker naevus: sun protection reduces contrast. Hair removal (shaving, waxing, or specialist laser). Examine for breast or limb asymmetry if extensive.
    • Mongolian spot: no treatment. Document site, size and colour in the red book and notes.
    • Epidermal naevus: emollients. Specialist options include keratolytics, calcipotriol, laser or surgery (recurrence possible). Refer if extensive, syndromic, a scalp sebaceous naevus, or ILVEN. Do not treat as warts with destructive OTC products.
    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Naevus of Ota involving the sclera or eye: glaucoma screening.
    • Multiple hypopigmented ash-leaf macules, seizures or developmental delay: tuberous sclerosis.
    • Extensive epidermal naevus with eye, neurological or skeletal features (epidermal naevus syndrome).
    • Extrasacral or atypical blue-grey marks without documentation: safeguarding questions. Document early.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • Mottled blue-grey pigment around the eye is not ‘just a birthmark’ if the sclera is involved.
    • Clear documentation of Mongolian spots protects families from later misunderstanding.
    • Naevus anaemicus does not redden on rubbing, but vitiligo and achromic naevus do.
    • Moles present at birth, melanoma risk by size and surveillance: see congenital melanocytic naevi.
