    Pigment & colour change

    Progressive macular hypomelanosis (PMH)

    Module 5 · Week 1

    Ill-defined, non-scaly pale patches merging on the trunk of young adults. Think of it when ‘pityriasis versicolor’ has no scale and does not respond to antifungals.

    CoversPMHpale patches trunkhypopigmentationCutibacterium acnesWood's lamp red fluorescence
    Think of it when

    Poorly defined, non-scaly, symptom-free pale macules merging on the back and trunk of a teenager or young adult, often with darker skin.

    What drives it

    Probably Cutibacterium acnes in the follicles producing a depigmenting factor. Melanin is reduced but melanocyte numbers are normal.

    Backbone of care

    Reassure. Options with variable evidence: benzoyl peroxide ± clindamycin, narrowband UVB, oral tetracycline.

    Never miss

    Persistent, atypical, progressive patches in an adult may be hypopigmented mycosis fungoides. Refer.

    What it is

    PMH is an acquired, benign condition of ill-defined, non-scaly hypopigmented macules. They often merge into patches on the trunk. It is asymptomatic, with no itch, pain or preceding inflammation. It progresses slowly over years and may stabilise or improve after mid-life.

    2

    How it’s caused

    The cause is not fully known. The leading hypothesis is Cutibacterium (Propionibacterium) acnes in the hair follicles producing a depigmenting factor. This is supported by follicular red fluorescence under a Wood’s lamp in many, but not all, patients. Genetic factors may contribute.

    Melanin is reduced while melanocyte numbers are normal, and melanosome packaging is altered on electron microscopy.

    3

    Clinical features

    • Round or oval, poorly bordered pale macules that merge on the back and trunk, with or without the proximal limbs and neck.
    • No scale. This is the key difference from pityriasis versicolor.
    • No preceding rash. This is the key difference from post-inflammatory hypopigmentation.
    • More noticeable, and more often reported, in darker skin. Typically teens and young adults, possibly more common in women.
    4

    Diagnosis

    • Clinical.
    • Wood’s lamp: follicular coral-red fluorescence supports C. acnes.
    • Skin scrapings (KOH) are negative, unlike pityriasis versicolor.
    • Dermoscopy shows ill-defined white macules with a faint pigment network. Biopsy is rarely needed.
    5

    Differentials

    Look-alikeDistinguishing clues
    Pityriasis versicolorFine scale; ‘spaghetti and meatballs’ on KOH; often yellow-green under Wood’s lamp
    Post-inflammatory hypopigmentationHistory of eczema, psoriasis or another rash at the same sites
    VitiligoSharp, chalk-white patches, often on the hands, face and around orifices; bright white under Wood’s lamp
    Pityriasis albaFaces of atopic children; mild scale
    Hypopigmented mycosis fungoidesPersistent, atypical, progressive patches in an adult. Refer
    Ash-leaf macules (tuberous sclerosis)Congenital, leaf-shaped; look for other features of tuberous sclerosis
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    • Reassure that it is benign. Explain the C. acnes theory and the slow course.
    • Options with variable evidence: topical benzoyl peroxide ± clindamycin, narrowband UVB (specialist), oral tetracyclines, or combinations. Isotretinoin appears only in case reports.
    • Relapse after stopping treatment is common.
    • Refer to dermatology if the diagnosis is uncertain, or if the patient wants treatment for cosmetic reasons.
    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Atypical, persistent or progressive patches in an adult: exclude hypopigmented mycosis fungoides.
    • Congenital or ash-leaf-shaped macules with other signs: consider tuberous sclerosis.
    • ‘Pityriasis versicolor’ that does not respond to antifungals: re-check for scale and do KOH.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • Do not keep treating it as ‘fungus’. If there is scale, take a KOH scraping first.
    • Photoprotection still helps even out skin tone.
    • Manage expectations: response is slow and often incomplete.
