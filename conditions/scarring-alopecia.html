    Cosmetic & hair

    Scarring alopecia overview

    Module 3 · Week 3

    When follicular openings are lost, hair loss is permanent. Recognise LPP, FFA, DLE, FD and CCCA and refer early — primary care holds the line while waiting.

    Coverscicatricial alopecialichen planopilarisfrontal fibrosing alopeciadiscoid lupusfolliculitis decalvansCCCAlost follicular ostia
    Think of it when

    Shiny bald patch without follicular openings, often with itch, burning, perifollicular scale or pustules at the active edge.

    What drives it

    Inflammation destroys the stem-cell bulge → fibrosis replaces the follicle.

    Backbone of care

    Photograph, swab/mycology if pus or scale, baseline bloods, Advice & Guidance / urgent dermatology — do not watch and wait for “AA to regrow”.

    Never miss

    Scarring alopecia is irreversible once established. Also refer non-healing nodules on bald scalp via suspected-cancer pathways.

    What it is

    Scarring (cicatricial) alopecia permanently destroys follicles. Primary types target the follicle (lymphocytic: LPP, FFA, DLE, CCCA; neutrophilic: folliculitis decalvans). Secondary scarring follows burns, trauma, tumours, kerion or long-standing traction.

    Contrast with non-scarring loss (alopecia areata, telogen effluvium, androgenetic alopecia) where ostia remain.

    2

    How it’s caused

    • Lymphocytic attack (LPP/FFA/DLE/CCCA) or neutrophilic folliculitis (FD, often S. aureus).
    • FFA incidence is rising, mainly in post-menopausal women.
    • CCCA predominantly affects women of African descent; hair practices contribute alongside genetics.
    3

    Clinical features

    ClueNon-scarringScarring
    Follicular ostiaPresent (yellow/black dots)Absent — shiny polished scalp
    Edge signsExclamation hairs / miniaturisationPerifollicular erythema, scale, plugs, pustules, tufting
    SymptomsOften noneItch, pain, burning, tenderness
    PrognosisRegrowth possiblePermanent — treat to halt spread
    • LPP: white patches with spiky perifollicular scale; check mouth/skin LP.
    • FFA: frontal/temporal recession, lonely hairs, eyebrow loss, “unsunned” forehead band.
    • DLE: red scaly plugged plaques → dyspigmented scars; ears/face; ask lupus systemic features.
    • FD: pustules, crusts, doll’s-hair tufting at the vertex — swab.
    • CCCA: crown spreading centrifugally.
    4

    Diagnosis

    Clinical + trichoscopy (absent ostia). Specialist biopsy from the active inflamed margin. Primary care: good photos (clinical + dermoscopy if available), swab pustules, mycology if scale.

    5

    Differentials

    Look-alikeClues
    Alopecia areataOstia preserved; yellow dots
    KerionBoggy child scalp infection — can scar; start antifungal pathway; do not incise
    Skin cancer on bald scalpUlcerated/bleeding nodule → NG12
    End-stage traction / trichotillomaniaHistory; may finally scar
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    • Urgent dermatology / Advice & Guidance with photos (PCDS: scarring alopecia needs early referral).
    • While waiting: swab, mycology if indicated; baseline FBC, ferritin, U&E/LFT, TFT, vitamin D; ANA if lupus suspected; syphilis serology if indicated.
    • Do not delay referral for blood results.
    • Dermatologist may suggest potent topical steroid via A&G for symptoms.
    • Specialist ladders (awareness only): hydroxychloroquine, doxycycline, anti-androgens (FFA), systemic immunosuppressants, culture-guided antibiotics for FD — not routine IP initiation.
    • Signpost Alopecia UK and camouflage.
    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Any loss of ostia, symptomatic inflamed edge, FFA pattern, CCCA pattern, lupus features, kerion, or uncertain “AA that will not regrow”.
    • Ulcerated/bleeding lesion on bald scalp → suspected-cancer pathway.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • Side-by-side teaching: yellow dots (AA) vs absent ostia (scar).
    • FFA is often dismissed as “just ageing” — check brows and the frontal band.
    • Once scarred, the goal is halt progression, not regrowth.
