    Ageing skin & older adults

    Skin fragility & dermatoporosis

    Module 6 · Week 4

    Actinic purpura, thin skin, skin tears and stellate pseudoscars — protect fragile skin and review anticoagulants/steroids carefully.

    Coverssenile purpuraactinic purpuraBateman purpuradermatoporosisskin tearsstellate pseudoscars
    Think of it when

    Painless purple patches on thin sun-damaged forearms after minimal trauma in an older person.

    What drives it

    UV and age destroy dermal support around vessels → easy shear bleeding; advanced form is dermatoporosis with skin tears.

    Backbone of care

    Reassure, protect (sleeves, pad furniture), daily emollient and sun protection; medication review with the prescriber.

    Never miss

    Palpable purpura, non-exposed-site bruising, mucosal bleeding or safeguarding patterns — investigate / escalate.

    What it is

    Actinic (senile/Bateman) purpura is benign purple-red ecchymoses on thin sun-damaged forearms and hands after minimal trauma. Dermatoporosis describes chronic cutaneous insufficiency — atrophy, purpura, stellate pseudoscars and skin tears.

    2

    How it’s caused

    • Chronic UV + intrinsic ageing → dermal collagen/elastin loss; vessels rupture with minor shear.
    • Aggravators: anticoagulants, antiplatelets, topical/oral/inhaled corticosteroids, SSRIs, fish oil/ginkgo/high-dose vitamin E.
    • Platelets and clotting are usually normal in classic actinic purpura.
    3

    Clinical features

    • Irregular dark-red/purple patches 1–5 cm on extensor forearms and dorsal hands; fade to brown haemosiderin.
    • Thin translucent wrinkled skin; stellate pseudoscars; skin tears from shear/adhesive dressings.
    • Not tender; not palpable (palpable = vasculitis).
    4

    Diagnosis

    Clinical at typical sites. Investigate FBC/clotting/LFTs (± INR) if purpura is on covered sites, palpable, with mucosal bleeding, systemic symptoms, new anticoagulant, or unexpected INR rise. Consider elder abuse if history/pattern mismatch.

    5

    Differentials

    Look-alikeClues
    Steroid purpuraLong-term steroid exposure
    Thrombocytopenia / coagulopathyPetechiae, mucosa, abnormal bloods
    VasculitisPalpable purpura, legs, systemic features
    ScurvyPerifollicular haemorrhage, corkscrew hairs
    Non-accidental injuryGrip marks, multiple ages of bruise
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    • Reassure: benign but marks fragility.
    • Protect: long sleeves, tubular forearm protectors, pad furniture, careful carer transfers; avoid harsh adhesives.
    • Sun protection and daily emollient.
    • Medication review with the GP — never stop an anticoagulant solely for actinic purpura without discussion.
    • Skin tears: gently approximate, non-adherent dressings, tetanus status; refer complex wounds.
    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Palpable purpura or systemic vasculitis signs.
    • Unusual distribution / safeguarding concern.
    • Large unexplained bruises with rising INR.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • Actinic purpura is a counselling and protection problem more than a cream problem.
    • Inhaled as well as topical steroids can contribute — ask.
    • Document skin-tear classification if using local wound pathways.
