    Solar lentigines

    Benign sun-induced lentigines versus freckles, melasma and seborrhoeic keratoses — with lentigo maligna as the red-flag differential.

    Overview

    Module 6 · Week 5solar lentigoage spotsliver spots

    • Spot it

      Flat, evenly brown macules on the face or backs of the hands of an older outdoor worker that do not fade in winter.

    • Why

      Chronic UV increases local melanocyte number and activity along elongated rete ridges.

    • Treat

      Photoprotection forever; topical retinoids/azelaic acid for modest fading; cryotherapy/IPL/laser only after cancer exclusion — usually private.

    • Never miss

      Irregular, multicoloured, enlarging facial pigment → lentigo maligna — urgent suspected-cancer referral. Never laser an undiagnosed pigmented lesion.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    What it is

    Solar lentigines (“age spots”, “liver spots”) are benign flat tan-to-dark-brown macules on chronically sun-exposed skin. They mark cumulative UV damage and skin-cancer risk but are not themselves premalignant.

    How it’s caused

    UV-driven localised melanocyte hyperactivity. Risk rises with fair skin, outdoor work, sunburn history, sunbeds and age.

    Step 3 of 9 · 1 min

    What you’ll see

    • Uniform light-to-dark brown macules with a regular border; face, dorsal hands, forearms, shoulders.
    • Do not fade in winter (unlike freckles).
    • May coexist with AK field change and photoageing.

    Diagnosis

    Clinical ± dermoscopy (fingerprint/moth-eaten border patterns). Biopsy or refer asymmetric, irregular, enlarging, ugly-duckling or grey-regression lesions — especially on the face.

    Step 6 of 9 · 1 min

    Treat

    Doses as in the source notes. Always confirm in the current BNF and local formulary.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    • Daily broad-spectrum SPF 30–50 (± tinted); hats/gloves for driving and gardening — without this, treated lesions return.
    • Examine the whole photodamaged field for AK/BCC/SCC/lentigo maligna.
    • Modest topicals: retinoids, azelaic acid; short specialist hydroquinone courses only (ochronosis risk).
    • Procedural (usually private): light cryotherapy (hypopigmentation risk, especially in darker skin), pigment laser for discrete spots, IPL for diffuse pigment+redness — only after diagnosis is secure.
    • Melasma is a poor IPL indication and can worsen.

    Step 7 of 9 · 1 min

    Red flags & referral

    • Urgent

      Any lesion suspicious for lentigo maligna or melanoma → urgent suspected-cancer pathway.

    • Don’t

      Do not treat undiagnosed pigment with cryotherapy, IPL or laser.

    Step 8 of 9 · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • Solar lentigines are a UV dosimeter — use the visit for skin-cancer education.

    • Hand spots are a classic patient question; same rules as the face.

    • See also photoageing and IPL & eyelash enhancement.

    • Flat brown marks that become raised and warty are usually seborrhoeic keratoses; a lentigo that darkens or spreads unevenly on the face may be lentigo maligna.
