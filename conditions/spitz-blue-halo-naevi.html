    Halo, blue, Spitz & recurrent naevi

    Special naevi that worry patients and clinicians: halo naevi with a white ring, blue naevi, Spitz and Reed naevi in children, and moles that regrow in a scar.

    Step 1 of 9 · 1 min

    Overview

    HaloBlueSpitz / ReedRecurrent

    • Spot it

      A mole with a white ring, a steel-blue papule, a fast-growing pink dome in a child, or pigment returning in a scar.

    • Why

      Immune clearance (halo), deep dermal melanocytes (blue), epithelioid / spindle melanocytes (Spitz / Reed), residual cells (recurrent).

    • Treat

      Reassure typical halo and blue naevi; refer Spitz-like lesions in adults, atypical or changing ones; send previous histology with recurrent naevi.

    • Never miss

      Melanoma with regression or a halo; spitzoid melanoma in adults; recurrent melanoma in a scar.

    Start: Why it happens
    2

    Step 2 of 9 · 1 min

    Why it happens

    Four special naevi, four mechanisms.

    • Halo: T cells destroy the naevus and surrounding melanocytes; vitiligo association.
    • Blue: pigment deep in the dermis looks blue (Tyndall effect).
    • Spitz / Reed: epithelioid or spindle melanocytes; benign but mimics melanoma.
    • Recurrent (‘pseudomelanoma’): residual cells after shave / incomplete excision.
    Next: What you’ll see
    3

    Step 3 of 9 · 2 min

    What you’ll see

    Naevus by naevus.

    • Halo: naevus + symmetrical white ring; children / teens; may be multiple.
    • Blue: steel-blue, firm dome; head, hands, buttocks.
    • Spitz: pink-red or pigmented dome growing fast in a child; dermoscopy starburst / dotted vessels.
    • Reed: black symmetrical starburst on a young woman’s thigh.
    • Recurrent: pigment returning in a scar; mimics melanoma on dermoscopy.
    • Also: naevus spilus (tan patch with speckles); acral naevi (parallel-furrow; ridge = melanoma).

    Next: Confirm the diagnosis
    4

    Step 4 of 9 · 1 min

    Confirm the diagnosis

    Dermoscopy

    • Halo: regular globules centrally; Spitz: starburst / dotted vessels.

    History

    • Age, speed of growth, prior excision / histology.

    Histology

    • Spitz in adolescents / adults; recurrent: compare with previous report.

    Next: Treat
    6

    Step 6 of 9 · 1 min

    Treat

    Reassure the typical; refer the rest.

    Treatment ladder

    1. 1

      FoundationHalo

      • Reassure if the central naevus is benign and symmetrical; sun-protect the white ring.
      • Refer adult > 40 with new halo or atypical centre.
    2. 2

      Step 1Blue

      • Reassure if long-standing and stable; refer if new in an adult, enlarging or > 1 cm (cellular blue / melanoma).
    3. 3

      Step 2Spitz / Reed

      • Typical lesion < 12 years: specialist may monitor; adolescents / adults: excision (atypical Spitz tumour / spitzoid melanoma DDx).
      • Reed: usually excised for histology.
    4. 4

      SpecialistRecurrent

      • Refer with the previous histology.

    Doses as in the source notes. Always confirm in the current BNF and local formulary.

    Next: Red flags & referral
    7

    Step 7 of 9 · 1 min

    Red flags & referral

    • Urgent

      Spitz-like lesion in an adult, atypical halo naevus, recurrent pigment in a scar: suspected cancer pathway.

    • Routine

      New / enlarging blue naevus in an adult, or > 1 cm.

    More detailfull notes · 1 min

    Red flags & referral

    • A Spitz-like lesion in an adult, an atypical halo naevus, or a recurrent pigmented lesion in a scar: refer on a suspected cancer pathway.
    Next: Prescriber pearls
    8

    Step 8 of 9 · 1 min

    Prescriber pearls

    • Typical halo or blue naevus in a young person → reassure; same appearance new in an older adult → refer.

    • Recurrent naevus: always send the original histology.

    • Acral: furrow = naevus, ridge = melanoma.

    Step 9 of 9 · 1 min

    Quick self-check

    1. Q1Why is a blue naevus blue?Reveal

      Deep dermal pigment: Tyndall effect.

    2. Q2Halo naevus association?Reveal

      Vitiligo.

    3. Q3Spitz-like lesion in a 45-year-old?Reveal

      Refer on suspected cancer pathway (excise).

    4. Q4What to send with a recurrent naevus referral?Reveal

      Previous histology.
