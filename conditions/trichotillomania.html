    Hair & scalp

    Trichotillomania

    Module 3 · Week 3

    Habitual hair pulling causing irregular patches of broken hairs. Habit-reversal therapy is first-line; watch for trichophagia and trichobezoar.

    Coverstrichotillomaniahair pullinghair-pulling disordertrichophagiatrichobezoar
    Think of it when

    Irregular or geometric bald patch with hairs of many different lengths; pull test negative; often on the dominant-hand side.

    What drives it

    Body-focused repetitive behaviour (OCRD spectrum) — automatic or focused pulling; broken shafts, not autoimmune bulb attack.

    Backbone of care

    Non-judgemental recognition, habit-reversal / CBT referral, wound care if excoriated; do not treat as alopecia areata with steroids alone.

    Never miss

    Trichophagia with abdominal pain, vomiting or mass → urgent surgical assessment for trichobezoar.

    What it is

    Trichotillomania (hair-pulling disorder) is a body-focused repetitive behaviour causing noticeable hair loss. It is not scarring in early disease. It is commoner in children and adolescents and often under-disclosed.

    2

    How it’s caused

    • Classed with obsessive–compulsive related disorders; anxiety, boredom or sensory urge often precede pulling.
    • Automatic pulling (unaware, while reading/screens) vs focused pulling (conscious tension relief).
    • May coexist with skin picking or nail biting.
    3

    Clinical features

    • Irregular, angular or tonsure-like patches; hairs of unequal lengths; broken hairs firmly anchored.
    • Common sites: vertex, frontotemporal (dominant-hand side), brows and lashes.
    • Trichoscopy: black dots, broken hairs, V-sign, flame hairs — contrast with AA exclamation marks.
    • Chronic severe pulling can eventually scar; secondary infection/crust possible.
    4

    Diagnosis

    Clinical pattern ± behaviour history (ask privately, without blame). Pull test negative. Exclude tinea if scaly. Differentiate from alopecia areata and scarring alopecia.

    5

    Differentials

    Look-alikeClues
    Alopecia areataSmooth patch; exclamation hairs; positive edge pull test
    Tinea capitisScale; mycology
    Traction alopeciaHairstyle distribution; fringe sign
    Scarring alopeciaLost ostia; symptoms
    6

    Management (UK pharmacist / IP)

    • Validate and reduce shame; involve parents/carers sensitively in children.
    • Habit-reversal training / CBT via CAMHS, psychology or specialist services is first-line.
    • Stimulus control (fidget tools, gloves at night, shorter nails); treat secondary infection if present.
    • Medicines are not first-line in primary care; any drug therapy is specialist.
    • Ask about chewing/swallowing hair every time.
    7

    Red flags & referral

    • Abdominal pain, early satiety, vomiting, weight loss, halitosis, epigastric mass → urgent surgical review (trichobezoar / Rapunzel syndrome).
    • Self-harm or suicidal ideation → urgent mental-health assessment.
    • Diagnostic doubt or progressive scarring → dermatology.
    8

    Prescriber pearls

    • Different hair lengths in one patch is the bedside clue.
    • Eyebrow/eyelash pulling in a child is still trichotillomania until proven otherwise — also consider AA and hypothyroidism for lateral brows.
    • Steroids will not fix the behaviour.
