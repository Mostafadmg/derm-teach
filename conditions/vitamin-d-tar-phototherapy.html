    Vitamin D analogues, coal tar, dithranol & phototherapy

    The non-steroid topical options for psoriasis and the hospital light treatments: how they work, where they fit on the NICE CG153 ladder, how to counsel, and their safety issues.

    Also calledcalcipotriolcalcitriolDovobetEnstilarcoal tardithranol+4

    Key facts

    • Plaque, scalp or flexural psoriasis needing more than a steroid, or a patient referred for light treatment.
    • Vitamin D analogues normalise keratinocyte growth; tar and dithranol slow proliferation; UVB and PUVA suppress skin immune cells.
    • Potent steroid + vitamin D (separately or combined) first; calcitriol for face / flexures; phototherapy is specialist.
    • Hypercalcaemia with excess vitamin D analogue; burns and skin cancer with cumulative PUVA.

    Overview

    • Vitamin D analogues (UK): Calcipotriol 50 micrograms/g ointment or scalp solution (Dovonex), and combined with betamethasone dipropionate 0.05% (Dovobet gel/ointment, Enstilar foam, generics); Calcitriol 3 micrograms/g ointment (Silkis) — gentler, used on face/flexures; Tacalcitol 4 micrograms/g (Curatoderm) — once daily
    • Coal tar: crude coal tar or refined extracts in creams, ointments, scalp shampoos and bath products (e.g. Exorex lotion, Sebco, Capasal, Polytar, Cocois). Specialist units compound crude tar.
    • Dithranol (anthralin): short-contact therapy for chronic plaques in specialist/day units. It stains purple-brown and burns normal skin.

    Phototherapy and PUVA

    Controlled ultraviolet exposure, delivered in hospital dermatology units and some home-phototherapy schemes. Main types:

    ModalityWavelengthMain uses
    Narrowband UVB (NB-UVB, TL-01)311–313 nmFirst-line phototherapy: psoriasis, atopic eczema, PLE desensitisation, vitiligo, pruritus
    Broadband UVB290–320 nmLargely replaced by NB-UVB
    PUVA = psoralen + UVAUVA 320–400 nmThick/resistant psoriasis, palmoplantar pustulosis, hand eczema (hand-and-foot PUVA), mycosis fungoides, some vitiligo
    UVA1340–400 nmMorphoea, scleroderma, some eczema (specialist)

    Psoralens are furocoumarins. 8-methoxypsoralen (methoxsalen, 8-MOP) is taken orally ~2 h before UVA, or used as bath/soak (topical) PUVA. 5-MOP, where available, causes fewer GI effects.

    Aetiology: causes and risk factors

    Clinical features

    How it works

    • Vitamin D analogues: They bind the nuclear vitamin D receptor (VDR), which pairs with RXR to regulate genes; This slows keratinocyte proliferation (e.g. via the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors p21/p27) and promotes normal differentiation (cornified-envelope proteins); They are also immunomodulatory: they lower Th1/Th17 cytokines such as IL-17 and IL-8, and promote regulatory responses; Clinically they flatten and de-scale plaques but do less for erythema than steroids
    • Coal tar: A complex mixture (polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, phenols). It activates the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR), which normalises keratinocyte differentiation, restores barrier proteins (filaggrin) and damps Th2/Th17 inflammation; It is also anti-proliferative (reduces DNA synthesis), antipruritic and antifungal (Malassezia); It is phototoxic with UVA; the historic Goeckerman regimen combined it with UVB
    • Dithranol: generates free radicals, giving an anti-proliferative effect; it stains and irritates.

    Clinical uses & where it fits

    • Plaque psoriasis (NICE CG153), trunk/limbs: First-line: potent corticosteroid once daily + vitamin D once daily, applied separately (one in the morning, the other in the evening), for up to 4 weeks; No improvement after 8 weeks: vitamin D twice daily alone; Still poor after 8–12 weeks: potent steroid twice daily for up to 4 weeks, or coal tar once/twice daily, or the combined calcipotriol + betamethasone dipropionate product once daily for up to 4 weeks. NICE places the combined product where the separate regimen fails or adherence is the problem
    • Scalp psoriasis: potent steroid for up to 4 weeks first, then the combined product, then vitamin D alone.
    • Face/flexures/genitals: mild–moderate steroid short-term, or calcitriol / a TCI (off-label).
    • Coal tar: chronic plaque and scalp psoriasis, seborrhoeic dermatitis (shampoos), and some specialist use in lichenified eczema.

    How to choose / assess

    • Thin plaques, or patients who struggle with adherence → combined once-daily gel/foam.
    • Thick scale → descale first (salicylic acid; coal tar/salicylic acid/sulfur scalp ointments such as Cocois).
    • Flexures → avoid calcipotriol irritation (use calcitriol).
    • Large BSA → watch the vitamin D maximum doses.

    Phototherapy and PUVA

    How it works

    • UV light is immunosuppressive and anti-proliferative: It induces T-cell apoptosis and depletes Langerhans cells; It shifts cytokines (↓ IL-12/IL-23/IL-17 and TNF-α; ↑ IL-10 and regulatory T cells); It forms DNA photoproducts that slow keratinocyte turnover
    • Psoralens intercalate into DNA. On UVA activation they form mono-adducts and interstrand cross-links, which block DNA replication and proliferation, and they also generate reactive oxygen species.
    • UVA penetrates to the dermis, deeper than UVB.

    Clinical uses & where it fits

    NICE CG153: offer NB-UVB for plaque or guttate psoriasis that topical treatment alone cannot control. Offer PUVA for palmoplantar pustulosis and when NB-UVB fails.

    How to choose / assess

    • Course logistics: 2–3 visits a week for ~6–10 weeks (NB-UVB ~20–30 exposures). Dose is set by skin type or minimal erythema dose (MED) testing. Patients must be able to attend regularly.
    • Exclude: photosensitising drugs or conditions (lupus, porphyria, xeroderma pigmentosum); previous skin cancer or high cumulative UV; inability to stand, or claustrophobia; pregnancy (oral PUVA)

    Diagnosis and investigations

    Ladder (NICE CG153)

    • Potent TCS od + vitamin D od, separately, up to 4 weeks.
    • No better at 8 weeks: vitamin D bd alone.
    • Still poor at 8–12 weeks: potent TCS bd ≤ 4 weeks, coal tar, or combined product.

    Max doses (BNF, recheck)

    • Calcipotriol 100 g / week.
    • Combination: 15 g / day, 100 g / week, ≤ 30% BSA, 4-week courses.
    • Calcitriol 30 g / day, ≤ 35% BSA.

    Avoid

    • Hypercalcaemia, severe renal impairment; caution in erythrodermic or pustular psoriasis.

    Differential diagnoses

    PhotoConditionDistinguishing features

    Management

    • Maximum doses (adults, BNF — recheck): Calcipotriol: 100 g/week (less if the scalp solution is also used); Calcipotriol + betamethasone products: max 15 g/day and 100 g/week, ≤30% BSA, 4-week courses. The foam can be used longer as twice-weekly maintenance per its SmPC; Calcitriol: max 30 g/day and ≤35% BSA; Children: specialist use, with lower age-based maxima
    • Avoid in: hypercalcaemia and severe renal impairment (calcium handling). Use with caution in generalised pustular or erythrodermic psoriasis (absorption, hypercalcaemia).
    • Counsel: wash hands after use; keep calcipotriol off the face; irritation usually settles; with phototherapy, apply after UV exposure, not before.
    • Coal tar safety (course teaching question): Percivall Pott (1775) described scrotal squamous cell carcinoma ("chimney sweeps' cancer") from soot — the first recognised occupational cancer; Large cohort data (e.g. Roelofzen et al. 2010, ~13,000 psoriasis/eczema patients) found no increase in overall cancer risk from therapeutic coal tar, so the risk at dermatological concentrations is considered very low; Coal tar is used less now mainly because of mess, odour, staining and EU/UK reformulation of products, not because of cancer data; Avoid on inflamed/pustular psoriasis and broken skin, avoid concentrated products on face/genitals, and avoid sun after application (phototoxicity)

    IP scope

    Starting vitamin D analogues, combined calcipotriol/betamethasone and coal-tar preparations for chronic plaque/scalp psoriasis is within primary-care IP competence. Dithranol is specialist.

    Phototherapy and PUVA

    • Oral psoralen: Wear UVA-protective wrap-around glasses for 24 h after each dose (some units say 12 h); Avoid the sun on treatment days, and take the dose with food to reduce nausea; Avoid eating large amounts of furocoumarin-rich foods (celery, figs, parsnips, limes)
    • Long-term: lifelong skin-cancer surveillance after high PUVA exposure, with self-examination. Any new keratotic or ulcerated lesion goes on the NICE NG12 pathway.
    • With calcipotriol or acitretin: units may combine them with light (e.g. re-PUVA with acitretin). Apply topical vitamin D after UV exposure, not before.

    IP scope

    Referral and counselling only; phototherapy prescription and dosing are specialist.

    Treatment ladder

    1. 1

      Counsel

      Wash hands after; keep calcipotriol off the face; irritation usually settles. With phototherapy, apply vitamin D after UV, not before.

    2. 2

      First line

      Steroid in the morning, vitamin D at night (or the reverse) for up to 4 weeks, or one combined product daily.

    3. 3

      Step up

      Vitamin D bd; coal tar; combined product; calcitriol for face and flexures.

    4. 4

      Specialist

      NB-UVB (often safest step-up in pregnancy), PUVA, dithranol, systemics.

    Caution

    Prescribing limits and pitfalls

    • Hypercalcaemia: Over weekly maximum or symptoms: check calcium, stop.
    • PUVA eyes & cancer: UVA-blocking eye protection ~24 h after oral psoralen; lifelong skin checks.
    • Tar: Avoid inflamed / pustular skin, face, genitals; avoid sun after (phototoxic).

    Referral

    • Same day

      Erythrodermic or generalised pustular psoriasis: urgent dermatology.

    • Routine

      Not controlled on topicals at 8–12 weeks: refer for phototherapy or systemics.

    Red flags and when to refer

    • More than the BNF weekly maximum of calcipotriol, or symptoms of hypercalcaemia: check calcium and stop.
    • Patients on PUVA need UVA-blocking eye protection for about 24 hours after oral psoralen, and lifelong skin-cancer checks.

    Red flags / refer

    • Erythrodermic or generalised pustular psoriasis (same day).
    • Psoriasis not responding to two topical steps, extensive (>10% BSA) or high-impact disease (NICE CG153 criteria for referral, phototherapy or systemic therapy).
    • Suspected hypercalcaemia (thirst, confusion) after heavy vitamin D use.

    Red flags / refer

    Prescriber pearls (UK pharmacist IP)

    • "Steroid in the morning, vitamin D at night" (or the other way round) for the first 4 weeks, or one combined product once daily.

    • Combined products contain a steroid — count them in the steroid budget and use breaks.

    • Tar shampoo: lather, leave 5–10 minutes, rinse. It can discolour blond, grey or dyed hair.

    • "Phototherapy is a prescription of light" — the dose, course length and lifetime total are recorded like a drug's.

    • NB-UVB is often the safest step-up in pregnancy for psoriasis/eczema.
